## 0. The hinge — why this poster does an extra job

Every earlier Appearance poster drew **biology**: mechanism, KPIs, footnotes (drain the face, survey the head). ==This article is the explicit pivot of the whole series — its own first callout says "this article and the next two are *signaling*, not biology."== So Part 4.0 carries two arguments at once and the poster must hold both:

1. **The local argument:** clothing is a *decision-removal system* (four matched sets + three measurable-or-conventional levers), not a question of taste-as-law.
2. **The series-level argument:** the ground has shifted from *nature* to *convention*. The warm-biology register that dominated Parts 2.0/3.0 now **recedes to a thin warm thread** (only the genuinely measurable things stay warm — GSM, fit, the coherence rules), while **cool convention expands** to fill most of the board (colour, contrast, the sets themselves).

The poster should make a returning reader *feel* that tonal shift before reading a word: less warm than 3.0, more cool, and an honest banner that says so. That is the single most important thing this concept adds over a generic "wardrobe" graphic — it is the only poster in the pillar whose colour balance itself tells a story.

## 1. The Big Idea

_One sentence: **Your wardrobe is not a closet full of choices — it is four pre-built kits matched to four demands, so getting dressed becomes indexing, not deciding; and the few variables that actually move the needle are measurable (fabric weight, fit, metal/leather coherence), while the rest is honest convention you classify once.** The poster should make the reader feel, instantly, that the daily "what do I wear?" puzzle is a solved engineering problem — you point at a demand, the system hands you the set — and that the one thing worth obsessing over is the **fabric**, not the **logo**._

## 2. Visual Metaphor & Structure — the hero object

**A drafting-table "STANDARD-ISSUE LOADOUT" / parts-catalog spec sheet for the reader's own wardrobe** — drawn the way an equipment manual draws a modular kit: a **demand selector wired down into four labelled bays**, each bay a small **technical garment flat** (a tech-pack line drawing — the industry's own engineering drawing of a garment, with seams, hems, and spec ticks), the whole thing dimensioned and annotated. This is the anchor; nothing replaces it. The point the geometry must make: **you address the board by *demand*, and it returns a *set* — the choosing has been pre-done.**

The board reads top → down, in the article's own order (sets → fabric → colour → footwear):

- **TOP — THE DEMAND SELECTOR: "INDEX BY DEMAND, NOT BY MOOD."** A horizontal input rail with four demand-keys, each a small stamped tab wired by a clean leader down into exactly one bay below it: `TRAIN` → Gym · `LOUNGE` → Casual Lounge · `EXECUTE` → Daily Casual · `HIGH-STAKES` → Formal. The selector is the decision-removal engine made literal: a switch with four detents, not an open field. Caption: `POINT AT THE DEMAND — THE SET IS ALREADY BUILT.`

- **CENTRE — THE FOUR BAYS: "FOUR SETS, NOT A WARDROBE."** Four vertical bays in a row, each holding the core silhouette of one set as a tech-pack flat, each stamped with its demand and a tiny `5–7 PIECES` capacity plate (the article's functional minimum, "anything beyond 7 is collection, not utility"):
  - **Bay 1 · GYM / PERFORMANCE** — fitted athletic tee + tapered jogger flat; tag `functional · signals readiness, not effort`.
  - **Bay 2 · CASUAL LOUNGE / NIGHT** — relaxed straight-leg + crewneck/henley flat; tag `comfortable but STRUCTURED`. A small ghosted amber warning hangs off this bay: a struck-out *oversized old gym tee* labelled `casual ≠ unkempt` (the article's named lounge trap).
  - **Bay 3 · DAILY CASUAL — "THE GO-TO"** — drawn **largest and brightest**, slightly forward of the others (premium plain tee/OCBD + tapered chino/dark jean + overshirt flat); plate `60–70% OF DAYS · BUILD THIS FIRST`. This bay is the load-bearing column of the whole rack — size = how often it's worn.
  - **Bay 4 · FORMAL / HIGH-STAKES** — tailored two-piece flat (navy/charcoal) + dress shirt; tag `cannot be faked off the rack → see a tailor`.
  - A small **BUILD-ORDER stamp** runs under the bays: `1 DAILY · 2 FORMAL · 3 GYM · 4 LOUNGE` (the article's explicit build sequence), so the reader knows where to spend first.

- **THE GSM GAUGE — running horizontally *through* the bays (warm, the under-rated hero lever): "THE SHAPE COMES FROM THE FABRIC."** A single **fabric-weight dimension scale** threaded across the tee/shirt flats like a ruler, calibrated `<140 → 140–180 → 180–240 → 240–300 → 300+ GSM`. At the **low end** the garment flat is drawn **thin, limp, clinging, ghosted** (`hugs every line you don't want hugged`); at the **240–300 sweet-spot band** (highlighted in warm terracotta as the only true measurable in the colour zone) the flat **stands up off the body, square on the shoulder, structured**. Hero callout, the biggest non-headline line on the board: `SHAPE COMES FROM THE FABRIC, NOT THE BODY UNDERNEATH.` Sub-tick: `280 GSM at RM 80 > 130 GSM at RM 200 — the fabric does the work.` This gauge is warm because GSM is *genuinely measurable*; it is the article's single largest visible-upgrade lever and must read as load-bearing, not decorative.

- **RIGHT PANEL — THE COLOUR CALIBRATION CARD (cool slate, convention): "CLASSIFY ONCE."** A docked, separate **calibration chip-card** (cooler register = the article flags colour as *useful default, not law*), holding the two stable variables:
  - **UNDERTONE** — three swatch chips `WARM / COOL / NEUTRAL` with the three quick tests as tiny icons (wrist-vein · silver-vs-gold · white-paper), tagged `two of three agree = confident · most Malaysian/SE-Asian men sit warm-to-neutral → soft white & cream beat stark white`.
  - **CONTRAST** — a small **grayscale face strip** (hair / skin / eye values) with three settings `HIGH · MEDIUM · LOW`, and the one rule large: `OUTFIT CONTRAST = FACE CONTRAST · don't out-shout the face` (`most Asian men = MEDIUM`).
  - Card header: `CLASSIFY ONCE → HALF THE COLOUR DECISIONS ARE PRE-MADE.` The whole card sits in slate-blue precisely because this is the conventional half — the visual cue is the teaching.

- **BOTTOM — THE FOOTWEAR FOUNDATION RAIL: "FOUR PAIRS, MAINTAINED."** A ground rail of four shoe-flats, one per demand, mirroring the bays above: `1 MINIMAL WHITE LEATHER SNEAKER (build first)` · `2 PREMIUM ATHLETIC` · `3 LOAFER / CHELSEA` · `4 OXFORD / DERBY`. Two **warm coherence ties** stitch across the rail because these are *mechanical, grounded rules*: `LEATHER → LEATHER (brown shoe = brown belt)` and `METAL → METAL (silver buckle = silver buckle)` with a tiny struck mismatched-metal note `the mismatch is a tell of inattention`. An amber maintenance plate: `CLEAN RM 400 > SCUFFED RM 1,500 · maintain before you buy a fifth`.

- **THE ONE RED — THE LOGO / PRICE TRAP (the single restrained alarm).** Off to one side, ghosted like a rejected sample: an **expensive, logo-stamped tee that is *limp* (<140 GSM on the gauge)**, struck diagonally in signal-red `REJECTED — you paid for the logo, not the fabric.` One line beneath: `brand & price are weak predictors once fit & fabric are controlled.` It is the exact inversion of the warm GSM hero, and it is the article's sharpest single mistake. Red appears **nowhere else** — the lounge "unkempt" trap and the scuffed-shoe note are amber, calm cautions, not alarms.

- **THE EPISTEMIC HINGE BANNER + LEFT-RAIL KEY.** A thin banner across the top under the headline: `FROM HERE ON: SIGNALING, NOT BIOLOGY — a system to remove the decision, not a law of nature.` The left margin carries the pillar's two-register **epistemic key**, but *reweighted for the hinge*: `■ warm = MEASURABLE (GSM · fit · coherence)` over a **larger** `■ cool = CONVENTION (the sets · colour · contrast — strong defaults, not laws)`. The key's proportions (small warm / large cool) are themselves the series-pivot argument.

**Why this metaphor fits and nothing else does:** the article's literal moves are *index a demand → get a pre-built set* (the selector + bays), *upgrade the one measurable variable* (the GSM gauge), *classify two stable variables once* (the calibration card), and *match four grounded pairs* (the footwear rail) — a **standard-issue loadout / parts catalog** is the only object that holds all four at once **and** renders the core feeling: getting dressed is *selection from a solved system*, not daily invention. A styled outfit grid, a capsule-wardrobe hanger rack, or a colour wheel would each destroy the one idea that matters — that this is a *spec'd system with measurable levers*, not a lookbook of taste.

## 3. Layout Description

**Landscape (poster / in-article banner, ~3:2 or 16:10)** — the four-bay rack + footwear rail + side calibration card want width; it flips back to landscape after 3.0's portrait, echoing 2.0. (A portrait variant works by stacking: selector on top, bays in a 2×2, calibration card and footwear rail below.)

- **Top band (~14%):** the headline (see §4), small against the paper, kerned wide, thin drafting border beneath; the **hinge banner** sits directly under it; a tiny slug `Part 4 of 7 · Appearance · the Attractive pillar`. The **DEMAND SELECTOR** rail lives just below the banner so the very first thing the eye meets is *input → set*.
- **Centre stage (~58%) — the hero:** the **four bays** in a row with the **Daily Casual bay enlarged and pushed forward**; the **GSM gauge** threads horizontally through the tee/shirt flats (its limp→structured transformation is the most eye-catching internal event). The **colour calibration card** docks at the right edge as a separate, cooler panel (its different register reads instantly as "this part is convention").
- **Bottom band (~20%):** the **footwear foundation rail** of four shoe-flats with the two warm coherence ties; the **build-order stamp** bridges centre and bottom. The **rejected logo-tee (one red)** sits in the opposite negative space to the calibration card (lower-left ghosted margin), so the single alarm is found *after* the system is understood.
- **Left margin rail (full height):** the reweighted **epistemic key** (small warm / large cool), docked vertically so the colour-coding is legible before any label is read — and so the hinge is felt structurally.
- **Footer micro-caption (~8%):** the thesis lockup `FOUR DEMANDS. FOUR SETS. ZERO CYCLES.` plus a thin line: `index the demand → upgrade the fabric → classify colour once → match the shoes · spend on tailoring & fabric, not the logo.`

Reading path is deliberate and matches the article: **hinge banner (the ground has shifted) → demand selector (point at today) → the matched bay (the set is built) → the GSM gauge (the one lever worth obsessing over) → the calibration card (classify colour once) → the footwear rail (four grounded pairs) → glance to the rejected logo-tee that proves fabric > brand.**

## 5. Color & Mood

**Mood:** the calm, exacting confidence of an equipment manual / quartermaster's spec sheet — a *standardized kit* drawn on a drafting table, grown-up and un-anxious, the opposite of a fashion mood-board. The feeling of being handed the loadout for something you've been improvising daily. The **warm-measurable / cool-convention split is the personality and does the teaching**, and here it is deliberately *tilted cool* to mark the series hinge.

**Palette (light-first — the established Attractive drafting-paper register):**
- **Background:** warm bone / drafting paper (`#F4F1EA`-ish), faint blueprint grid or drafting-dot texture, very low contrast.
- **Ink / linework / type:** graphite near-black (`#1E2024`) for all structure — the selector, bays, garment flats, dimension lines, labels.
- **Accent 1 — MEASURABLE / GROUNDED (used *sparingly* — the hinge demands restraint):** structural **terracotta / ochre** (`#C2703D`-ish) for the **GSM gauge sweet-spot band**, the **fit/structure** cue on the heavyweight flat, and the **leather→leather / metal→metal coherence ties** — the only genuinely measurable things in the article. Warm = real, load-bearing, worth obsessing over.
- **Accent 2 — CONVENTION (the dominant accent here):** muted **slate-blue / pewter** (`#5E7585`-ish) for the **colour calibration card**, the **contrast strip**, and the **bay set-logic** — the parts the article calls *useful defaults, not laws*. Cooler, lighter, "taste standardised, not nature."
- **One alarm colour — the rejected logo-tee only:** restrained **signal-red / vermilion** (`#D9483B`), used *exclusively* for the `REJECTED` stamp on the expensive-limp-logo tee. Scarcity is the force; it inverts the warm GSM hero.
- The **demand selector + build-order stamps** read in graphite (with warm ticks), like a real catalog's index — never the alarm red.

The cool-over-warm balance ties Part 4.0 directly to the article's "signaling, not biology" hinge and to Part 1.0's mechanism→convention line: a reader who saw the earlier posters reads the *shift* before reading a word.

## 6. Style Reference

**"Architect's Elevation / Spec Sheet" (Attractive-pillar locked style) — the STANDARD-ISSUE LOADOUT / PARTS-CATALOG variant.** Touchstones:

- The honesty and labelled-dimension discipline of a **technical garment flat / tech pack** (the fashion industry's *own* engineering drawing — seams, hems, GSM callouts, spec ticks) and of an **equipment / quartermaster's loadout manual** (a modular kit indexed by use-case, capacity plates, a parts catalog). This keeps it credible rather than decorative, and it *is* the article's logic: a spec'd system, not a lookbook.
- The cross-section + annotation calm of **editorial explainer graphics** (Monocle / Kinfolk-grade typographic restraint; a Wait-But-Why concept *drafted by a quartermaster*, not styled by a fashion blog).
- The **two-register colour surface** carried over from Parts 1.0/3.0 — warm for measurable, cool for convention — here *tilted cool* to render the hinge. This is the single most distinctive, un-generic move.

It should sit as the **fourth sibling** within the pillar family: **1.0 = elevation (the appearance stack) · 2.0 = hydraulic section (the draining face) · 3.0 = survey/spec drawing (the head) · 4.0 = standard-issue loadout (the wardrobe system).** Same bone-paper palette, same epistemic coding, same single-alarm-red discipline, a new drawing type (the first *catalog/matrix* form in the pillar). This carries the Attractive signature forward into the signaling half (Parts 5.0–6.0).

## 7. What to Avoid

- **The fashion-blog / lookbook cliché:** a styled male model, a flat-lay "OOTD" photo, a Pinterest capsule-wardrobe collage, a mood-board, a row of aspirational outfits. It says "style influencer," teaches nothing, and worst of all valorises *taste-as-law* — the exact thing the article's hinge attacks. The subject is the reader's *wardrobe drawn as a spec'd kit*, never an aspirational stranger.
- **The generic minimalist "capsule wardrobe" hanger rack** of evenly-spaced pastel garments. It is the single most over-used wardrobe image, and it carries **no measurement** — which kills the article's whole point that the variables that matter (GSM, fit, coherence) are *measurable*. If garments appear, they are **technical flats with spec ticks**, not styled product shots.
- **A "colour season" wheel / colour-analysis swatch fan.** The article explicitly refuses "the colour of the season"; undertone+contrast must read as a *classify-once calibration card*, not a mystical seasonal wheel.
- **Letting GSM be an afterthought.** Fabric weight is the article's largest single lever; if the gauge isn't a hero device that visibly transforms a limp flat into a structured one, the poster has buried the lede.
- **(Bonus to avoid) Drowning the hinge.** The cool-over-warm tilt and the "signaling, not biology" banner are mandatory — without them the poster silently claims the dress rules are *laws* (the precise dishonesty the article is built to avoid). And **don't redraw a sibling** — keep clear of 1.0's vertical building, 2.0's hydraulic section, and 3.0's two-view head survey; 4.0 is a horizontal indexed loadout, a different object.
