## Series context & the key point _(why this concept, not a generic one)_

This is the **first** article of the Advanced track, and it does only two things: it states the **goal** and it installs the **framework**. There is no protocol here, no macro split, no program. That is the trap a generic chat would fall into — it would try to visualise "muscle-building tips." This article is the opposite: it is a _mental model_, and the poster's whole job is to draw the model so cleanly that every later article (the five systems) has a place to hang.

The model has one load-bearing reframe:

> **Stop thinking of "metabolism" as a single dial with a number on it.** Your body is a **stack of control loops** — the structure an engineer draws for a thermostat. Every loop has a **sensor** (reads a state), an **integrator** (decides), and an **effector** (acts). You do **not** operate the effectors. You can't command "partition toward muscle." What you _can_ do is move the **inputs the sensors read** — leanness, carb timing, mechanical tension, energy availability, sleep — and let the body's own control system do the work in the direction you've biased it.

Three more ideas the visual must carry, in order, because the article is emphatic that they are ranked:

1. **The master variable is the P-ratio** (the fraction of an energy imbalance that lands on lean tissue, not fat). It is the **output** of the loops, never a loop itself. And it is **a curve, not a constant**: leaner bodies partition a surplus better (the Forbes/Hall relationship). That single curve is the mechanistic spine of "stay lean-ish while you bulk."
2. **Five systems set it**, covered macro → molecular: the **sensors** (leptin + insulin), the **throttle** (thyroid, which sets the _size of the budget_ / RMR), the **amplifier** (androgen receptor, density & sensitivity), and the **switch** (AMPK vs mTOR, the build-vs-conserve fork). They are one wired circuit, not a list.
3. **Three goals, in priority order:** (1) bias the partitioning ratio toward lean, (2) defend the size of the budget, (3) maximise anabolic signal per unit of training. Partitioning first — because if the big lever is broken, no receptor tweak saves you (the 90/10 rule in a new costume).

And one boundary: this is the **natural** treatment — density & sensitivity (more output from the testosterone you already make). The **enhanced** treatment (occupancy — what you put _on_ the receptor) lives in Performance Enhancement. Same receptor, two doors.

**The single key point to make a reader feel:** _you are not driving the engine — you are setting its sensors._ You can't grab the output and turn it to LEAN. You can only move the five inputs the loops read, and the machine biases itself. The poster is a control panel a reader studies to stop chasing "the output dial" (there isn't one) and start asking "which sensor, which input, in which direction."

This is the concept a generic chat would miss: it would draw a metabolism dial, a muscle-vs-fat split, or a stack of supplement icons. The _real_ payload is the **closed-loop logic** — sensor → integrator → effector, input knobs only on the sensor side, four control-loop modules descending from body-wide to molecular and summing into one master P-ratio needle whose _range itself is a curve_ that widens when you're lean.

## 1. The Big Idea

_Your body is not a dial you turn; it is a stack of control loops you set. The output that matters — fat vs lean, the P-ratio — is something the machine computes, not something you command. The amateur grabs at the output ("eat less, train more") and wonders why it stops responding. The operator reaches one layer back, to the **sensors**, moves the five inputs they read, and lets the loops bias the whole engine toward LEAN._ The poster is an engine-room control panel a reader studies to re-frame the whole game: not "what do I take / how hard do I cut," but "which sensor, which input, which direction" — and why partitioning is the lever you fix before any other.

## 2. Visual Metaphor & Structure

An **engine-room control panel rendered as a closed-loop control schematic** — the thermostat-diagram the article explicitly invokes, scaled up into a vertical mimic board. The metaphor is not decoration; the article hands it over directly ("the same structure an engineer would draw for a thermostat"), and it earns its place three ways:

1. **"Sensor → integrator → effector" is literally a control loop.** Drawing each system as a labelled loop _is_ the article's framework, not an illustration of it.
2. **"You operate the sensors, not the effectors" draws itself.** Put the input knobs **only** on the sensor side of each loop and grey the effectors as read-outs. The composition teaches the thesis before a word is read: the only things a hand can touch are the five inputs.
3. **The P-ratio is the output, never a loop.** A control schematic has exactly one master output meter at the end of the chain. Making the P-ratio needle the single summed read-out — fed by all four modules — encodes "partitioning is the output of the loops running together" structurally.

**Structure:** a **vertical stack of four control-loop modules**, signal descending from **body-wide (top)** to **molecular (bottom)** — the article's own macro-to-molecular order, so the downward read _is_ the zoom. Each module is a self-contained loop: a **sensor** (with the input knob you can turn), an **integrator** (the decision node), and an **effector** (a greyed read-out you don't touch). The four modules converge at the base into a single **master output: the P-RATIO meter**, a needle swinging between **FAT** and **LEAN**. Beside it, three stacked **priority gauges** = the three goals in rank order. A small **Forbes/Hall curve plate** clamps onto the master meter to show its range is a curve (leaner → wider LEAN window). A single sealed **door** on the amplifier module points to PE. A faint **failure overlay** shows the naive crash-diet running through the same circuit.

This is a deliberately _different instrument_ from every prior Fit poster — the PE set ran horizontal (consoles, benches, strip-charts); this is a **vertical control-loop descent**, which both fits "a _stack_ of loops" and gives the Advanced sub-series its own silhouette while staying unmistakably inside the locked Engineering Diagnostic Instrument language.

## 3. Layout — exact composition

Orientation: **portrait / tall.** A stack of loops descending macro → molecular wants vertical travel; portrait also reads well inline and on a phone, and it visually announces "new sub-series" after the landscape PE consoles.

Zones, top to bottom:

1. **Kicker (top-left):** mono tag `FIT · AESTHETIC & STRENGTH — ADVANCED · PART 1.0 — THE ENGINE ROOM`, preceded by a tick/rule so it reads like a panel label-strip. A small secondary mono note: `the science layer — explains the basics, doesn't replace them`.
2. **Headline block (top, large):** headline (copy §4) in the display face, up to two lines; one-line subhead beneath in muted secondary text.
3. **The framework legend (thin band under the subhead):** one compact line establishing the loop grammar so the whole board is legible at a glance — `EVERY LOOP:  SENSOR ▸ INTEGRATOR ▸ EFFECTOR   —   you turn the sensor, the loop moves the effector`. Quiet technical chrome; a tiny key shows a **knob glyph = you can turn this** vs a **greyed plate = read-out, hands off**.
4. **The control-loop stack (HERO — the central ~58%, four modules descending):** four loop modules, each a horizontal sensor→integrator→effector strip with the **input knob on the left (sensor) only** and the **effector greyed on the right**. Descending body-wide → molecular:
   - **① THE SENSORS — Leptin & Insulin** _(Part 3.0 · body-wide reporting)_. Two sub-gauges share the module: **Leptin** = a slow **chronic FUEL GAUGE** (how much fat is on board); **Insulin** = a fast **STORE ⇄ MOBILISE** toggle (meal-by-meal). Input knob: `leanness · carb timing`. Effector (grey): `store / mobilise`. Caption tag: `report energy status to brain + tissues`.
   - **② THE THROTTLE — Thyroid** _(Part 4.0 · sets the size of the budget)_. A throttle valve sizing a **BUDGET (RMR)** tank. Show the fork: **T4 → T3 (active, opens throttle)** vs **T4 → reverse-T3 (decoy, throttles down)**. Input knob: `energy availability — don't crash leptin`. Effector (grey): `RMR ↑/↓ (can fall 15–40%)`. A thin **feedback wire runs UP from ① to ②** — leptin's fall pulls this throttle down (the master trigger).
   - **③ THE AMPLIFIER — Androgen Receptor** _(Part 5.0 · sets the gain)_. A **gain stage**: `same testosterone × receptor DENSITY = output`. The natural lever is _more receptors_, not more hormone. Input knob: `mechanical tension · AR density (training)`. Effector (grey): `nutrient → contractile protein`. A **wire exits the bottom of ③ into ④** labelled `via IGF-1 / Akt` — the physical link to the switch.
   - **④ THE SWITCH — AMPK ⇄ mTOR** _(Part 6.0 · molecular · the final call)_. A two-position fork: **mTORC1 = BUILD** (tension + leucine flip it on) vs **AMPK = CONSERVE** (deficit / long endurance flips it on, inhibits mTOR via TSC2). Input knobs: `leucine threshold · tension` (toward BUILD) and the honest note `deficit / cardio → CONSERVE`. Effector (grey): `muscle protein synthesis on/off`.
   - All four effector outputs run down a **summing bus** into the master meter. Draw the bus so it is obvious: **four controllers, one output.**
5. **The master output (lower-centre, the payoff):** **THE P-RATIO METER** — a single large gauge, needle swinging on an axis `◄ FAT ———— LEAN ►`. It is fed by the summing bus. Label: `P-RATIO — where the imbalance lands. The OUTPUT of the loops, never a dial you set.`
6. **The three priority gauges (right of / beside the master meter, stacked in rank order):** the three goals, numbered to show they are _ranked_, not parallel —
   - `①  BIAS THE RATIO` → toward LEAN (this IS the P-ratio needle; priority 1).
   - `②  DEFEND THE BUDGET` → a small RMR gauge fed by the throttle (priority 2).
   - `③  MAX SIGNAL / REP` → a small gain gauge fed by amplifier + switch (priority 3).
   A bracket ties them: `fix ① before ② before ③ — broken partitioning can't be out-tweaked (90/10).`
7. **The "range is a curve" plate (clamped onto the master meter):** a small inset — the **Forbes/Hall curve**: x = body fat, y = % of a surplus that goes to lean; a downward slope. A marker on the lean end reads `stay lean-ish = wider LEAN window`. This is the only _data_ object on the poster; keep it small but legible. It teaches that the needle's reachable range _shifts with leanness_.
8. **The PE boundary door (right edge of module ③ only — ONE door, rationed):** a single sealed panel-door on the amplifier: `→ PERFORMANCE ENHANCEMENT`. This side of the door: `density & sensitivity — more from the test you already make`. Through it: `occupancy — what you put ON the receptor`. Caption: `same receptor, two doors. This series owns the first.`
9. **The failure overlay (ghosted, integrated — NOT a separate box):** the naive hard cut, drawn as a faint second state running through the _same_ circuit — **leptin crashes** (① fuel gauge reads empty) → **throttle drops** (② reverse-T3 decoy path lit amber, BUDGET tank shrinking) → **switch flips to CONSERVE** (④ AMPK side lit) → **P-ratio needle swings to FAT** and the BUDGET gauge collapses. Caption: `Crash the calories, crash leptin — the throttle quietly shrinks the budget and the needle swings to FAT. That's the plateau, drawn as a circuit.`
10. **Footer:** the framework key as one line — `knob = you can turn it (the inputs) · grey plate = read-out (the effectors) · feedback wire = the loop closing` — plus the state key (`green = biased to lean / in-window · amber = budget shrinking / decoy path · red = needle railed to FAT, budget collapsed`) and the series mark `Fit — Aesthetic & Strength (Advanced)`.

**Eye path:** kicker → headline → framework legend (learn the loop grammar) → drop into module ① at the top and **descend the stack**, reading each loop and noticing every knob is on the sensor side and zoom tightens body-wide → molecular → land on the **P-RATIO master meter** at the base → read the **three ranked priority gauges** → catch the small **Forbes/Hall curve** (range is a curve) → notice the single **PE door** on the amplifier → register the faint **failure overlay** (the crash-cut) → footer legend. One top-to-bottom descent with a single payoff at the base.

## 5. UX / visual encoding expectations _(semantic — colors come from design.md, not from here)_

- **The knob-vs-plate split is the lesson before any text is read.** Every input knob sits on the **sensor** side; every effector is a **greyed read-out**. A viewer must see at a glance that the only turnable controls are the five inputs (leanness, carb timing, energy availability, mechanical tension, leucine/tension). That asymmetry _is_ "you operate the sensors, the loops do the rest." Protect it; do not draw knobs on effectors.
- **The descent must read as a zoom, not a list.** Top module = body-wide (hormones reporting to the brain); bottom module = molecular (a fork between two enzymes). Use scale/inset cues so descending the stack feels like increasing magnification — that ordering is the article's "macro-to-molecular." Number them ①→④ so the path is unambiguous.
- **The summing bus must make "four controllers → one output" structural.** All four effector outputs visibly merge into the single P-ratio needle. The P-ratio must never look like a fifth knob; it is the read-out the whole board computes.
- **The three goals must read as _ranked_, not parallel.** Numbering + a "fix ① before ②" bracket is mandatory; this priority order (partitioning > budget > signal) is a load-bearing claim in the article. If they look like three equal options, the poster has lost the point.
- **Red is rationed to exactly the failure end-state.** The single red object is the **P-ratio needle railed to FAT (with the budget gauge collapsed)** in the failure overlay. Use **amber** for the cautionary mid-states — the reverse-T3 decoy path, the shrinking budget, the AMPK/CONSERVE side when lit. Green marks "biased to lean / in-window." Do not invent hues; map to design.md success / caution / danger roles. (Consistent with the Fit set: red ≈ 1 object, the one true failure.)
- **The failure overlay is the same circuit in a second state, not a separate "bad" panel.** Show the crash-cut as a faint trace running the identical loops: ① empty → ② throttled (amber) → ④ CONSERVE → needle to FAT. "Right vs wrong" must be one instrument in two states.
- **The Forbes/Hall curve is small but real.** It is the only quantitative object; keep it legible (a labelled downward slope), clamped to the master meter so it clearly modifies the needle's _range_. Don't let it grow into a second hero — it's a spec plate.
- **Hierarchy of attention:** headline → the four-module stack (hero) → the P-ratio master meter → the three ranked gauges → the curve plate + PE door → the faint failure overlay → footer legend (quiet chrome). Within each module: the **role name + knob** loudest, the system/Part tag and effector supporting.
- **Restraint = the mood.** Instruments on a precision control panel, not an infographic with personality turned to max. Dark console ground, mono labels, ruler-drawn loop wires, generous negative space between modules; color rationed to the windows (green), the cautions (amber), and the one railed needle (red).
- **Saveability:** use shape/position as well as hue — knob vs grey plate, needle position, the ranked numbering, the bus geometry — so the panel survives grayscale and a phone screen. A reader should still parse "knobs only on sensors" and "four loops → one needle" with color stripped.

## 7. What to Avoid

1. **A single "metabolism dial" or a muscle-vs-fat split image.** The obvious moves are a glowing gauge labelled "metabolism" or a body silhouette half-muscle/half-fat. Both reinstate the exact mental model the article is _demolishing_ ("metabolism is one dial with a number on it"). The upgrade is the **closed-loop panel**: the output is computed by four loops, and the only controls are the sensors.
2. **Knobs on the effectors / making the P-ratio a dial you turn.** If a hand can grab the output, the poster says "just command lean" — the opposite of the thesis. Knobs belong **only** on the sensor side; the P-ratio is a read-out at the end of the bus, never an input. This asymmetry is non-negotiable.
3. **Drawing the failure state as a separate red "bad" panel, or flooding the board with color.** Keep the crash-cut as a faint overlay on the _same_ circuit (leptin empty → throttle down → CONSERVE → needle to FAT). Red stays rationed to the single railed needle; everything cautionary is amber. A rainbow control room teaches noise; a one-needle-railed panel teaches the plateau.
