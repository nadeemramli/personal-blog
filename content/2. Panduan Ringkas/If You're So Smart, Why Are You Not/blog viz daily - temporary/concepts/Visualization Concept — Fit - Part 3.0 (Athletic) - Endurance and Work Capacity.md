## Series context & the key point _(why this concept, not a generic one)_

Part 3.0 opens the **"Five Qualities"** half of the Athletic series. Parts 2.0/2.1 built **the engine** (heart, lungs, mitochondria — central/cardiovascular capacity). Part 3.0's entire intellectual move is a **disambiguation**: it takes the single word **"endurance,"** which everyone treats as one thing, and proves it is actually **two separate problems wearing one word** — and that a third thing sits *underneath* both, quietly setting the ceiling on everything.

The three objects the reader must come away able to name and tell apart:

1. **Central / cardiovascular endurance** — the engine. Limited by *oxygen delivery* (heart, lungs, VO2 max). **This is Part 2's subject — already owned.** The article deliberately points at it and says: not here, go build it there. Visually it should read as a module you've *already finished* — present, dimmed, checked-off.
2. **Local / muscular endurance** — the muscle's own staying power. Limited by what happens *inside the working muscle*: fatigue resistance, local fuel stores, the ability to buffer the metabolic mess of hard contractions. **This is the lit hero of the poster.** The killer line: *this is what fails on rep 18 of a push-up test even when your heart is fine.* The dissociation — strong lungs, weak local staying power — is the whole reason the word needed splitting.
3. **Work capacity (GPP — General Physical Preparedness)** — the total work you can perform **and recover from** across a session, week, block. It sits *on top of* both endurances but functions as a **foundation/reservoir**: ==the bigger it is, the more quality training you can absorb before you break.== "The base of the pyramid." The quiet multiplier. This is the non-obvious idea most readers have never had a mental model for, so the poster must give it a *shape*.

The protocols fall straight out of the split — two distinct prescriptions, each aimed at its own target:

- **Muscular endurance = high reps, short rest.** 40–60% 1RM (or bodyweight), 15–30+ reps near failure, **30–60 s rest** — *the incomplete recovery IS the stimulus*. Trained as finishers, after the heavy work, never before it.
- **Work capacity = lots of low-soreness work.** Circuits, EMOMs, sleds/prowlers, and especially **loaded carries** (farmer's at ~bodyweight). The tools are *deliberately non-specific* and **concentric-biased** → conditioning without deep muscle damage → **low interference** with strength and size. This is the "why it doesn't steal your gains" point.

Then the mechanism — the article's "now the physics" turn — rests on **three pillars**, all of which want instrument readouts, not prose:

- **Fibre-type continuum.** Type I (slow, very fatigue-resistant, 2–3× mitochondrial volume) → Type IIa (the fast-but-repeatable hybrid) → Type IIx (fastest, almost no aerobic machinery, fatigues instantly). A left-to-right *gradient*, not a table.
- **Two adaptations from training it.** (1) **Peripheral adaptation** *inside the trained muscle* — more capillaries, more mitochondria, better lactate/H⁺ buffering ("the burn"). (2) A **fibre-type shift**: consistent endurance work nudges adaptable **IIx → IIa**. You don't grow new slow-twitch fibres; you make your fast ones more enduring.
- **Fatigue is two-sided.** **Peripheral fatigue** = chemistry inside the muscle (metabolite build-up, glycogen depletion). **Central fatigue** = the CNS dialling down drive to protect you. High-rep/short-rest pushes *both* ceilings up.

Two structural truths close it, and they're the series' recurring "it rests on the floors you already poured" motif:

- **The repetition continuum.** Heavy/low-rep → strength; moderate → size; light/high-rep near failure → local endurance. The adaptation *tracks the rep range* — a loading **spectrum/dial**, perfect for an engineering ruler.
- **It rests on the engine AND on strength.** A bigger aerobic base = faster *between-set* recovery (refill the tank sooner → do more work). A bigger strength base = every rep is a *lower % of max* → the same task feels lighter → you last longer. Both raise the endurance ceiling "for free."

Prior Fit posters used distinct sub-archetypes inside the locked style: lookup matrices (1.1/1.2), a signal-trace strip (2.0), an operating-band curve dashboard (3.1 old), a build-up assembly schematic (3.2), and a P&ID oxygen-flow diagram (2.1). Part 3.0 needs a **new** sub-archetype that is unmistakably "Engineering Diagnostic Instrument" yet its own thing. The article's native geometry is a **SPLIT sitting on a FOUNDATION** — one word fanning into two channels, both standing on a capacity reservoir. So the right instrument is a **two-channel diagnostic bench: a signal SPLITTER up top routing "ENDURANCE" into two labeled channels (one owned/dim, one lit), both bolted onto a full-width WORK-CAPACITY RESERVOIR at the base, with the mechanism rendered as instruments wired to the lit channel.** It's a fault-isolation panel — exactly the article's command: *name which quality is failing before you train it.*

**The single key point to make a reader feel:** *"Endurance" is two problems wearing one word. Half of it is your lungs (the engine — already built in Part 2). The other half lives inside the muscle — the local staying power that quits on rep 18 even when your heart is fine. And underneath both sits work capacity: the reservoir that decides how much hard training you can absorb before you break. Train the local staying power directly with high reps and short rest — but fill the reservoir first, because it's the multiplier on everything.* A second free lesson the diagram teaches: *the gains aren't all local — a bigger engine and a bigger strength base raise your endurance ceiling for free.*

## 1. The Big Idea

_One word, "endurance," splits into two independent systems: CENTRAL (the engine — oxygen delivery, owned in Part 2) and LOCAL/MUSCULAR (staying power inside the muscle — this article's hero). Both stand on a foundation of WORK CAPACITY (GPP), the reservoir that sets how much quality training you can absorb and recover from. The poster is a fault-isolation bench an engineer would mount on a wall: the word ENDURANCE entering a splitter at the top, fanning into two channels (one dimmed and check-marked "owned," one lit and instrumented), both bolted to a full-width capacity reservoir at the base — with the mechanism (fibre gradient, the two adaptations, two-sided fatigue), the loading-spectrum ruler, and the two "free-gain" support beams docked around the lit channel._

## 2. Visual Metaphor & Structure

A **two-channel diagnostic / fault-isolation bench**. The instrument's job is the article's first instruction: *when a quality stalls, name which quality.* So the master gesture is a **SPLITTER** — the single input `ENDURANCE` enters at top-center and is routed into **two parallel channels**:

- **CHANNEL A — CENTRAL (the engine).** Drawn as an **"OWNED" module**: present but visually *receded* — lower contrast, a small `✓ built in Part 2` tag, a heart+lungs glyph, label `limited by O₂ delivery · VO₂ max`. It exists so the reader sees what's being *set aside*. It is deliberately the quiet channel.
- **CHANNEL B — LOCAL / MUSCULAR (this article).** The **lit hero channel**: full accent, a **muscle-fibre-bundle cross-section** glyph, label `limited by what happens INSIDE the muscle`. The signature callout wired to it: `⚠ fails on rep 18 of the push-up test — even when your heart is fine.`

Both channels are **bolted onto a full-width foundation bar** running across the bottom third — the **WORK CAPACITY (GPP) RESERVOIR**, drawn as a fluid-level tank / capacity meter with the master line: `THE MULTIPLIER — the more total work you can do AND recover from, the more quality training you absorb before you break. Build it first.` A small `base of the pyramid` tag. The reservoir physically *supports* both channels — the visual argument that capacity underwrites everything above it.

Wired to **Channel B and the reservoir** are the two protocol cards and the mechanism instruments:

**Two protocol prescription cards (the "how"):**

- **MUSCULAR-ENDURANCE card → wired to Channel B:** a spec block — `LOAD 40–60% 1RM (or bodyweight) · REPS 15–30+ near failure · REST 30–60 s · 2–3×/wk`. The hero sub-line: `the incomplete recovery IS the stimulus.` A small placement rule chip: `AFTER strength work, never before — don't pre-fatigue what you're about to load heavy.`
- **WORK-CAPACITY card → wired to the reservoir:** a tool rack — `CIRCUITS · EMOMs · SLEDS/PROWLERS · LOADED CARRIES (farmer's @ ~bodyweight)`. The hero sub-line: `concentric-biased = low soreness = LOW INTERFERENCE. Conditioning that doesn't steal your lifts.`

**Mechanism instruments (the "why," docked around Channel B):**

1. **FIBRE-TYPE GRADIENT bar** — a horizontal gradient strip read left→right, three labeled bands:
   `TYPE I (slow · very fatigue-resistant · 2–3× mitochondria)` → `TYPE IIa (fast · repeatable hybrid)` → `TYPE IIx (fastest · ~no aerobic machinery · fatigues instantly)`. Two micro-axes under it: `← fatigue resistance` and `power →`.
2. **THE TWO ADAPTATIONS panel** — two stacked readouts: (a) `PERIPHERAL: +capillaries · +mitochondria · better buffering of H⁺/lactate ("the burn")` and (b) a directional arrow `IIx → IIa SHIFT — you don't grow new slow fibres; you make your fast ones enduring.`
3. **TWO-SIDED FATIGUE gauge** — a single dial with two needles/ceilings: `PERIPHERAL FATIGUE = chemistry in the muscle (metabolites + glycogen)` and `CENTRAL FATIGUE = CNS dials down drive to protect you`. Caption: `high reps + short rest pushes BOTH ceilings up.`

**The loading-spectrum ruler (a thin full-width scale, ties protocol to mechanism):**
`HEAVY / low-rep → STRENGTH | MODERATE → SIZE | LIGHT / high-rep near failure → ENDURANCE`. A small needle parked at the right end (`you are here`). Caption: `the adaptation tracks the rep range — the repetition continuum.`

**The two "free-gain" support beams (under Channel B, structural):** two columns drawn as literal *load-bearing supports* propping up the local-endurance module —
- `RESTS ON THE ENGINE → faster between-set recovery (refill the tank sooner → more work).`
- `RESTS ON STRENGTH → every rep is a lower % of max (60 kg bench: push-ups = max effort; 100 kg bench: push-ups = light endurance).`
Header over the two beams: `half the gains come from floors you already poured.`

**Tracking strip (footer, small):** four green check-readouts — `MORE reps @ same load · LESS rest for same work · FASTER between-set HR recovery · MORE weekly volume tolerated.` Header: `THE NUMBER IS THE BOSS.`

This sub-archetype — **splitter → two channels (one owned/dim, one lit) → bolted onto a capacity reservoir → mechanism instruments wired to the lit channel** — is visually distinct from every prior Fit poster while remaining unmistakably "Engineering Diagnostic Instrument."

## 3. Layout — exact composition

**Orientation: portrait** (poster / blog-hero friendly, ~3:4 or 4:5). Eye path is top→down with a deliberate left/right contrast in the middle.

- **Top band (~12%) — MASTER HEADLINE + SPLITTER.** Centered input token `ENDURANCE`, with a splitter glyph (a single line forking into two) immediately beneath it fanning left and right. Headline lockup: **"ONE WORD. TWO PROBLEMS."** sub: *name which one is failing before you train it.*
- **Upper-middle band (~34%) — THE TWO CHANNELS, side by side.**
  - **Left = CHANNEL A · CENTRAL (dimmed, `✓ Part 2`)** — clearly the quieter, receded column.
  - **Right = CHANNEL B · LOCAL/MUSCULAR (lit hero)** — higher contrast, the `rep 18` callout, the muscle-fibre glyph. Right side carries ~60% of the visual weight so the eye lands here.
- **Lower-middle band (~14%) — FOUNDATION.** The full-width **WORK CAPACITY RESERVOIR** bar that both channels visibly *sit on*. This is the structural punchline — keep it wide and solid.
- **Protocol cards (~16%)** docked at the channel/reservoir junctions: muscular-endurance card pinned to Channel B (right), work-capacity card pinned to the reservoir (center-left).
- **Mechanism cluster (~16%)** — the fibre gradient bar (full width, just below the reservoir, since it underpins both), with the two-adaptations panel and the two-sided-fatigue gauge flanking it.
- **Footer (~8%)** — the loading-spectrum ruler as a thin full-width scale, then the four-check tracking strip beneath it.

The two "free-gain" support beams are drawn *physically between* Channel B and the reservoir on the right side — literal props — so the "rests on" metaphor is spatial, not just labeled.

Visual hierarchy, largest→smallest: (1) the SPLIT (ENDURANCE → two channels) and the lit Channel B, (2) the WORK CAPACITY reservoir, (3) the two protocol cards, (4) the fibre gradient + mechanism gauges, (5) the spectrum ruler + tracking checks.

## 5. Color & Mood

**Defer to `aether-call-to-action-DESIGN.md` for the actual palette, type, and surfaces.** What this poster needs the design system to *express*:

- **A two-state contrast for the channels.** Channel A (CENTRAL) must read as **receded / owned / done** — lower contrast, desaturated, a checkmark state. Channel B (LOCAL) must read as **active / live / instrumented** — full accent, the brightest element on the poster. This light/dark, settled/live tension carries the whole "split" thesis at a glance.
- **A "foundation" weight for the reservoir.** The work-capacity bar should feel *structural and solid* — the heaviest, most grounded surface, something the channels visibly rest on. Fluid-level / fill cue welcome.
- **A gradient channel for the fibre-type bar.** One smooth ramp from the calm/endurance end (Type I) to the hot/explosive end (Type IIx) — the only place a true gradient appears, so it reads as the "physics" instrument.
- **Mood:** clinical, grounded, diagnostic — a fault-isolation panel, not a motivational gym poster. Confident and quiet. The energy comes from the *contrast* between the dimmed and lit channels, not from saturation everywhere.

## 7. What to Avoid

- **The literal GPP pyramid.** The article says "base of the pyramid," but drawing an actual triangle with "GPP" at the bottom is the single most overused fitness-infographic cliché. Use the **reservoir/foundation bar** instead — it carries the same "everything rests on this" meaning without the tired shape.
- **The runner-vs-lifter split-screen.** A sweaty marathoner on one side and a buff lifter on the other is the obvious, generic way to show "two kinds of endurance" — and it misframes the split (the real division is *central vs local*, not *cardio person vs gym person*). Keep it to the abstract splitter + the fibre/muscle instruments.
- **(Bonus) A fibre-type comparison TABLE.** The article already has the table; the poster's job is to turn it into the **gradient bar**. Reproducing the grid wastes the visual medium.
