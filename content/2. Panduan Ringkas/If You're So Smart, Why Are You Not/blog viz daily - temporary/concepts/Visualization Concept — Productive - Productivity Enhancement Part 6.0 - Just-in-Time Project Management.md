## Series context & the key point _(why this concept, not a generic one)_

Part 6.0 is the **capstone discipline of the whole stack** — the layer that finally fixes *overwhelm*. By now the lower layers work: the [[Part 3.0 - The Workflow Engine|workflow]] catches everything, the [[Part 4.0 - PARA|knowledge system]] feeds it, the [[Part 5.0 - The Tactical Toolkit|toolkit]] sharpens it. The failure that's left is **scope**: people try to push every project they've ever started forward at once, and end up with "forty half-open loops, each generating a low hum of guilt, none actually moving." The article's emotional core and its hardest-to-picture idea are the same sentence:

> ==Carrying every project at once is how you stay **maximally busy and minimally finished**.==

**Just-in-Time Project Management** is the discipline of pulling a project into active work **only when it's genuinely due**, and leaving the rest **dormant without guilt**.

The moves the poster has to carry:

1. **The Toyota analogy (the spine).** Old way = **just-in-*case***: stockpile huge inventories "so they're ready" → tied-up capital, full warehouses, idle stock that goes obsolete. Toyota's **just-in-*time*** flipped it: produce/pull parts **only as demand calls for them**, holding almost no inventory. ==Your projects *are* the inventory.== The just-in-case mind stockpiles open projects; the just-in-time mind keeps almost all of them **dormant** and pulls one forward **only when its moment arrives**. Dormant ≠ abandoned — they're [[Part 4.0 - PARA|archived or parked]], **costing you nothing**, ready to activate when a deadline actually approaches.

2. **Pull, not push.** The trigger to activate is **"its moment has come"** (a deadline, an opportunity) — **not** "I feel bad about it." Real demand pulls; **anxiety must not**. This is the single behavioural correction the poster must make visible.

3. **The stack assembles the project for you (the payoff for building the lower layers).** When a project activates: (1) it activates (deadline approaches *or* you deliberately pull it from the parked list) → (2) you **pull its Resources just-in-time** from [[Part 4.0 - PARA|PARA]] + relevant [[Part 4.1 - Progressive Summarization|maps]] → (3) you **learn what it pulls for**, on the job, *now* (so it sticks) → (4) you **define the next action** ([[Part 3.0 - The Workflow Engine|3.0]]) and execute, **single project in focus** → (5) on completion the whole thing **sweeps to Archives** and the active inventory drops back down, ready to pull the next. You start from "everything past-you already gathered," **not a blank page.**

4. **The AI multiplier (locked fundamental-before-multiplier law).** When you pull a project forward, an assistant can **assemble the kit**: gather resources + maps, draft the project plan and the first next action, summarise what you already know. ==The blank-page cost of starting drops to near zero — which is exactly what removes the resistance that made you stockpile in the first place.== And a *fully repeatable* project type can be handed to an [[Part 7.0 - The Agentic AI Framework|agent]] to run (→ Part 7). The multiplier only works because the lower layers (3.0/4.0/4.1) are actually built and live.

5. **The just-in-case / just-in-time split also explains wasted learning** (the secondary payload). Just-in-case learning = hoarding knowledge you might need someday (the course bought and never finished, the pre-built curriculum you couldn't stick with — ==it doesn't match a problem you have *right now*, so it's abstract, doesn't stick, and you resent the time==). Just-in-time learning = learning the thing **when a live project demands it** — the specific React hook when the feature needs it, the specific clause when the contract hits your desk. ==Projects and learning are two faces of one principle: let real demand pull the work *and* the learning forward, instead of stockpiling either against a hypothetical future.==

6. **The operating rules + the feeling.** **Cap active projects** (3–5 is the honest ceiling). **Parked is a real, guilt-free state** (reviewed in the weekly review: "does anything need to activate yet?" — usually no, and that's fine). **Let deadlines and opportunities pull, not anxiety.** **Finish before you start** (completing-and-sweeping frees a slot). ==The goal is a high *completion* rate, not a high *open* rate.== The reward is **calm**: a short list genuinely in motion, a long list safely waiting, **no guilt about the difference** — only possible because the layers beneath mean nothing parked is ever lost.

**The single thing a reader must walk away owning:** *A finite amount of throughput can only run a few projects at full power at once. Stockpiling open projects doesn't make you finish more — it spreads you so thin that everything stalls (busy, unfinished). So keep almost all projects dark and dormant (they cost nothing), and let a real deadline or opportunity — never guilt — pull one into the small active bay, where the stack you built assembles its kit for you. Finish it, sweep it to Archives, and only then pull the next. Optimise for completion, not for open loops.*

That is a story about a **capped, demand-pulled supply feeding a field of mostly-dark loads** — which is why the honest anchor is a **pull-triggered capacity governor / project manifold**, not a Kanban board (which shows everything in parallel — the very disease the article treats) and not a motivational "focus" poster.

## 1. The Big Idea

**One sentence:** *Your throughput is a power supply with a hard ceiling: try to run every project at once and they all brown out (busy, unfinished); keep almost all of them dark and let a real deadline — not guilt — pull just a few into the lit active bay, where the stack assembles each one for you, finish, sweep, repeat.*

The feeling on first glance: a vast field of **dark, calm, dormant** project cells drawing zero current (no guilt), a small **brightly lit active bay** capped at 3–5, and a **demand line** (a deadline clock) reaching in to close exactly one switch — while an **overload inset** proves that flipping everything on doesn't speed you up, it stalls you. The relief is the calm of the dark field; the jolt is the paradox that *more active = less finished.*

## 2. Visual Metaphor or Structure _(the hero — non-negotiable)_

**Sub-archetype (NEW, distinct from all nine prior Productive posters): the PULL-DEMAND CAPACITY GOVERNOR — a finite-rated supply feeding a wide manifold of project loads, almost all switched OFF and dark, with a hard concurrency limiter and a demand-pull trigger.**

Where 4.0 answered *where a project is filed* (a heat-graded storage bus), 6.0 answers *whether a project is drawing power right now* — and enforces that **only a few can, at once.** The whole instrument is built on one finite **RATED SUPPLY** (your attention/throughput) wired to a long **PROJECT MANIFOLD**. Three hero devices ride on it:

**HERO DEVICE 1 — the capped active bay vs the dark dormant field (the core geometry).** The manifold has *many* loads. The overwhelming majority are **switched OFF — open switches, dim grey, drawing ZERO current**: the **PARKED / DORMANT FIELD**, tagged *"INVENTORY WAITING FOR DEMAND · COSTS YOU NOTHING."* This field must read **calm, not alarming** — dormant is the correct, restful state, the literal source of the article's "calm." Off to one side, a small **ACTIVE BAY** holds **exactly 3–5 sockets, brightly lit (cyan)**, each running one project at full power. Between supply and active bay sits the load-bearing constraint: a **CURRENT LIMITER / rated fuse** stamped **"ACTIVE CAP · 3–5"** — you physically *cannot* light a sixth without tripping it. The brightness asymmetry (a few brilliant, a sea of calm dark) is the argument.

**HERO DEVICE 2 — the demand-pull trigger vs the blocked anxiety-push (pull, not push).** Above the active bay, a **DEMAND LINE** drops in from a thin top **deadline/opportunity timeline**: a small **DEADLINE CLOCK** + an **OPPORTUNITY spark**. This is the *only* thing that closes a dormant project's switch and pulls it up into an open socket — drawn as a clean cyan pull-arrow energising one cell. Running **upward from below**, a ghosted **ANXIETY PUSH-LINE** (amber, labelled *"I feel bad about it"*) tries to force parked projects active — and is **stopped dead at the gate** ( *"deadlines & opportunities pull · guilt doesn't"* ). The contrast — top-down demand admitted, bottom-up guilt blocked — is the behavioural correction made into wiring.

**HERO DEVICE 3 — the overload paradox (the myth-buster A/B, the emotional punch).** A compact side-by-side inset, **same total supply both sides**, proving the counter-intuitive core:
- **LEFT — "EVERYTHING ON" (just-in-case):** every switch closed → the rated supply spread across dozens of loads → **all loads BROWN OUT / flicker** below the do-useful-work threshold → **COMPLETION METER ≈ 0.** Caption: *"maximally busy, minimally finished."*
- **RIGHT — "A FEW ON, REST PARKED" (just-in-time):** only 3–5 closed, the rest dark → the active few sit at **FULL brightness / clean current** → **COMPLETION METER HIGH.**
This inversion (more active projects → *less* finished) is 6.0's equivalent of 4.4's inverted aldosterone float and 3.1's equal-nitrogen datum — the geometry that carries the whole thesis.

**THE STACK-ASSEMBLES-IT FEED (the payoff, docked under the active bay).** The moment a project lands in an active socket, it is **fed from below by a CONVERGENCE / KIT bus** that pulls its kit up so it lights up **pre-loaded, never blank**:
- `RESOURCES → 4.0 PARA` (everything past-you filed under it)
- `MAPS → 4.1` (the progressive-summarisation map nodes)
- `NEXT ACTION → 3.0` (the workflow defines the first move)
The **×AI multiplier** is a small **KIT-ASSEMBLER / PRE-CHARGE arm** tapped onto this feed: it *gathers the resources + maps, drafts the project plan and the first next action, summarises what you already know* — collapsing the **blank-page / inrush resistance to ≈ 0** ( *"the blank page disappears"* ). Locked fundamental-before-multiplier law: the assembler is **lit only because the 3.0/4.0/4.1 feeds beneath it are live**; draw them thin and the arm delivers nothing.

**THE COMPLETION SWEEP (finish before you start).** An active project that finishes flips to a **DONE** state and **sweeps one-way OUT to a dark ARCHIVE SINK** (callback to 4.0's P→A sweep) — and **only that sweep returns headroom to the limiter**, re-opening a socket for the next pull. Draw the loop literally: *completion is what frees a slot.* Tag: *"FINISH ⇒ FREE A SLOT · completion rate > open rate."*

**THE JUST-IN-CASE / JUST-IN-TIME LEARNING SUB-PANEL (secondary payload, same governor).** A small twin panel shows the *same pull logic governing learning*:
- **JUST-IN-CASE STOCKPILE** = a buffer of pre-bought **capacitor banks** (courses, pre-built curricula) charged "in case," sitting idle and **slowly self-discharging / going obsolete** (amber, faint leak), tagged *"doesn't match a problem you have now → doesn't stick."*
- **JUST-IN-TIME LEARNING** = a **skill component that conducts only when a live project upstream pulls for it** — a small tap off an active socket, tagged *"learn what it pulls for · sticks because it's used now."*
Header on the panel: *"PROJECTS & LEARNING — TWO FACES OF ONE PULL."*

**FORWARD CONTINUITY (off-board, faint).** One active socket carries a small **AUTONOMOUS RUNNER** badge handing a *fully-repeatable* project type **off-board → Part 7 (agentic)** — the same off-board convention 1.0 used for Habits, 1.1 for the behavioural damper, and 4.2 for scheduled/autonomous.

**THE ONE RED OBJECT — the named fault (overload).** A hand **cramming extra switches closed past the limiter**; the **CURRENT LIMITER glows red and TRIPS**; the active bay **browns out**. Red label: **"EVERYTHING ON = NOTHING FINISHES."** Red appears **only** here. Everything else cautionary is **amber** (the blocked anxiety-push line, the self-discharging just-in-case stockpile). Crucially, the **parked/dormant field is calm grey, NOT red** — a parked project is *inventory waiting for demand*, not a failure. The only true fault is overloading the supply.

## 3. Layout Description

**Orientation: LANDSCAPE** (a supply → manifold → archive flow reads wide, and the dormant field needs room to feel *vast and calm*). Portrait fallback only if the host template forces it (stack the dormant field above the active bay). The composition must read as a **capped power system with a demand trigger**, never as a sequence or a storage gradient.

- **Left ~40% — the DORMANT / PARKED FIELD:** a large, quiet array of many identical project cells, switches **open**, **dim grey**, drawing nothing. Deliberately the biggest, calmest region of the poster (its restfulness *is* the reward). Small tag: *"PARKED · COSTS YOU NOTHING · reviewed weekly: does anything need to activate?"* No heat gradient, no PARA zones — a **flat dark field**, undifferentiated (this is what keeps it distinct from 4.0).
- **Top edge — the DEMAND TIMELINE:** a thin horizontal rail carrying a **DEADLINE CLOCK** and an **OPPORTUNITY spark**, with a single bright cyan **pull-arrow** dropping down through the limiter into one active socket ("its moment has come"). 
- **Center — the GOVERNOR: the CURRENT LIMITER (`ACTIVE CAP · 3–5`)** straddling the path from supply to active bay — the hero gate, where the demand-pull is admitted and the anxiety-push (rising from the bottom, amber, ghosted) is **blocked**.
- **Center-right — the ACTIVE BAY:** 3–5 **brightly lit** sockets, each a running project at full power. Directly **beneath** them, the **CONVERGENCE / KIT feed bus** pulls up `RESOURCES→4.0 · MAPS→4.1 · NEXT ACTION→3.0`, with the small **×AI KIT-ASSEMBLER** arm tapped on top ("blank page → 0"). One socket wears the off-board **→ Part 7 autonomous-runner** badge.
- **Right edge — the COMPLETION SWEEP → ARCHIVE SINK:** a finished project flips **DONE** and sweeps one-way into a dark archive; a return path shows the **freed socket** ("finish frees a slot").
- **Lower-center, prominent — the OVERLOAD PARADOX inset:** the two-state A/B (EVERYTHING ON → brown-out → completion ≈ 0 · vs · A FEW ON → full brightness → completion high). Place it where the eye lands second, right under the active bay, so the cap's logic is immediately justified. The **ONE RED** trip/brown-out lives on the LEFT half of this inset (or on the limiter itself).
- **Lower-left margin — the LEARNING sub-panel:** the just-in-case capacitor-bank stockpile (idle, self-discharging) vs the just-in-time skill-tap off a live project ( *"two faces of one pull"* ).
- **Footer strip — the operating-rules legend (one line):** **`CAP 3–5 · PARKED = GUILT-FREE · DEADLINES PULL, NOT ANXIETY · FINISH BEFORE YOU START · COMPLETION > OPEN`**, with a faint **`← 5.0 toolkit · 4.x knowledge · 3.0 workflow feed this`** continuity callback and a faint **`→ Part 7: hand a repeatable project to an agent`** pointer.

**Eye path:** the calm dark field (left) → the bright active bay + demand pull (center-right) → the overload paradox that explains the cap (down) → the completion sweep that frees the next slot (right) → the rules legend (bottom).

## 7. What to Avoid _(so it doesn't go generic)_

1. **The Kanban / Trello board — banned.** No columns of cards ("To Do / Doing / Done"), no sticky-note wall. It is the reflexive "project management" image *and it depicts the exact disease the article treats* (everything visible and in-parallel). The instrument is a **capped, demand-pulled supply**, where most projects are **dark and off-board**, not laid out as equal cards.
2. **Don't confuse it with 4.0's heat-graded storage bus.** 4.0 = *where a project is filed* (a horizontal gradient of zones by heat). 6.0 = *whether a project is drawing power right now*, governed by a **hard concurrency limiter + demand trigger**. The parked field is a **flat, undifferentiated dark array** — **no heat gradient, no PARA zones, no left-right "hotness" ramp.** The new objects (the `3–5` current limiter, the deadline pull-arrow, the overload A/B, the pre-charge kit-assembler) are what make this a different instrument.
3. **Don't make the active and dormant loads equal-weight / equal-bright.** The whole point is the **asymmetry**: a few brilliant active sockets against a vast calm dark field. Equalising them erases the thesis (priority + a hard cap, not breadth).
4. **Don't render the dormant field as alarming, cluttered, or red.** Parked = *inventory waiting for demand, costing nothing.* It must read **calm grey and intentional** — the article's reward is precisely "no guilt about the difference." The **only** red is the overload trip.
5. **Don't render the AI multiplier as a robot/assistant cartoon.** It's a **pre-charge / kit-assembler arm tapped onto the live `3.0/4.0/4.1` feed**, lit only because those lower layers are built (fundamental-before-multiplier). Point it at thin feeds and it delivers nothing. No humanoid helper.
6. **Avoid the stock "overwhelm" clichés** — the juggling/spinning-plates person, the tangled-string wall, the stressed figure at a desk, the literal Toyota factory/assembly-line photo, and the glowing-brain / lightbulb / gears / rocket kit. The overwhelm is shown as a **supply brown-out**, not a sweating cartoon.
7. **Don't red-flood, and don't redraw a sibling instrument.** Keep clear of 4.0's heat-graded storage bus, 5.0's command-bench rack, 3.0's clockwise ring, 3.1's RAM↔DISK latch, 4.1's distillation bench, 4.2's activation bench, 1.0's vertical boot-stack, and 1.1's L→R threat trace. This poster's novelty = **a capacity-capped, demand-pulled load manifold with an overload paradox.**
