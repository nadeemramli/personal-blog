## §0 — Why a new sub-archetype, and how it stays distinct

Every Productive poster gets its own instrument inside the one locked style. This one must not repeat:

- **1.0 The Productivity Stack** = a *vertical series-wired boot stack* (build **order**, current climbs once each tier's LED lights).
- **1.1 The Enemy** = a *left-to-right threat-intercept trace* (one signal under four attacks).
- **2.0 The Machine and the Room** = an *enclosure cross-section*.
- **3.0 The Workflow Engine** = a *closed clockwise processing ring* (things you **do**).
- **3.1 The Self-Improving Workflow** = a *RAM↔DISK write-back latch* across runs (persistence).

PARA is none of those. It is a **storage topology**, not a process: the article's own framing is "the workflow handles things you'll *do*; the knowledge layer handles things you'll *consult*." So the instrument is a **graded bus** sorted by one axis only — **how soon you'll act** — with **packets that flow both ways** between zones. No boot order, no single trace, no loop, no clock. The hero geometry is a *temperature/voltage gradient* plus *migration arrows*. That is new to the set.

Continuity hook (use it, it's free): the **Projects** zone physically **bonds onto the live EXECUTE rail carried over from 3.0's ring**. The article says it outright ("Each maps to a project in your workflow… two views of the same thing"). The knowledge system's hottest bucket plugs straight into the workflow's output stage. One small solder joint between this poster and 3.0's = the two halves of the system clicking together.

## §1 — The Big Idea

**One sentence:** *You don't file by what a thing is about — you file by how soon you'll touch it again, so the live work glows at the front and the dead weight cools into the dark on its own.*

The reader should feel the instant relief of an axis that **self-sorts urgency**: the Friday deadline and the article you'll never reopen stop sharing a shelf. The poster makes "where does this go?" collapse into a single four-way answer, and makes *retrieval* (the layer's only real job) look like the natural consequence of heat, not of discipline.

---

## §2 — Visual Metaphor / Structure

**The anchor: a HEAT-GRADED STORAGE BUS.** A single horizontal conductor runs left (hot, live) to right (cold, dark). Four tap-zones sit along it at descending potential — the four buckets, in the article's exact hottest→coldest order:

1. **PROJECTS** — bonded directly to the **live work rail** (the EXECUTE node from 3.0). Full cyan current, the brightest and busiest zone, dense with probe-contacts (touched daily). "Active, deadlined, ends."
2. **AREAS** — not on the live rail but **permanently energised at a low standby current** (no deadline, on forever). Its signature device: **budding spin-off taps** that continuously sprout new Project-packets onto the live rail ("the health area spins off 'book the blood panel'").
3. **RESOURCES** — a **cool high-impedance shelf**, dim, and deliberately the **physically largest bin** in the frame (encodes "default to Resources — most saved content lives here"). Connected up to Projects by a normally-open switch that **closes the moment you act**.
4. **ARCHIVES** — **de-energised, grey, behind a one-way sweep gate**, drawn small and near-invisible ("you should almost never see it").

**Why this metaphor fits the argument better than any folder diagram:**

- **Actionability = heat/potential.** The single insight of the article ("organise by how soon you'll act, not by topic") becomes one literal axis. You cannot draw "topic" as a gradient; you *can* draw "proximity to live work" as one. The form **is** the thesis.
- **It's a flow, not a cabinet.** The article's most important point ("the movement is most of the value") is rendered as **migrating charge-packets**, not static contents. The four named transitions are the four arrows (see §3).
- **Visibility scales with heat.** Brightness, size, and probe-density all rise toward Projects — a direct render of "the closer to the top, the more often you touch it and the more visible it should be."

**The myth-buster panel (the visual argument).** Off to one side, ghosted and struck through, sits the **rejected TOPIC-SORTED rack**: three dim vertical silos labelled `MARKETING · HEALTH · FINANCE`. Inside `MARKETING`, a hot `Q3 PAGE — DUE FRI` chip and a cold `saved article · 2 yrs` chip sit on the **same undifferentiated shelf with no current gradient** — no way to tell what's live. Caption: **"TOPIC CAN'T TELL YOU WHAT'S HOT NOW."** This is the "it feels logical and it fails" paragraph drawn as a dead, flat, un-prioritised board beside the living heat-bus.

## §3 — Layout Description

**Orientation: LANDSCAPE** (the gradient wants a horizontal run; also rotates the set, since 1.0/3.0-ring/Cognitive-4.0 were portrait).

Eye path: **left→right down the heat gradient, then the migration arrows pull the eye back up.**

- **Far left — THE LIVE WORK RAIL.** A thick cyan bus entering frame-left, labelled as the hand-off from 3.0's EXECUTE node ("← from The Workflow Engine"). This is the heat source; everything's temperature is defined by distance from it.
- **Left-of-centre — PROJECTS.** Bonded onto the live rail. Biggest, brightest zone. A cluster of bright item-packets, many probe-contacts, a small "= your workflow's project list" tie-line down to a mirrored row (the "two views of the same thing" note).
- **Centre — AREAS.** A horizontal standby rail under Projects, glowing dim-cyan (always-on, never deadlined). Three or four **budding taps** arc upward, each pinching off a new bright packet onto the Projects rail ("Areas spin off Projects continuously").
- **Centre-right — RESOURCES.** The large cool bin. Many faint blue-grey packets at rest. One **normally-open switch** mid-frame, caught at the instant of closing, sending a single packet **up-left into Projects** — arrow labelled **"the moment you act."** (The saved negotiation article going live.)
- **Far right — ARCHIVES.** Small, dark, behind a **one-way sweep gate**. The biggest single arrow in the composition runs **Projects → Archives**, labelled **"the day it's done"** (the satisfying sweep). A thin, faint **back-path** loops Archives → Resources/Projects, labelled "resurrect if relevant again."
- **Top band — THE ×AI MULTIPLIER (locked signature device).** A thin **CAPTURE rail** spans the whole bus as the *fundamental* ("you must get it onto the bus first"). Two bolt-ons tap off it, dark-until-capture-is-live:
  - small **×AI FILING** bolt-on over Resources ("drafts the bucket + which project/area").
  - a **bigger ×AI RETRIEVAL PROBE / RAG antenna** that reaches into **all four zones at once by *meaning*, ignoring which zone the packet sits in** → the kicker label: **"FILING MATTERS LESS · CAPTURING MATTERS MORE"** with a small "→ Part 5.0" pointer.
- **Bottom strip — ONE SCHEME, EVERY TOOL.** The same four-zone bus, miniaturised and stamped four times across tool-ports: `NOTES · FILES · CLOUD · BOOKMARKS`. Caption: **"ONE SCHEME, EVERY TOOL → the decision becomes automatic."**
- **Lower-left inset — the rejected TOPIC rack** (the §2 myth-buster), ghosted/struck.

**The ONE RED object.** Welded onto the hot Projects rail sits a single **DONE** packet that was **never swept** — still drawing current, radiating red **"NOISE"** into the live space. It's the article's explicit enemy callback ("a finished project sitting in your active space is noise — the [[1.1 enemy]]"). Caption: **"FINISHED ≠ FILED. ARCHIVE AGGRESSIVELY."** Red appears **nowhere else**: Areas standby = dim-cyan, Resources = cool blue-grey, Archives = dead grey (cold, not alarm). Coldness carries no red; only the un-swept hot item does.

## §4 — Text Elements (keep minimal)

- **Headline (pick one):**
  - **"FILE BY HEAT, NOT BY TOPIC."**
  - "ORGANISE BY WHEN YOU'LL ACT — NOT WHAT IT'S ABOUT."
- **Thesis lockup (small, under headline):** **"RETRIEVAL IS THE ONLY JOB."**
- **Quotable (corner):** *"A base you can't retrieve from is just a hoard."*
- **Four zone labels (hottest→coldest):**
  - `PROJECTS — active · deadlined · ends`
  - `AREAS — ongoing · no end · always on`
  - `RESOURCES — future reference · default here · the big cold bin`
  - `ARCHIVES — done or dormant · one-way sweep · near-invisible`
- **Four migration captions:** "the moment you act" (R→P) · "the day it's done" (P→A, the sweep) · "spins off continuously" (Area⇒P) · "resurrect if relevant" (A→R/P, faint).
- **Myth-buster caption:** "TOPIC CAN'T TELL YOU WHAT'S HOT NOW."
- **Red caption:** "FINISHED ≠ FILED · ARCHIVE AGGRESSIVELY."
- **×AI key:** "GOOD CAPTURE = fundamental · ×AI RETRIEVAL = multiplier · PERFECT FOLDERS = no longer the bottleneck."

That's the ceiling. If the board feels busy, cut the quotable and the tool-port strip first; the gradient + four zones + four arrows + one red must survive.

## §5 — Colour & Mood

Locked pillar palette, used verbatim:

- **Background ink:** `#0B0F14` (near-black).
- **Cyan `#2DD4BF`** = live current / the live rail / Projects (brightest) / closing-switch packet in motion. Brightness steps **down** across the gradient: Projects full cyan → Areas dim-cyan standby → Resources desaturated blue-grey → Archives dead grey.
- **Amber `#E8A23D`** = ordinary cautions and the rejected topic-rack (out-of-spec, not an alarm): the "default to Resources / don't over-build folders" guidance, the topic-silo strike-through.
- **ONE red `#FF5C5C`** = exactly one object — the un-swept DONE packet leaking "NOISE." Nowhere else.
- **Dim grey** = ghosted topic rack, the faint Archive back-path, de-energised Archive packets, leak-to-ground on uncaptured items.
- **Off-white** = type.

**Mood:** clinical, calm, instrument-grade — a heat map on a lab bench, not a motivational poster. The emotional beat is *relief through order*: the live zone glows, the dead weight cools and recedes, and the single red defect is the only thing demanding attention. Heat = attention; cold = peace.

## §6 — Style Reference

- Locked house style: **"Neural Signal Schematic"** — engineering/electrical schematic with restraint and personality (the Fit pillar's instrument language, ported to the productivity *system*).
- Touchstones: a **thermal-gradient bus-bar / power-distribution schematic**, a **heat map**, the clean diagrammatic logic of *Wait But Why* crossed with a real datasheet figure. Think "annotated lab instrument," not "infographic."
- Typographic register: mono or technical sans for labels; one strong condensed display cut for the headline. Hairline leader-lines, small-caps zone labels, datum/axis ticks for the heat gradient.

## §7 — What to Avoid

1. **The stock PARA diagram.** Do NOT draw four pastel rounded rectangles in a row labelled P/A/R/A, four nested folders, or a 2×2 quadrant grid. That's the cliché the whole series exists to replace; rendering it straight would erase the one insight (actionability axis + flow).
2. **Folder / filing-cabinet / cloud-icon kit, and the generic "second brain" glowing-brain.** The article explicitly *rejects* "second brain as storage" (it points forward to 4.2's tools-that-act). No glowing brains, no lightbulbs/rocket/gears, no robot-at-a-desk for the AI (AI is only a bolt-on ×multiplier governed by the live CAPTURE rail — same convention as 1.0/3.0).
3. **Red-flooding** (breaks the ONE-red rule) and **drawing the zones as equal-size bins** (kills the "visibility scales with heat / default to Resources is the biggest bin" encoding). Cold must read as *receding and calm*, never as a second alarm colour.
