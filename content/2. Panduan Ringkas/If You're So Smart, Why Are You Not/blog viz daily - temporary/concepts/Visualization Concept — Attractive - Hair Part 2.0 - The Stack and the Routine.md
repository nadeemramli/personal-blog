## Series context & the key point _(why this concept, not a generic one)_

This is the **execution payload of the 3-part Hair series** — the article that finally hands over the compounds, doses, and the daily/weekly routine. The trap with a "stack" article is that it wants to become a *list*: nine compounds in a column, a dose next to each. A list is the one thing this article is explicitly arguing against. Its real thesis is **architectural, not enumerative**: a stack is not a pile of chemicals, it is a *layered, sequenced assembly that must not fight itself.* The poster's whole job is to make the reader feel the **structure and the ordering**, not memorize the molecules (the molecules are in the article; the poster carries the reasoning).

The article makes five structural moves the poster must carry:

1. **THE central insight — 90% is the foundation, 10% is the chemistry.** *"90% of the result comes from the diagnostic loop and basic consistency… the other 10% is the chemistry."* And the killer line: *"People run this backward. They obsess over RU58841 vehicle ratios while applying minoxidil three nights a week."* The poster must make the chemistry visually *small and on top*, sitting on a massive load-bearing foundation slab of **adherence + the Part 1.2 diagnostic loop**. The proportions are the argument.
2. **A two-tier stack on a structural spine.** **Tier 1 (Pharmacy)** is the load-bearing course: oral minoxidil, topical minoxidil + tretinoin, ketoconazole, alpha-estradiol — *"Most of the response lives here."* **Tier 2 (Research)** is an *optional upper course* that *"layers on top… meaningful on top of Tier 1, trivial without it."* Tiers are floors of an assembly, not a menu.
3. **The mechanical layer is a multiplier membrane, not another drug.** *"The protocols in this section don't replace pharmacology. They multiply it."* Microneedling > scalp massage > LLLT. This is a distinct *band* in the build-up whose job is to amplify every coat above and below it — drawn as a multiplier interlayer, not as more bottles.
4. **The schedule is a sequencing problem — wet trades can't overlap.** The heart of the article is *"a daily schedule that doesn't fight itself."* Dry-time clearances (RU58841 dry 15–20 min → minoxidil → dry 10 min → tretinoin), the **morning-only / evening-only split**, ketoconazole on non-topical days. This is *construction phasing*: the same reason you don't lay a finish coat over wet primer.
5. **One hard red interlock — the microneedling breach.** The single most dangerous rule: microneedling opens transcutaneous channels, so applying RU58841 to a freshly needled scalp *"converts a topical compound into a systemic one — defeating the localization rationale."* This is the exact functional twin of Hair 1.1's condemnation line and Appearance 1.0's epistemic line: **the one hard rule the whole design points at.** Plus its conceptual cousin — the **deliberate exclusion of finasteride/dutasteride** to keep the system on a *local loop, not tied to the systemic mains*, preserving the upstairs tenant (allopregnanolone / mood / drive).

**The single thing a reader must walk away owning:** *The stack is a building, not a shopping list. It stands on a 90% foundation of adherence — get that wrong and the chemistry on top is worthless. The chemistry itself is layered: a Tier-1 structural spine, a mechanical multiplier membrane, an optional Tier-2 top course — and it only works if the coats are applied in sequence with the right gaps between them, kept on a local loop rather than wired into the systemic mains, and never breached by laying a localized topical onto a freshly needled (open) scalp.*

That is a **layered, load-ordered assembly governed by sequencing rules and a single safety interlock** — which is why the honest anchor is, again, an **architect's drawing**, but this time a **build-up / assembly section with a phasing schedule**: layers stacked by load-bearing importance, applied along a timed sequence, with dimensioned clearances and a hazard detail. The article literally hands us *"the spine,"* *"layers,"* *"layering rules,"* *"multiplies it,"* *"a schedule that doesn't fight itself,"* *"localized,"* *"systemic."* We render the assembly it's describing.

## 1. The Big Idea

_One sentence: **A hair protocol is a layered building assembly — a vast 90% foundation of adherence carrying a thin 10% of chemistry, built up in load-ordered courses (Tier-1 spine → mechanical multiplier membrane → optional Tier-2 top course), and it only holds if the coats are applied in the right sequence with the right gaps, kept on a local loop instead of the systemic mains, and never breached by a topical on an open (freshly needled) scalp.** The poster must make the reader feel, instantly, that (a) the foundation dwarfs the chemistry, (b) the chemistry is structured in floors not a list, and (c) the routine is a sequencing discipline whose one unbreakable rule is the red breach interlock._

## 2. Visual Metaphor & Structure — the hero object

**An architect's build-up / assembly section of the protocol — drawn as layered courses stacked bottom-to-top by load-bearing importance, seated on the fixed-follicle substrate, with a vertical 24-hour phasing schedule running up the side that times each coat and dimensions the mandatory dry-time clearances between them.** This is the anchor; nothing replaces it. The protocol is drawn as what the article insists it is: *an assembly that must not fight itself* — so it renders as a wall/coating build-up detail, the kind of section an architect draws to specify exactly what goes on, in what order, with what gaps.

Read bottom-to-top for **hierarchy** (what carries what), and read the side rail top-to-bottom for **the day's sequence** (when each coat goes on). The two axes are the two halves of the article: *structure* and *routine*.

- **BELOW GRADE — the fixed substrate (callback to Hair 1.1).** At the very base, the **follicle lot** drawn in ghosted graphite as the ground the whole assembly sits in, with a one-line callback caption: `the living follicles Part 1.1 said are still left of the line.` Everything above only works because there is salvageable structure beneath it. (Direct visual rhyme with 1.1's fixed-parcel ground line — the two Hair posters share a foundation.)

- **THE FOUNDATION SLAB — the 90% (massive, load-bearing, the largest single object).** A thick, dominant terracotta foundation course captioned **`THE 90% · adherence + the diagnostic loop`**, sub-label `hit it every day · hold the Month-3 shed · re-audit at 6 & 12 mo`. It must be visibly *enormous* relative to everything stacked on it — this proportion is the whole point. A small dimension note hammers it: `everything above is the 10%.` A faint hairline-crack motif at one corner carries the failure line *`skip two weeks and the slab cracks — the coats above go with it.`*

- **TIER-1 COURSE — the structural spine (warm, solid, the real building).** The primary built course resting on the slab, labelled `TIER 1 · THE PHARMACY SPINE — most of the response lives here`. Four neat structural blocks, each a labelled course-stone with dose as a dimension tick in the cool instrument register: `oral minoxidil 2.5–5 mg` · `topical minoxidil 5% + tretinoin 0.025%` · `ketoconazole 2% (or 1%)` · `alpha-estradiol 0.025%`. Drawn blueprint-crisp and warm — this is the load-bearing masonry.

- **THE MECHANICAL MEMBRANE — the multiplier interlayer (a distinct hatched band, not bottles).** A thin, full-width interlayer drawn *between* Tier 1 and Tier 2, rendered as a structural membrane with a multiplier glyph `×`, captioned `THE MECHANICAL LAYER — doesn't replace the chemistry, multiplies it`. Three ribs in leverage order: `microneedling 1.0–1.5 mm ×weekly` (boldest) · `scalp massage 4–5 min daily` · `LLLT 650–670 nm, 3–4× wk`. Small leaders show it *amplifying the courses above and below* (little upward/downward gain arrows) — it is the only band whose job is to make its neighbours work harder.

- **TIER-2 COURSE — the optional top course (warm but lighter, drawn as an add-on).** A clearly *additive* upper course, set slightly inset and lighter-weight, labelled `TIER 2 · THE RESEARCH STACK — meaningful on top, trivial without`: `RU58841 5%` · `MK-677 20 mg (8-wk cycles)` · `GHK-Cu 2 mg SubQ EOD` · `KPV 500 mcg SubQ daily`. Two of these (`MK-677`, `retatrutide`) carry a small **cycle clock** glyph to mark *"cycle these, not the rest."* It must read as *removable* — the building stands without it.

- **THE LOCAL-LOOP DETAIL (the finasteride exclusion, a side callout off the spine).** Off to one side of Tier 1, a small services detail: the protocol drawn as a **closed local loop at the scalp**, with the **systemic mains drawn ghosted and deliberately DISCONNECTED** (a clean break-symbol), captioned `KEPT LOCAL ON PURPOSE — no finasteride/dutasteride wired to the mains`. Above the disconnect, a faint protected "upstairs" room labelled `allopregnanolone · mood · drive — left intact`. This carries the article's single most important *reasoning* move in one architectural gesture: localized blockade preserves the upstairs tenant.

- **═══ THE BREACH INTERLOCK ═══ (the one red object — the article's hard line).** The single alarm-red mark on the poster: a **hazard detail at the membrane** showing what happens when microneedling (open channels) meets a localized topical. Drawn as a sealed-surface-vs-breached-surface pictogram with a red interlock symbol and the rule `MICRONEEDLE NIGHT = TOPICALS OFF. resume +24h (RU58841 +48h).` A short red caption states the consequence the way the article does: `a needled scalp turns a LOCAL topical SYSTEMIC — the breach defeats the whole point.` This is the exact functional twin of 1.1's condemnation line: the hardest mark on the page, and everything else is calm graphite around it.

**THE PHASING RAIL — the 24-hour sequence (right or left margin, the "routine" axis).** A vertical day-axis in the cool instrument register, ticking the actual schedule and — critically — drawing the **dry-time clearances as dimensioned gaps** (the way an architect dimensions a clearance), because *the gaps are the lesson*:
`8:00 AM ▸ oral min + alpha-E2 (AM only)` → `9:00 PM ▸ RU58841 ▸▏dry 15–20 min▕` → `9:30 PM ▸ minoxidil ▸▏dry 10 min▕→ tretinoin` → `M/W/F ▸ ketoconazole (non-topical window)` → `weekly ▸ microneedle ▸ TOPICALS OFF` . A bracket spans the morning tick and the evening block labelled `AM / PM SPLIT — never one saturation event`. The clearance gaps are drawn as literal empty dimensioned spaces — *the white space is doing work.*

**The two-register surface (the device, reused).** Everything **structural and living is the warm "build register"** — terracotta course-stones, blueprint leaders, dimension ticks, drawn as *assembled and load-bearing*. Everything **measured/timed is the cool "instrument register"** — doses, the phasing rail, gauges, drawn as the observing schedule. The lone **red** is reserved for the breach interlock and the foundation-crack failure note. The surface itself teaches *structure vs schedule vs hazard* before a word is read.

Why this metaphor fits and nothing else does: the article's literal claims are *a foundation that dwarfs the chemistry* (proportion), *tiers that stack* (load order), *a layer that multiplies* (interlayer), *a routine that mustn't fight itself* (sequencing with clearances), and *one localization rule that must not break* (a single interlock). A bottom-to-top assembly section with a timed phasing rail is the only object that holds all five at once — it is proportional, layered, sequence-able, clearance-able, and interlock-able. A flat list of nine compounds (the obvious move) collapses hierarchy into enumeration and *hides the 90/10 proportion and the sequencing* — the two ideas the article exists to install. A circular daily-routine clock would imply *order doesn't matter, it all comes round* — the opposite of the dry-time/breach discipline.

## 3. Layout Description

**Portrait or tall-square (~3:4 / 4:5)** is primary here — opposite to 1.1's landscape survey — because the hero is a **vertical build-up** (hierarchy reads up) with a **vertical phasing rail** beside it. (A landscape variant works for a wide blog hero by setting the assembly left and the phasing rail + spec sheet as a right column; portrait is primary and is the more honest "section drawing" format.)

- **Top band (~12%):** the headline (see §4), set small against the bone paper, kerned wide, thin drafting border beneath it, and a tiny `Part 2.0 of 3 · Hair · the Attractive pillar · the final article` slug. A faint upward glyph `↑ LOAD-BEARING ORDER` primes the bottom-to-top reading.
- **Center stage (~60%) — the hero:** the assembly section, seated on the ghosted follicle substrate, built up: **foundation slab (90%, dominant) → Tier-1 spine → mechanical multiplier membrane → Tier-2 top course**, with the **local-loop detail** branching off the spine and the **red breach interlock** at the membrane. The **phasing rail** runs full-height down the right (or left) margin, its dimensioned dry-time gaps aligned to the relevant courses. The foundation slab should occupy *roughly half the vertical mass of the assembly* — non-negotiable proportion.
- **Lower-third spec sheet (~16%) — the impact hierarchy, docked as an instrument card:** the article's own honest ranking, drawn as a leverage ladder in the cool register: `1 catch it early (left of the line) · 2 daily adherence · 3 Tier-1 spine · 4 mechanical multiplier · 5 Tier-2 · 6 transplant`. Plus the **90/10 grace note** as a single proportion bar (a slim bar, 90% terracotta / 10% slate) captioned `chemistry is the 10%.`
- **Bottom band (~12%):** the thesis lockup (see §4) plus the **sequencing micro-strip**: three chips — `STACK BY LOAD · APPLY IN SEQUENCE · NEVER BREACH THE LOOP` — color-coded warm / cool / red to the three registers.

Reading path is deliberate and matches the argument: **enter at the ghosted follicle substrate (callback) → feel the massive 90% slab → climb the Tier-1 spine → cross the multiplier membrane (and clock the red breach interlock) → reach the removable Tier-2 course → glance to the local-loop detail (why no finasteride) → run the phasing rail to learn the order and the gaps → drop to the impact ladder → exit on the sequencing strip.**

## 5. Color & Mood

**Mood:** the same calm, exacting confidence of a drafting table as the rest of the Attractive pillar — precise, grown-up, unhurried — here in its most *constructive* register: an engineer specifying a build-up, dimensioning the clearances, and flagging the one detail that must not be got wrong. Not pharmacy-ad hype, not a busy "ultimate stack" infographic. The feeling of a spec sheet you could hand to a contractor: this course carries the load, this gap is mandatory, this interlock is non-negotiable. The single red detail is where the calm picks up its one note of consequence.

**Palette — the locked Attractive signature, carried over verbatim from Appearance 1.0 / Hair 1.1:**
- **Background:** warm bone / drafting paper (`#F4F1EA`), faint blueprint grid or drafting-dot texture, very low contrast.
- **Ink / linework / type:** graphite near-black (`#1E2024`) for all structure and labels.
- **Accent 1 — LIVING / load-bearing warm (`#C2703D` terracotta):** the **foundation slab, the Tier-1 spine, the Tier-2 course, the mechanical membrane's gain arrows** — everything that is *built, assembled, and acting on living tissue.* Warm = alive, load-bearing, the real building.
- **Accent 2 — MEASUREMENT / schedule cool (`#5E7585` slate-blue):** the **instrument layer only** — the dose dimension ticks, the 24-hour phasing rail, the dry-time clearance dimensions, the impact-ladder card. Cool = the observing/timing register, never the tissue itself. (Same role it played in 1.1: the cooler, lighter register that *measures and schedules* rather than carries load.)
- **The fixed substrate & the systemic mains:** ghosted graphite / grey only — the follicle lot below grade (present, essential, un-creatable — the 1.1 callback) and the deliberately-disconnected systemic mains in the local-loop detail (drawn but cut off on purpose).
- **One alarm color — the breach only (`#D9483B` signal red):** used *exclusively* for the **microneedling breach interlock** and the small **foundation-crack failure note** (`skip two weeks and the slab cracks`). Scarcity is what gives it force — red = the rule you must not break and the failure you must not cause. (Faithful reuse of the locked "red-for-failure/hazard only" rule; in 1.1 red was fibrosis-the-irreversible-failure, here it is the breach-the-self-inflicted-failure.)

The warm-build / cool-schedule / lone-red split does double duty: it's handsome *and* it encodes *structure vs routine vs the one hard rule*, so the color teaches the article's three layers before a word is read.

## 7. What to Avoid

- **The cliché of a flat compound list / "supplement stack" grid — nine bottles in a column with doses beside them.** It is the obvious move and it is exactly the framing the article refuses (*"a stack is not a pile of chemicals"*). A list destroys the two ideas the poster exists to install: the **90/10 proportion** and the **sequencing discipline**. Bottles, pills, and pipette icons must never be the hero; doses appear only as small dimension ticks on the course-stones.
- **A circular 24-hour clock / daily-routine wheel as the hero.** A wheel implies *it all comes round and order is interchangeable* — the opposite of the article's dry-time clearances and the breach interlock, where *sequence and gaps are everything.* The schedule must be a *linear, dimensioned phasing rail*, never a clock face.
- (Bonus to avoid: flattening the foundation slab so the chemistry looks equal-sized, or softening the red breach interlock into just another step. If the 90% slab isn't visually dominant and the interlock isn't the single hardest mark on the page, the poster has quietly told the reader the chemistry is the point and the rules are optional — precisely the "run it backward" mistake the article is built to refuse.)
