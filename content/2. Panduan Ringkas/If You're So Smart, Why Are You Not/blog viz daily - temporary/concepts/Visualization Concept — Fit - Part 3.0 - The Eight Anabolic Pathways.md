## Series context & the key point _(why this concept, not a generic one)_

Part 3.0 opens the **"Optimize" sub-arc** of the Performance Enhancement track (1: the decision → 2: monitoring → **3: optimize** → 4: protection → 5: assembly). It is the first article in the whole PED series that is actually about _what to nudge_, and it is built entirely on one contrarian thesis from Vigorous Steve:

> **Stop body-slamming one pathway.** Most enhanced athletes do exactly one thing — push more and more androgen at the androgen receptor, higher and higher, chasing a single mechanism until the side effects pile up and the Part 2 health markers fall apart. The opposite move wins: **marginally exploit multiple pathways.** Each of eight distinct anabolic pathways contributes maybe an extra **5–10%**, and tapping all of them adds up to _more total muscle than abusing any single compound, with vastly fewer health risks._

The article is explicit that these are "**levers to nudge,** not drugs to take" — and the deepest, most-missed nuance is that **not every lever goes up.** Each pathway has an _optimal window_, and the skill is parking each in its window, not maxing any:

- **Raise** — Androgen Receptor (AR), and gently the Progesterone Receptor (PR), and the powerful gated GH/IGF-1/Insulin group.
- **Keep in range, do NOT crush** — SHBG (it's a delivery service, not just a sponge; drive it to zero and you break the mail route) and Estrogen (it has its _own_ independent anabolism + protects joints, bone, libido, lipids; the "AI trap" of crashing it is a textbook newbie error).
- **Block / pull down** — Cortisol (Glucocorticoid + Mineralocorticoid): these don't build muscle up, they stop it breaking down (anti-catabolic).

Two more load-bearing ideas the visual must carry: (1) the **90/10 prerequisite** — "the pathways are the margin; if training, calories, protein, and sleep aren't already excellent, you're nudging levers attached to nothing"; and (2) **choose by job, not hype** — testosterone is the base ("increase everything beyond what your testicles produce naturally"), and _every other compound must answer a sharper question: what does it add that test alone, at a sane dose, doesn't?_

**The single key point to make a reader feel:** _Eight small, correct nudges beat one violent shove. The win isn't a bigger dose on one channel — it's all eight channels parked in their green windows, summing to a fuller output with no clipping._

This is the concept a generic chat would miss: it would draw a labelled diagram of receptors, or a list of eight drugs. The _real_ payload is the **mixing-desk logic** — eight faders, each with a target band, where the failure mode is one fader railed to max while everything else is ignored, and the master meter clips into the red (= the Part 2 health markers falling apart).

## 1. The Big Idea

_Building muscle on enhancement is a mixing job, not a power-lifting job. Eight channels, each with an optimal window — nudge all of them into the green and the summed output is higher and cleaner than slamming any one channel to max. The amateur rails the testosterone fader and clips the master into the red (side effects, wrecked health markers). The professional rides eight faders a little, and the board never distorts._ The poster is a control surface a reader studies to re-frame "what should I take?" as "which channel, which window, and why" — not a motivational image, and not a drug list.

## 2. Visual Metaphor & Structure

A **horizontal mixing console** — the audio/broadcast mixing desk reimagined as a physiological control surface. The metaphor is near-perfect for this article and earns its place three ways:

1. **"Levers to nudge"** is literally what a fader is. The article's own word for the eight pathways is _levers_.
2. **Every channel has a target window, not a "max."** A mixing engineer doesn't push every fader to the top — they ride each to its right level and watch the master meter. That is exactly the article's "marginally exploit, keep in window" thesis, expressed by the instrument's native logic.
3. **The failure mode draws itself.** One channel railed to maximum while the rest sit dead, and the **master output clips into red** — that _is_ "body-slamming one pathway until the side effects pile up and the health markers fall apart." No extra explanation needed.

Eight **channel strips** run left to right, one per pathway. Each strip is a vertical fader travelling inside a marked **target window** (a green band the fader should sit in), plus a small set of channel chrome (direction-of-nudge arrow, family tag, the by-the-job compound, and the OTC "trim" knob). On the right, a **master section** sums everything into a single **NET MUSCLE** output meter, gated by a **90/10 power switch** (the baseline signal the whole board amplifies). A ghosted **failure overlay** shows the wrong way: AR railed, all else dead, master clipping.

This is a deliberately _different instrument_ from the prior PE posters — 1.0 was an authorization console, 2.0 a calibration bench, 2.1 a telemetry strip-chart — but it lives in the same locked "Engineering Diagnostic Instrument" language. The console keeps the pillar varied yet unmistakably one body of work.

## 3. Layout — exact composition

Orientation: **landscape / wide** (a console is natively horizontal; also a deliberate flip back to landscape after the portrait calibration-bench, giving the PED set rhythm). It still embeds cleanly inline and reads as one left-to-right sweep.

Zones:

1. **Kicker (top-left):** mono tag `FIT · PE · PART 3.0 — THE EIGHT ANABOLIC PATHWAYS`, preceded by a tick/rule so it reads like a console label-strip.
2. **Headline block (top, large):** headline (copy §4) in the display face, two lines allowed; one-line subhead beneath in muted secondary text.
3. **The thesis chip (thin band under the subhead):** a compact legend establishing the whole argument in one line — `BODY-SLAM ONE PATHWAY  ✕   →   NUDGE EIGHT, +5–10% EACH  ✓`. Quiet technical chrome; primes the board below.
4. **The 90/10 POWER RAIL (far-left vertical spine of the console, ~8%):** the input the console amplifies. A labelled **power switch / input-gain stage** — `BASELINE SIGNAL IN: training · calories · protein · sleep` — feeding a master bus that runs along the bottom of all eight channels. Drawn so it is visually obvious the eight faders do nothing without this signal. Caption: `The board amplifies a signal. No baseline = nudging levers attached to nothing.` This carries the prerequisite without a separate box.
5. **The eight channel strips (HERO — give them the most area, the central ~62%):** eight vertical strips in a row, left→right in the article's order. Each strip carries, top to bottom:
    - **Channel number + pathway name + receptor** (mono header).
    - **Direction-of-nudge glyph** — a small up-arrow (`RAISE`), a centred bracket (`HOLD IN WINDOW`), or a down-arrow (`BLOCK / LOWER`). This is the teaching object: the eight arrows are NOT all the same direction.
    - **The fader in its target window** — a vertical track with a green band marking the optimal zone and the fader cap parked _inside_ it. For "hold" channels the green band is a narrow mid-track window with **amber caution zones** at both extremes (crush-it / overdo-it). For "block" channels the green sits low.
    - **Family tag chip** — `(T)` / `(DHT)` / `(19)` so both lenses (pathway + family) read at once.
    - **The by-the-job compound** — one line, the lead example named generically (e.g. AR: _Testosterone — the base_; SHBG: _Proviron (DHT) — frees test, no AI needed_).
    - **The OTC "trim" knob** — a small secondary control = the legal, dose-given supplement lever for that channel (this is the only place doses appear, per the article's house rule). E.g. AR: `trim — L-Carnitine L-Tartrate 2 g/d ↑ AR density`.
    - Channels (left→right): **① AR — Androgen Receptor** (RAISE; T base; trim LCLT/zinc/Vit-D) · **② SHBG — Binding-Globulin Complex** (HOLD, don't crush; Proviron DHT; trim Boron 10 mg/d) · **③ ER — Estrogen Receptor** (HOLD in range; AIs sparingly; trim omega-3 + pin-frequency) · **④ PR — Progesterone Receptor** (RAISE gently; 19-nors; trim P5P 50–200 mg/d for prolactin) · **⑤+⑥ GR/MR — Cortisol** (BLOCK / anti-catabolic; Tren 19; trim Ashwagandha 300–600 mg/d) · **⑦+⑧ GH · IGF-1 · INSULIN** (the powerful gated group; drawn as a single wider, fenced strip with an interlock).
    - **Two channels carry an amber over-drive LED** — PR (`prolactin tax`) and ER (`AI trap → joints, libido, lipids, blunted gains`) — lit only in the failure overlay (see §5). These are the article's two loudest "more is worse" warnings.
6. **The master section (right ~22%):** the payoff.
    - **NET MUSCLE master meter** — a single large output VU/bar showing the _summed_ result of eight nudged channels, sitting high and **clean (in the green, no clip)**.
    - **Two ghosted comparison readings beside it:** `ONE PATHWAY, SLAMMED` (lower net output **+ red CLIP**) vs `EIGHT, NUDGED` (higher net, clean). The counter-intuitive truth — multi-pathway nets _more_ — is the whole article, shown as two meter heights.
    - **`CHOOSE BY JOB, NOT HYPE` routing plate** — a small note: _Test is the base. Every other channel must answer: what does it add that test alone, at a sane dose, doesn't?_
7. **The failure overlay (ghosted, integrated — NOT a separate box):** the wrong way, drawn _on the same console_ as a faint second state — channel ① railed to the top, channels ②–⑧ dead at zero, the amber over-drive LEDs lit on PR/ER, and the master meter **clipping into red**. Caption: `Body-slamming one pathway. More dose, more sides, Part 2 markers falling apart — for less total muscle.`
8. **Footer:** the family-pattern grace note as a one-line legend — `Pattern: DHT-family → SHBG/restraint levers · 19-nor → PR/cortisol levers · T-family → the aromatizing base` — plus the rail-state key (`green = in window · amber = over-driven · red = master clip`) and the series mark `Fit — Performance Enhancement`.

**Eye path:** kicker → headline → thesis chip → drop to the 90/10 power switch on the left (nothing runs without it) → sweep the eight faders left→right, reading each direction-arrow and noticing they differ → land on the master NET MUSCLE meter → catch the two ghosted comparison heights (eight beats one) → register the faint failure overlay (one railed fader, clipping master) → footer legend. One left-to-right read with a single payoff on the right.

## 5. UX / visual encoding expectations _(semantic — colors come from design.md, not from here)_

- **The eight direction-arrows are the lesson before any text is read.** A viewer must see at a glance that the faders are NOT all pushed up — some say RAISE, some HOLD (centred bracket), some BLOCK (down). That mixed pattern _is_ the article's correction of "more = better." Protect it; make the arrows large and unmistakable.
- **Every channel shows a target window, not a ceiling.** The green band + parked fader teaches "optimal zone." For the two HOLD channels (SHBG, ER), put **amber caution zones at both extremes** so "crush it" and "overdo it" both read as wrong — the window is the point.
- **Red is rationed to exactly the master clip.** The single red object on the poster is the **clipping master meter in the failure overlay**. Use **amber** (caution role) for the PR/ER over-drive LEDs and the HOLD-channel extremes — they are warnings, not catastrophes. Do not invent new hues; map to design.md success / caution / danger roles. (Consistent with the PED set: red is reserved for the one true failure, ~1–2 objects max.)
- **The master comparison must be pre-attentive.** `EIGHT NUDGED` taller-and-clean vs `ONE SLAMMED` shorter-and-clipping should land in under a second — that height inversion is the counter-intuitive core ("multi-pathway nets _more_"). If a reader takes only the master section, they should still get the thesis.
- **The 90/10 power switch must visibly gate the board.** It should read that the eight faders are inert without the baseline signal — wiring, a power LED, a greyed-out board state behind it, whatever the system expresses. The prerequisite is structural, not a footnote.
- **Hierarchy of attention:** headline → the eight-channel board (hero) → the master NET MUSCLE meter + comparison → the failure overlay (faint) → power switch and footer legend (quiet chrome). Within each strip: the **direction-arrow + pathway name** loudest, the compound and trim supporting.
- **The GH/IGF-1/Insulin strip is wider and fenced.** Give ⑦+⑧ a slightly larger, interlocked treatment (a guard/fence motif) to read as "most powerful, most gated" — tying to the monitoring interlock from Part 2.1 without re-explaining it.
- **Restraint = the mood.** Status indicators on a precision desk, not a highlighter. Dark console ground, mono labels, ruler-drawn channel dividers; color rationed to the windows (green), the cautions (amber), and the one master clip (red). Generous negative space between strips.
- **Saveability:** use shape/position as well as hue (arrow direction, fader height, band placement) so the board survives grayscale and a phone screen — a reader should still parse RAISE/HOLD/BLOCK with color stripped.

## 7. What to Avoid

1. **A receptor/biology diagram or a labelled drug list.** The obvious moves are "draw the eight receptors with binding arrows" or "list eight compounds in a grid." Both bury the thesis. The upgrade is the **console**: eight faders with _different directions_ and _target windows_, where the instrument's own logic ("don't rail one channel") carries the argument.
2. **Eight identical up-arrows / a uniform equaliser.** If every fader points up, the poster says "more of everything" — the exact opposite of the article. The mixed RAISE / HOLD / BLOCK pattern is non-negotiable; SHBG and ER must visibly resist being pushed.
3. **Drawing the failure state as a separate "bad" panel, or flooding it with red.** Keep it as a faint overlay on the _same_ board (one railed fader, dead channels, one clipping master) so "right vs wrong" is the same instrument in two states. Red stays rationed to the single master clip; everything cautionary is amber. A rainbow console teaches noise; a one-clip console teaches the lesson.
