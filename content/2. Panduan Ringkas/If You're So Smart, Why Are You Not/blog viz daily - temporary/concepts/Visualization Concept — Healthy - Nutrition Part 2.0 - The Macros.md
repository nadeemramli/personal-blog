## Series context & the key point _(why this concept, not a generic one)_

Part 1 named four levers and a scoreboard and argued nutrition is a *control system, not a religion.* Part 2 does the unglamorous, load-bearing thing: **it puts the actual numbers on each lever.** The danger with a "macros" article is that it slides into the single most generic graphic in all of fitness — a balanced pie of protein/fat/carbs — which silently destroys the article's real claim. Because the real claim of Part 2 is **not** "here are your three macro slices." It is this:

> **The four levers are not four equal dials you balance. Each lever has a different *kind* of constraint, and you set them in a fixed order that ends in subtraction.** `Set protein → set calories → set fat floor → carbs are what's left.`

The load-bearing claims the poster must carry, in priority order:

1. **PROTEIN is a FLOOR, not a ceiling — and it has a hard number.** `1.6–2.2 g/kg of bodyweight per day.` Lower end (~1.6) at maintenance; upper end (~2.2) on a cut, when protein protects muscle in a deficit. Above ~2.5 g/kg "adds nothing for natural lifters — just more digestion." The reason it's a *floor* and set first: it's the only macro where falling **below** the line has direct, mostly-irreversible cost in the span of a single phase. The article's sharpest line: *"Falling below your carb target costs you a workout. Falling below your protein target for weeks costs you tissue."* Distribute it: `~0.3–0.4 g/kg per feeding, 3–5 feedings/day` (≈25–40 g a bolus).
2. **CALORIES are the DIRECTION lever.** Surplus / maintenance / deficit, set by goal, *measured by the weight trend* (handoff to Part 1's scoreboard). They decide whether weight moves up or down; protein decides whether the moving weight is muscle or fat.
3. **FAT is a FLOOR *and* a CEILING — a band, not a point.** `Floor ~0.8–1.0 g/kg` (hormones/testosterone, fat-soluble vitamins A·D·E·K, satiety, cell membranes). Soft ceiling because fat is **9 kcal/g** — the densest macro, the easiest to overshoot on a cut, and every extra gram of fat is a gram you didn't spend on training carbs.
4. **CARBS are the FLEX lever — the literal remainder.** `Carbs (g) = (Total kcal − protein kcal − fat kcal) ÷ 4.` They are the *output* of the sequence, not an input: pushed up on a bulk, pulled down on a cut. Quality > amount once the totals add up; timing is "mostly oversold" (~2% margin for most lifters).
5. **The calibrated bench produces a real day — within a band, for months.** The 80 kg lean-recomp worked example is the proof: `Protein 160 g · Fat 72 g · Carbs 378 g · ≈2,800 kcal.` And the tolerances that make it livable: `±5% on protein, ±10% on the rest` — *"hitting macros to the gram is for contest prep; hitting them within a band, on most days, for months, is for everyone else, and it works."* The fad diets (keto / IF / carnivore / clean) only ever "work" when they accidentally pull the right levers, and fail when they break a lever you needed.

**The single thing a reader must walk away owning:** *Macros aren't a balanced ratio you split — they're one ordered calculation. Protein is a hard floor (1.6–2.2 g/kg) set first and never breached; calories are the direction; fat is a band you hold above a floor and below a ceiling; carbs are simply what's left. Set them in that order, within a band, and the day builds itself.*

That is a **fixed-order, four-different-constraints, ends-in-subtraction object** — which is why the only honest anchor is a **calibration bench**: the same four levers from Part 1, but now with their real scales engraved, each shaped by its own constraint (hard floor / direction gate / floor-and-ceiling band / free remainder), wired into a calculation chain that literally subtracts its way to the carbs number and prints a real day. The article hands us the words *floor, ceiling, lever, flex, what's left, the math, the worked example.* We take it at its word and engrave the gauges.

## 1. The Big Idea

_One sentence: **Nutrition 2.0 is the calibration bench where the four abstract levers from Part 1 finally get their real numbers — and the poster makes the reader feel, in one glance, that "set your macros" is not four equal dials to balance but one ordered calculation with four differently-shaped constraints, ending in carbs as nothing more than what's left over after protein, calories, and the fat floor are locked.** The reader should see the protein hard-stop, the fat band, and the carbs remainder and instantly understand the order is the whole game._

## 2. Visual Metaphor & Structure — the hero object

**A dark nutrition CALIBRATION BENCH of "you": the four levers from Part 1, now drawn as precision gauges with their real numeric scales engraved, each with a *different* constraint geometry, wired left-to-right into a CALCULATION CHAIN — `SET PROTEIN → SET CALORIES → HOLD FAT FLOOR → CARBS = WHAT'S LEFT` — that subtracts its way into a free-floating carbs window and prints a calibrated "day ticket" (the 80 kg worked example) at the end.** This is the anchor; nothing replaces it.

**The four calibrated levers (the spine — left two-thirds, in pull-order `①②③④`). The non-negotiable design rule: they must NOT look identical. Their *difference in constraint shape* is the entire article.**

- **① PROTEIN — the HARD-FLOOR gauge.** A vertical gauge with the scale **engraved `1.6 — 2.2 g/kg`** as a bright cyan "SET" band. A machined **hard-stop / detent at the bottom of that band** (you cannot push the lever below it — "a floor, not a ceiling"). Above ~2.5 g/kg: a **greyed, hatched dead-zone** labelled `no more muscle — just more digestion`. **Below the floor: the ONE scarce saturated-red zone on the poster** (see below), stamped `↓ below this for weeks = lost tissue`. A small **distribution comb** clipped to the side: `0.3–0.4 g/kg × 3–5 feedings (~25–40 g each)`. Tag: `① SET FIRST · NEVER LET IT DRIFT.`
- **② CALORIES — the master DIRECTION gate.** The **largest throw** on the bench, a three-position selector `SURPLUS ↑ (+~250) · MAINTENANCE — (0) · DEFICIT ↓ (−~300)`. Caption: `② the direction of weight change · measured by the TREND, not a formula` (a thin wire nods back to Part 1's steering-wheel scoreboard). This is the lever that decides which way the scale moves.
- **③ FAT — the FLOOR-AND-CEILING band gauge.** Two detents: a **low floor** `0.8–1.0 g/kg` (engraved beside it, tiny: `hormones · vitamins A·D·E·K · satiety · membranes`) and a **soft high ceiling** (engraved `9 kcal/g — densest macro`). The **lit safe band is the travel *between* them**; an **amber temptation marker** sits at the ceiling: `easiest to overshoot on a cut`. Tag: `③ HOLD THE FLOOR · RESPECT THE CEILING.`
- **④ CARBS — the FLEX / REMAINDER window.** Deliberately drawn *without* detents — free travel — but its value is **computed, not set**: a **subtraction read-out window** fed by an arrow carrying `(Total kcal − protein kcal − fat kcal) ÷ 4`. Tag: `④ WHAT'S LEFT · UP ON A BULK ↑ · DOWN ON A CUT ↓`. It is visibly the *output* of the chain, the only lever you touch after the other three are locked.

**The calculation chain (the device that makes 2.0 ≠ 1.0).** An engraved sequence rail runs along the bench: `SET PROTEIN → SET CALORIES → HOLD FAT FLOOR → CARBS ARE WHAT'S LEFT`, drawn as a physical pipeline where the **calorie budget flows in, protein-kcal and fat-kcal are subtracted off at junctions, and the remainder pours into the carbs window.** Subtraction, made literal.

**The calibrated "DAY TICKET" (proof the bench works — docked right).** A printed readout slip, the 80 kg lean-recomp worked example, mono-spaced like a scale receipt:

```
80 kg · LEAN RECOMP
PROTEIN  160 g  (640 kcal)   ← 2.0 g/kg
FAT       72 g  (648 kcal)   ← 0.9 g/kg
CARBS    378 g  (1,512 kcal) ← what's left
TOTAL  ≈ 2,800 kcal  (maintenance)
tolerance:  ±5% protein · ±10% the rest
```

**The ONE scarce saturated-red object (single-alarm-color discipline).** Reserved for the **"below the protein floor" zone** on lever ①. This is the article's deepest, most irreversible claim — *"below your protein target for weeks costs you tissue"* — and the only place where dropping a lever has a real, mostly-permanent cost. Everything else stays calm cyan/amber. (This is deliberately a *different* scarce-red target than Nutrition 1.0's severed feedback wire — each poster spends its one red on its own sharpest claim.)

**Docked secondaries (small — the calibration bench must dominate):**
1. **The tolerance stamp.** A faint engraved plate near the day ticket: `±5% PROTEIN · ±10% THE REST — to the gram is for contest prep.`
2. **The miscalibrated-lever shelf (the fad-diet teardown).** A dim bottom shelf of **three jammed/forced levers**, each a real lever pushed *past its own constraint*: `KETO` (carbs lever rammed to ≈0, a small training-fuel light dimmed out), `IF` (the eating window compressed so the protein gauge **can't reach its floor**), `CARNIVORE` (the fiber/quality gauge forced to 0). Caption: `they "work" only when they accidentally pull the right levers — and fail when they break one you needed.` Rendered in **amber** (the seductive-but-wrong color), never the scarce red.
3. **The Malaysian feasibility readout.** A tiny affordability gauge clipped to the protein lever: `protein floor is affordable — eggs + chicken + OTG/HK powder ≈ < RM 15/day.` (Keeps the house "90/10 + Malaysian context" convention; a grace note, not a co-star.)

**Why this metaphor and nothing else:** the article's real subject is not "what to eat" — it's that the four macros are set by *four different rules in one fixed order that ends in subtraction.* Only a calibration bench holds all four constraint-shapes at once: a hard floor you can't breach, a direction gate, a floor-and-ceiling band, and a free remainder fed by a subtraction. A macro **pie/donut** is the single most dangerous trap (see §7): it renders the four as co-equal slices of a ratio and annihilates the order, the floor/ceiling/remainder distinction, and the lever idea in one stroke. The bench is the only shape that is simultaneously a credible instrument *and* the precise correction the article is making.

## 5. Color & Mood

**Mood:** the dry, exacting calm of a metrology lab after dark — a bench where each instrument is being set to its true number. Clinical, precise, quietly authoritative — **not** a bright "healthy eating" poster. The feeling is *measurement and order*, with one cold spot of danger under the protein floor.

**Palette (inherits the locked Healthy "Clinical Nocturne" tokens — verbatim where possible):**
- **Background:** deep near-black indigo / ink (`#0B0F1A`), barely-there top-to-bottom gradient — the bench surface, not pure black.
- **Structure / labels / engraved scales:** soft off-white (`#E6E8EE`) + muted slate for secondary type — never harsh pure white.
- **Accent — the CALIBRATED INSTRUMENT you trust:** the cool **clinical cyan / teal** family — the four lever gauges, the engraved numeric scales, the calculation chain, the day ticket. Cool = "set right, measured, trustworthy."
- **Accent — the WARM thing that misleads:** **amber / ember** (`#E8A23D`) for the seductive-but-wrong layer — the fat **ceiling overshoot** marker and the three **jammed fad-diet levers**. (Carries Nutrition 1.0 / Blueprint's device: the warm color is the one you can't fully trust — here, "the lever forced past its rule.")
- **The danger band:** a **dim, low-saturation red** for the hatched `>2.5 g/kg` protein dead-zone (waste, not harm).
- **One scarce SATURATED red object:** the **"below the protein floor" zone** on lever ① — the single high-saturation red on the whole poster, because that is the one lever-drop with a real, mostly-irreversible cost.
- **Noise vs signal carries:** engraved *set* bands glow cyan; everything outside a lever's safe travel is dim — so "the safe band is the answer, outside it is wrong" is read in value before a word.

The cyan-calibrated / amber-misleading split does double duty: handsome, and it encodes the thesis — the calm cyan gauges are the levers set correctly; the warm amber levers are the ones forced past their rule.

## 7. What to Avoid

- **A balanced macro PIE / DONUT chart — the single most dangerous trap.** It *looks* perfectly on-topic and **destroys the article**: it renders protein/fat/carbs as co-equal slices of a ratio, killing the *fixed order*, the *floor-vs-ceiling-vs-remainder* distinction, and the *lever* idea all at once. If the graphic could be redrawn as one tidy macro donut without losing information, it has failed Part 2.
- **Drawing the four levers as IDENTICAL gauges / equal sliders.** The whole point of 2.0 is that each lever has a *different constraint shape* (hard floor · direction gate · floor-and-ceiling band · free remainder). Four matching dials would say "balance these four equally" — the exact opposite of the article.
- **The diet-cliché kit:** a plate divided into food groups, a food pyramid, a fruit/veg flat-lay, a measuring tape around a waist, protein-shaker / meal-prep-container product shots, a smiling person eating a salad. Says "diet/wellness," teaches nothing.
- **A before/after body transformation,** or making any named diet (keto/IF/carnivore) the *subject*. The fad diets are the jammed bottom-shelf levers drawn smallest on purpose — never co-stars, never villains with their own panel.
- **Red-flooding / a screaming-alarm aesthetic.** The danger here is *quiet and specific* — one cold red zone beneath the protein floor. One scarce saturated red only; a code-red bench breaks both the calm tone and the single-alarm discipline.
- **The wrong-pillar surface:** do not render this as Fit's daylit engineering workbench or the Productive electronics schematic — keep the Healthy after-dark clinical-bench ground established by Sleep, Blueprint, and Nutrition 1.0.
