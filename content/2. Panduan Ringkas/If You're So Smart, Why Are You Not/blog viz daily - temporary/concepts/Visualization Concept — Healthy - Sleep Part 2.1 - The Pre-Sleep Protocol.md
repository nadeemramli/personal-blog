## Series context & the key point _(why this concept, not a generic one)_

Part 1.0 drew the **architecture** of a night (the deep-front / REM-back building). Part 2.0 drew the **room** that defends that architecture passively. Part 2.1 is the **behavioural** half of the setup: the things you actively *do and stop doing* in the hours before bed. And the article hands us its hero metaphor in one line, so on-the-nose that to draw anything else would be malpractice:

> ==Sleep is not a switch. It's a runway you descend, and the descent starts hours before takeoff.==

The whole article corrects one mistake: people treat sleep as a **light switch** (full intensity until 11 PM, then *click*), and then lie there "tired but wired." The fix is to see the last ~4 hours as **the beginning of sleep** — a glide-path your physiology has to fly down: core temperature must fall, cortisol and adrenaline must come down, melatonin must rise, sleep pressure must be high and unspent. None of that happens instantly; it happens across the runway.

The article makes these moves the poster must carry:

1. **The reframe: switch → runway.** This is the single emotional payload. The poster must make a reader who has never thought about it *feel* the difference between slamming a switch and flying a controlled descent.
2. **The master control is your morning, not your night.** Counter-intuitively, tonight's sleep is set by a **fixed wake time (7 days a week) + 10–20 min outdoor light within an hour of waking** — this anchors the clock and times melatonin ~14–16 h later. It is the *launch condition* that makes the evening descent possible.
3. **Two all-day levers that must already be down before the runway even starts:** **caffeine** (~5–6 h half-life; cut ~8–10 h before bed; it degrades deep sleep even when you "sleep fine" — and late pre-workout is the sneaky one) and **training** (great for sleep; only *vigorous* work in the last ~1 h hurts it).
4. **The four-hour runway, as a timeline of descending actions** (for an 11 PM target): **T-4h** finish intense training + last big meal ("sleep starts now"); **T-2h** dim the house, warm/amber light, screens down; **T-90m** warm shower (drops core temp via distal vasodilation); **T-1h** wind-down ritual — brain-dump, reading, long-exhale breathing (4-7-8 / box) to walk RHR down; **T-0** lights out into the cool/dark/quiet room from Part 2.0.
5. **Alcohol is the great deceiver** — a *sedative, not a sleep-inducer*. It fakes a fast, deep descent in the first half, then **suppresses REM and fragments the second half** (the 4 AM wake-up). Sedation is not sleep.
6. **The scoreboard is overnight resting heart rate.** A controlled descent shows up as a low sleeping RHR; an aborted one shows up as elevated RHR + worse recovery. And the quiet thesis: **this behavioural toolkit + Part 2.0 *is* CBT-I**, the first-line insomnia treatment that beats pills — "if your problem is a busy mind, the answer is here, not in a bottle."

**The single thing a reader must walk away owning:** *You don't fall asleep at 11 — you begin landing at 7. Sleep is a glide-path your body flies down over the last four hours: core temp, cortisol, heart rate all stepping lower at each waypoint. Fly the approach (fixed morning, early caffeine cutoff, dim the world, warm shower, walk your heart rate down) and you touch down clean. Slam the switch instead — late coffee, a nightcap, a 10:55 PM email, the phone — and you spend the first hour of "sleep" still in the air.*

**The native geometry is therefore an aviation descent / approach-profile chart** — a glide-path falling from "full daytime altitude" to "touchdown," with waypoints where each protocol action steps the descent down, a launch anchor at the morning that sets the whole approach, deceptive/aborted descent traces for the saboteurs, and an RHR instrument confirming the landing. It is a clean sibling to Part 1.0's hypnogram-building and Part 2.0's room-cutaway (same night palette, same discipline) while being an unmistakably distinct hero object.

## 1. The Big Idea

_One sentence: **Sleep isn't a switch you flip at 11 — it's a four-hour glide-path you fly down, and every pre-bed habit is a waypoint that steps your core temperature, cortisol, and heart rate lower until you touch down.** The poster is a night-sky **descent profile**: a calm, controlled approach line gliding from high daytime arousal to a clean landing, waypoint-by-waypoint; a rejected "light-switch" model shown failing beside it; the morning launch-anchor that sets the timer; the saboteurs drawn as spikes and a deceptive alcohol trace that crashes in the back half; and one red go-around — the phone — that throws you back up into the air. The reader should feel the difference between *slamming a switch* and *flying an approach* before reading a single label._

## 2. Visual Metaphor & Structure — the hero object

**A descent glide-path / approach-to-landing profile, drawn across a night sky.** This is the anchor; nothing replaces it. Think *aviation approach chart meets a sleep-lab trend line, rendered after dark.*

- **The axes (the frame of the whole night).**
  - **Vertical = AROUSAL / ALTITUDE** — and explicitly the stack of things that must fall: a thin gutter label reads `core temp ↓ · cortisol ↓ · heart rate ↓ · (melatonin ↑)`. High = sympathetic, alert, "in the air." Low = parasympathetic, calm, "on the ground / asleep."
  - **Horizontal = the runway timeline** — left to right: `morning` … `1–2 PM` … `T-4h (≈7 PM)` … `T-2h` … `T-90m` … `T-1h` … `T-0 (11 PM · lights out)`. The far right edge is **touchdown**, where the line crosses into the dark "ground" band that is literally the Part 2.0 room (callback).

- **THE GOOD GLIDE-PATH (the hero line).** A single calm, warm descent curve gliding smoothly from daytime altitude down to a clean touchdown at T-0. It does **not** plummet — it *steps down* at each waypoint, each step triggered by one protocol action drawn as a small labeled waypoint marker on the line:
  - **LAUNCH ANCHOR (far left, morning):** a small sunrise/origin glyph — `FIXED WAKE TIME · 10–20 min outdoor light`. A faint dotted "timer" arc runs from here all the way to T-0 with the note `sets melatonin ~14–16 h later`. This is drawn as the thing that *makes the whole approach possible* — the master control. The article's strongest counter-intuition: tonight's landing is set this morning.
  - **CAFFEINE CUTOFF (~1–2 PM):** a waypoint gate on the descent — `last caffeine · ~5–6 h half-life · watch late pre-workout`. Tiny etched note: `it hides tiredness, doesn't remove it`.
  - **T-4h — "SLEEP STARTS NOW":** the most important waypoint, slightly emphasized — `finish intense training · last big meal · the runway opens`. Caption: `the last 4 hours are not "evening" — they're the start of sleep`.
  - **T-2h — DIM THE WORLD:** `house to warm/amber · screens down + dimmed · stop stimulating content` → the descent steps lower (melatonin rises).
  - **T-90m — WARM SHOWER:** a small counter-intuition flag — `warm shower → core temp DROPS` (distal vasodilation). A neat visual beat: the line *dips* right after, the cooling that initiates sleep.
  - **T-1h — WIND-DOWN:** `brain-dump (paper) · reading · 4-7-8 / box breathing` with the sub-note `walk the heart rate down`. This is the steepest, most deliberate part of the glide.
  - **T-0 — TOUCHDOWN:** the line crosses into the dark ground band = the cool/dark/quiet room from Part 2.0, with a tiny callback emblem `the environment takes over → Part 2.0`. Clean landing.

- **THE REJECTED MODEL (the contrast that sells the idea).** Behind/above the glide-path, a faint, greyed **"LIGHT SWITCH" trace**: a flat line held at full daytime altitude all the way to 11 PM, then a vertical cliff straight down — captioned `the switch model · "tired but wired"`. It visibly *fails to land*: where it hits the ground it bounces/overshoots into a jagged "still landing for the first hour" scribble. This is the wrong mental model the article exists to correct, shown losing.

- **THE SABOTEURS (drawn as forces pushing the approach back UP).** Along the runway, three or four small upward-spike vectors that throw you off the glide-path — each labeled, restrained, in the "threat" treatment from Part 2.0:
  - `LATE CAFFEINE` — a spike under the deep-sleep portion: `degrades deep sleep even when you "sleep fine"`.
  - `VIGOROUS LATE TRAINING (last ~1 h)` — a spike at T-1h: `core temp + cortisol + HR ↑`.
  - `BIG / LATE MEAL` — a bump near T-2h: `digestion = heat + reflux`.
  - **ALCOHOL — THE DECEIVER (its own special trace, the emotional twist of the article).** A *separate deceptive descent line*: it drops **faster and deeper than the good glide-path in the first half** (looks like the best landing of all), then in the back half it **breaks violently upward** — REM suppression + fragmentation + the 4 AM wake-up — ending higher and rougher than where it started. Labeled `ALCOHOL — sedation is not sleep · wrecks the second half`. Rendered cool/amber, *not* red (it's a saboteur, not the single reserved alarm).

- **THE PHONE — the one red go-around (the reserved alarm).** At T-0 / in the room, the single signal-red element: the phone on the nightstand, throwing a red "abort the landing" vector that kicks the would-be-clean approach back up into a jagged go-around — `light + engagement = airborne again`. Rule-line, red: `charge it OUTSIDE the room` (shared discipline with Part 2.0; the two posters rhyme on the phone as villain).

- **THE SCOREBOARD (small instrument, corner).** A compact **overnight RHR readout** docked at touchdown — `clean descent → low sleeping RHR ✓` vs `aborted → RHR ↑ + recovery ↓`. This is the article's "scoreboard from Part 1.0," the instrument that tells you whether you actually landed. For the lifter, a one-line tag: `a controlled descent = a bigger deep-sleep GH pulse = a better session tomorrow`.

- **THE CBT-I grace note (docks the thesis, bottom).** A tiny lockup: `THIS + Part 2.0 = CBT-I — first-line for insomnia, beats pills`, with `a racing mind is behavioural, not chemical → the fix is the runway, not a bottle`. This positions the whole article (and sets up Part 3.0 as the "only if this is dialled in" next step).

Why this metaphor fits and nothing else does: the article's literal, repeated thesis is **descent over a runway**, not a switch. A tidy checklist of tips (cut caffeine, dim lights, shower…) would lose the two ideas that *are* the article — that the actions are a **sequenced descent on a timeline**, and that core temp / cortisol / HR are **falling together** toward a landing. Only a glide-path profile is simultaneously a real, actionable timeline (you can read your own evening off it) and the felt argument (sleep is flown, not flipped). It also chains cleanly: Part 1 = the building, Part 2.0 = the wall, Part 2.1 = the approach that lands you safely inside it.

## 3. Layout Description

**Portrait (poster / blog-hero, ~4:5 or 3:4)**, matching the Part 1.0 / 2.0 siblings, with the glide-path running on a gentle diagonal from upper-left (morning / altitude) to lower-right (T-0 / touchdown) so it has both width for the timeline and height for the altitude drop.

- **Top band (~12%):** the headline (see §4), small, kerned wide against the night sky; a thin horizon line beneath. The faint morning launch-anchor sits at the upper-left where the headline meets the chart.
- **Center stage (~64%) — the hero:** the descent profile fills the field. **Reading path:** the eye enters at the **morning launch anchor (upper-left)**, follows the warm glide-path **down and right through the waypoints** (caffeine → T-4h → T-2h → T-90m → T-1h), reads the **rejected switch trace** ghosting above it, snags on the **alcohol deceiver line** breaking upward in the back half, and lands at **touchdown (lower-right)** where the red phone go-around is the single hottest point. Waypoint labels sit on the line like approach-chart annotations — restrained, mono, etched. The saboteur spikes sit just off the line in the threat treatment.
- **Instrument strip (~12%), lower-right at touchdown:** the **overnight RHR scoreboard** (clean vs aborted) docked where the line meets the ground/room band, reading as the landing readout.
- **Bottom band (~12%):** the thesis lockup `YOU DON'T FLIP A SWITCH. YOU FLY AN APPROACH.`, the tiny CBT-I grace-note, and a thin footer: `Part 2 of 5 · Sleep · the Healthy pillar` plus the micro-caption `the behavioural half of the setup · lands you in the Part 2.0 room`.

The composition deliberately mirrors the siblings' vertical logic (headline → hero → instrument/foundation strip → thesis lockup) so all three Sleep posters read as an obvious set hung in a row: *the building → the wall → the approach.*

## 5. Color & Mood

**Mood:** the same **nocturnal, calm, quietly serious** register as Parts 1.0 / 2.0 — planetarium-meets-medical-chart — with this article's specific sub-feeling: **controlled descent.** Where Part 1.0 felt like being shown a blueprint and Part 2.0 like standing in a sealed room during a storm, Part 2.1 should feel like **flying a smooth, deliberate approach at night** — unhurried, instrument-guided, in command of the landing. The drama is entirely in the contrast between the calm good glide-path and the things that throw it off (the switch-cliff, the alcohol break-up, the red phone go-around); the alarm is reserved for the single fixable mistake.

**Palette (dark-first — reuse the siblings exactly):**
- **Background / night sky:** deep near-black indigo / ink (`#0B0F1A`-ish), with altitude reading subtly "busier/colder" up high (daytime arousal) and calmer toward the dark ground band (the Part 2.0 room) at touchdown.
- **Accent 1 — warm amber / ember (`#E8A23D`-ish):** the **good glide-path** itself and its waypoints — the safe, controlled descent. Also the morning launch-anchor (sunrise). Warmth = the approach you want.
- **Accent 2 — cool violet / periwinkle (`#8B7CF0`-ish):** the falling instruments (core-temp / HR trend), the cooled touchdown, the masked/calm ground band, and the Part 2.0 callback emblem — so the rhyme across posters is unmistakable. The alcohol deceiver line also lives in this cool family (a saboteur, but *not* the reserved alarm).
- **Structure / axes / text / ruler:** soft off-white (`#E6E8EE`) and muted slate for secondary labels — never harsh pure white. The rejected "switch" trace is the most desaturated, ghosted grey on the board (it's the loser).
- **One alarm color — the phone go-around only:** the established restrained **signal red / coral** (`#FF5C5C`), used *exclusively* for the phone's abort vector and its `charge it OUTSIDE` rule-line. It must be the single hottest point in the whole image — scarcity gives it force and makes the one fixable mistake jump out. (Shared discipline with Part 2.0, where the phone was also the lone red breach — the two posters intentionally rhyme on this.)

The warm-controlled-descent vs cold-busy-altitude split does double duty: atmospheric *and* it encodes the thesis — a calm flown approach versus a hostile, over-aroused "still in the air" — before a word is read.

## 6. Style Reference

**"Clinical Nocturne"** — inherited, unchanged, from Parts 1.0 / 2.0. A scientific diagram with restraint and atmosphere. Touchstones for this poster specifically:

- The labeled-line discipline of an **aviation approach / descent-profile chart** and a **sleep-lab trend graph** (a clean primary trace, waypoint annotations, axis gutters, surveyor-style callouts) — this is the dominant grammar here, picking up the article's literal "runway / descent / glide-path" language.
- The dark, layered elegance of **editorial science graphics** (*Quanta* / *Scientific American* full-page diagrams; a Wait-But-Why concept rendered for grown-ups, at night).
- The single-alarm-color and instrument-grade-label rigor established by the Fit pillar's "Engineering Diagnostic Instrument" style and carried into Sleep — **same seriousness, rendered after dark.**

It must read as an obvious **sibling to the Part 1.0 hypnogram poster and the Part 2.0 room-cutaway**: identical palette, identical type treatment, identical headline → hero → instrument-strip → thesis vertical structure, identical red-for-warnings-only rule, the same phone-as-lone-red-villain. Hung in a row, a viewer should instantly read *"Part 1 = the building you build in your sleep; Part 2.0 = the wall around it; Part 2.1 = the approach that lands you safely inside."* Parts 3.0–3.1 will continue reusing this night signature.

## 7. What to Avoid

- **The flat tip-checklist / icon-grid** (☕ no coffee, 🚿 shower, 📵 no phone, 🛏️ bed…). This is exactly what the article *isn't*: a grid loses the two ideas worth keeping — that the actions are a **sequenced descent on a timeline**, and that core temp / cortisol / HR are **falling together** toward a landing. If the graphic could be redrawn as a plain feature-grid without losing meaning, it has failed the article.
- **The literal "person sleeping / clock / crescent-moon / floating Zzz's" cliché** (and *never show a sleeper or a face*, consistent with Parts 1.0 / 2.0). A bedside clock counting to 11 is the obvious, generic "bedtime routine" image and it re-tells the *switch* model the article is demolishing.
- **A literal airplane/cockpit illustration.** The metaphor is a **descent profile / glide-path line**, not a drawn aeroplane landing on a tarmac. Keep it an abstract instrument trace (a chart an engineer or a sleep lab would read), not an aviation stock illustration — the moment a 737 appears, it reads as a travel poster, not a sleep diagram.
- **(Bonus to avoid)** Drawing the saboteurs as equal to the protocol, or letting **alcohol** read like the others. Alcohol is the article's special trap — it must be the *deceptive* trace that descends fastest and then breaks the worst, not just another upward spike; flatten that and you lose the "sedation is not sleep" payload. And keep the **switch model** clearly *losing* (ghosted, overshooting the landing) — if it looks like a neutral alternative rather than the corrected mistake, the whole thesis softens.
