## Series context & the key point _(why this concept, not a generic one)_

Part 2.0 sharpened the face. Part 3.0 frames it with **the cut** — and its single, repeated thesis is blunt: ==the right cut is dictated by **your** hair biomechanics and skull geometry, not a magazine, not a Korean idol, not the photo on your phone.== The article's opening failure is precise and universal: a man walks into a barber holding a picture of someone *"whose hair type, growth direction, and skull shape have almost nothing in common with theirs."* The cut on the screen worked on that person because their biomechanics supported it. His don't, and no pomade fixes it.

The fix is a **two-step procedure**, and the poster has to carry both steps in order:

1. **SURVEY your own biology first (measurable, non-negotiable).** Hair *type* (Walker scale → how each strand behaves under gravity, humidity, product). *Growth direction* — the crown **whorl** (94% clockwise), the **cowlicks**, the front growth angle. *Skull from above* — round / elongated / **flat occiput** (the under-served Asian-male case). These are not preferences; *"the patches where the hair refuses to lie are not a styling failure; they are your growth pattern asserting itself."* **Map first, choose second.**
2. **DRAW the spec off the survey (then communicate it without ambiguity).** Face-shape silhouette (explicitly flagged *"convention, not law"*), the **Blueprint Cut** asymmetry counter-balance (add ≤1–2 cm of weight to the *softer* side, keep the stronger side cleaner — *light* correction, never concealment), and finally **the five-line barber script** (type → silhouette → numbers → part/weight-bias → no-go list) that removes the interpretation gap where bad cuts are born.

And one validation, the only KPI in the whole article: **the 7-day mirror test.** Does the cut fall right on day 7 with **no product**, after a normal shower? Yes → it works *with* your biomechanics. Only-with-product → it is *fighting* your biology. *"A cut that only looks right with 10 minutes of styling daily is a cut you will fail to maintain."*

**The single thing a reader must walk away owning:** *Your own head is a site to be surveyed and spec'd — whorls, cowlicks, skull shape, asymmetry are the survey data, and the right cut is read off them, not copied off a phone. The photo of someone else's head is a rejected drawing.*

That is **an engineering deliverable: a survey of a specific object, a spec drawn off that survey, a title-block of instructions, and an inspection stamp** — which is exactly why a *survey / spec drawing* is the only honest anchor. The article literally names it the **Blueprint** Cut. We draw the blueprint it's describing — of the reader's actual head.

## 1. The Big Idea

_One sentence: **Your head is a site survey, and the correct cut is a spec drawn off that survey — your whorl, your cowlicks, your skull shape, your asymmetry — not a photo of someone whose biology you don't share.** The poster should make the reader feel, instantly, that the picture-on-the-phone approach is a **rejected drawing**, and that the real move is to read their own head like an engineer reads a site before specifying the build._

## 2. Visual Metaphor & Structure — the hero object

**A barber's "Blueprint Cut" working drawing of the reader's _own_ head** — rendered the way a mechanical part is drawn: **two orthographic views of the same object** (a top-down PLAN of the scalp = the survey; a front ELEVATION of the face = where the cut is spec'd), wired together with dimension lines and callout leaders, with a **spec title-block** docked bottom-right and an **inspection stamp** overprinted. This is the anchor; nothing replaces it.

The two views are the two steps of the argument, left-to-right / top-to-bottom:

- **VIEW 1 — PLAN (top-down scalp): "THE SITE SURVEY."** A clean circle = the crown seen from above. On it, drawn in **warm terracotta** (= measurable biology):
  - the **whorl** as a spiral with a rotation arrow and the note `94% clockwise`;
  - **cowlick** markers at the hairline / temple / nape, each a small "stand-up" caret with a leader (`won't lie down = biology, not failure`);
  - the **growth-direction field** as light flow-arrows sweeping across the scalp;
  - the **front growth angle** marked at the hairline, dictating where the natural part falls.
  Caption: `SURVEY FIRST — read the head before you cut it.`

- **VIEW 2 — FRONT ELEVATION (the face as geometry, NOT a portrait): "THE SPEC."** The face drawn as a **shape diagram**: the face-shape outline (oval / round / square / oblong / heart / diamond) with a vertical **centre-line**, plus the **asymmetry overlay** — a faint mirror-flip "ghost" offset from the original so the lean is visible. Then the **Blueprint Cut counter-balance**, annotated like a tolerance: a small `+WEIGHT` hatched wedge on the **softer** side, `CLEAN / TIGHT` on the **stronger** side, and a dimension tick reading `≤ 1–2 cm — light counter-balance, NOT concealment`. Guard-number callouts (`guard 1–4`) sit on the sides. The face-shape "what flatters" logic is drawn in **cool slate-blue**, because the article explicitly flags it `convention, not law`.

- **THE SPEC TITLE-BLOCK (bottom-right, like a real drawing's stamp): "THE 5-LINE SCRIPT."** A bordered spec table — the deliverable that turns the survey into something a barber executes, exactly as the article lists it:
  `01 TYPE & GROWTH` · `02 SILHOUETTE (shape, not a celebrity)` · `03 NUMBERS (guards + cm on top)` · `04 PART / WEIGHT BIAS` · `05 DO-NOT (name 1–2)`.

- **THE INSPECTION STAMP (overprinted, circular, like a QA "APPROVED" stamp): "THE ONLY KPI."** `7-DAY MIRROR TEST` around the rim; centre: `falls right · NO product = PASS`. This is the validation the whole drawing is built toward.

- **THE FAILURE OVERLAY (the emotional payload — single restrained red).** Ghosted to one side: a **phone** showing *a different head* (a generic celebrity silhouette), stamped diagonally in signal-red `REJECTED — not your biomechanics`. One line beneath: `it worked on their head. yours is a different drawing.` It must read at a glance as **the wrong source document** — the thing the survey replaces.

**Margin scope-note (small, like a drawing's "scope" box, top-left):** `THIS DRAWING = STYLING (cutting what you have). RETENTION (keeping it) → Hair folder.` This carries the article's clean styling-vs-retention boundary in one gesture without stealing focus.

**Optional materials strip (thin, along one edge):** the Walker types as a **stock-behaviour legend**, not the hero — `1 straight · falls flat, holds a line` / `2 wavy · holds with light product` / `3 curly · stands out, not down` / `4 coily · shrinks when dry`, with the band `MOST MALAYSIAN MEN: 1A–2B` highlighted. Treat it as a materials callout ("STOCK: type 1B"), never as the centrepiece.

Why this metaphor fits and nothing else does: the article's literal moves are *survey* (read whorls / cowlicks / skull), *spec* (draw the cut + counter-balance off the survey), *communicate* (the 5-line script), and *inspect* (the 7-day mirror test) — and it calls the result the **Blueprint** Cut. A two-view survey/spec drawing with a title-block and an inspection stamp is the only object that holds all four steps at once **and** keeps the reader's *own* head as the subject. A row of haircut styles, a groomed-man photo, or the Walker curl-chart would each destroy the one idea that matters: *the cut is read off you, not chosen off a shelf.*

## 3. Layout Description

**Portrait (poster / blog-hero, ~4:5 or 3:4)** to match Part 1.0 as the pillar primary. (A landscape variant works as an in-article banner with the two views side-by-side and the failure phone at far right.)

- **Top band (~12%):** the headline (see §4), small against the paper, kerned wide, thin drafting border beneath it, and a tiny slug `Part 3 of 7 · Appearance · the Attractive pillar`. The **scope-note box** sits at top-left.
- **Center stage (~64%) — the hero:** the **two views stacked or paired** — PLAN (survey) reads first (upper / left), ELEVATION (spec) second (lower / right), joined by a dimension/leader line so the eye travels *survey → spec*. The **spec title-block** anchors the bottom-right corner; the **inspection stamp** overprints near it (slightly rakish, like a real stamp). The **rejected phone** sits ghosted in the opposite negative space (upper-right or left), with its single red stamp.
- **Margin rail (left edge, full height):** a two-tone **epistemic key** docked vertically — `■ warm = YOUR BIOLOGY (non-negotiable)` over `■ cool = CONVENTION (a strong default)` — so the colour-coding is legible before any label is read. This mirrors Part 1.0's mechanism/convention device.
- **Bottom band (~12%):** the thesis lockup `CUT THE HEAD YOU HAVE.` plus a thin footer micro-caption: `survey → spec → script → the 7-day mirror test · the photo on your phone is a different drawing.`

Reading path is deliberate and matches the procedure: **scope-note (what this is) → PLAN/survey (read your biology) → ELEVATION/spec (draw the cut + counter-balance) → title-block (the 5-line script) → inspection stamp (the mirror test) → glance to the rejected phone that proves why you survey first.**

- **Headline (pick one):**

- **View captions:** `VIEW 1 · PLAN — THE SITE SURVEY` / `VIEW 2 · ELEVATION — THE SPEC`.
- **Survey callouts (warm):** `WHORL · 94% clockwise` · `COWLICK · won't lie = biology` · `GROWTH ANGLE → sets the part` · `FLAT OCCIPUT? leave length at the back` (the under-served Asian-male note).
- **Spec callouts:** `FACE SHAPE — a strong default, not a law` (cool) · `+WEIGHT → softer side` / `CLEAN → stronger side` · `≤ 1–2 cm · counter-balance, not concealment` (warm).
- **Title-block (the 5-line script):** `01 TYPE & GROWTH · 02 SILHOUETTE · 03 NUMBERS · 04 PART / BIAS · 05 DO-NOT`.
- **Inspection stamp:** `7-DAY MIRROR TEST · NO product = PASS`.
- **Scope-note (top-left):** `STYLING here · RETENTION → Hair folder`.

- **Failure overlay (single red):** `REJECTED — not your biomechanics`.
- **Thesis lockup (bottom):** `CUT THE HEAD YOU HAVE.` (use only if not already the headline; otherwise `survey first. cut second.`)

Nothing else. No body paragraph, no "tips" list — the survey/spec drawing *is* the argument.

## 5. Color & Mood

**Mood:** the calm, exacting confidence of a drafting table — a working drawing of *your own head*, precise and grown-up, not a glow-up or a lookbook. The feeling of being handed the survey for something you've been guessing at by holding up a phone. The warm-biology / cool-convention split is the personality and does the teaching.

**Palette (light-first — the established Attractive drafting-paper register):**
- **Background:** warm bone / drafting paper (`#F4F1EA`-ish), faint blueprint grid or drafting-dot texture, very low contrast.
- **Ink / linework / type:** graphite near-black (`#1E2024`) for all structure, views, dimension lines, labels — the discipline of a real survey drawing.
- **Accent 1 — YOUR BIOLOGY (measurable, non-negotiable):** structural **terracotta / ochre** (`#C2703D`-ish) for everything *surveyed* — whorl, cowlicks, growth arrows, the asymmetry weight-bias, the dimension ticks. Warm = real, load-bearing, dictated by you.
- **Accent 2 — CONVENTION (a strong default):** muted **slate-blue / pewter** (`#5E7585`-ish) for the face-shape "what flatters" logic only — the part the article explicitly calls *convention, not law.* Cooler, lighter, "taste, not biology."
- **One alarm colour — the rejected phone only:** restrained **signal-red / vermilion** (`#D9483B`), used *exclusively* for the `REJECTED` stamp on the someone-else's-head photo. Scarcity is the force.
- The **inspection stamp** reads in graphite (or a faded version of the terracotta), like a genuine rubber stamp — never the alarm red (a pass is not a warning).

The warm-below-the-skin / cool-for-taste coding ties Part 3.0 directly to Part 1.0's mechanism→convention line, so a reader who saw the first poster already knows how to read this one before reading a word.

## 6. Style Reference

**"Architect's Elevation / Spec Sheet" (Attractive-pillar locked style) — the SURVEY-DRAWING variant.** Touchstones:

- The honesty and labeled-dimension discipline of a **real mechanical / architectural working drawing** — orthographic multi-views of one object, dimension lines, callout leaders, hatching for "material," a bordered **title-block** in the corner, and a **rubber inspection stamp**. This is what keeps it credible rather than decorative, and it is exactly the article's own logic (survey → spec → inspect).
- The cross-section + annotation calm of **editorial explainer graphics** (Monocle / Kinfolk-grade typographic restraint; a Wait-But-Why concept *drafted by a surveyor*, not sketched by hand).
- The **two-register colour surface** carried over from Part 1.0: warm for measured biology, cool for convention — the single most distinctive, un-generic move.

It should sit as the **sibling to Appearance Part 1.0** within the same pillar family: **1.0 = the elevation (the appearance stack, built bottom-up); 3.0 = the survey/spec drawing (the head, read then spec'd).** Same bone-paper palette, same warm/cool epistemic coding, same single-alarm-red discipline, different drawing type. This confirms the Attractive signature for Parts 4.0–7.0.

## 7. What to Avoid

- **The men's-grooming cliché: a groomed-man portrait, a before/after split, a fan of trendy haircut styles, or a photogenic face with arrows pointing at "good hair."** It says "barbershop flyer" and teaches nothing. The subject is the *reader's own head as a drawing*, never an aspirational stranger. (A real face/photo may appear ONLY as the small, rejected phone in the failure overlay.)
- **The Andre Walker curl-type chart as the hero.** That swatch grid is the single most over-used hair infographic on the internet, and it's a *lookup table*, not the argument. Demote it to a thin materials-legend strip (with the Malaysian 1A–2B band highlighted) or cut it; the survey/spec drawing must lead.
- **(Bonus to avoid) Making "skull geometry" read as phrenology** — a head carved into labeled zones with mystical-sounding regions. Keep it as engineering survey (dimension lines, growth-direction arrows, an honest "convention, not law" tag on the face-shape part), never a pseudoscience head-map. And don't render a generic decorative "blueprint look" with no real reader-specific annotation — an un-annotated blueprint is just wallpaper and kills the entire point that *this is YOUR head, surveyed.*
