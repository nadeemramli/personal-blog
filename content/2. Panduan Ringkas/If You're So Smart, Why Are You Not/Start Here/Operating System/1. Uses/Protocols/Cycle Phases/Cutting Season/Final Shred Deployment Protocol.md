# Final Shred Deployment Protocol

> [!abstract] Owns the rare terminal cut.
> This is only for an already-lean state after a productive bulk or long cut. It is not the protocol for digging out of high body fat.

> [!danger] Boundary
> Highest monitoring burden. Stimulants, thyroid, beta-agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors, diuretics, and peptides can create dangerous overlap. This note blocks deployment unless the safety layer is already written.

## Deployment Clock

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Already lean, photos/waist confirm it, and the goal is a short sharpness push. |
| Duration logic | Short terminal block, not an open-ended lifestyle. |
| Deployment logic | Tier 2 to Tier 3 only when earned. Highest-risk tools are layered only after the base cut is stable. |
| Stock math | Rare pulse demand; do not buy full-year supply for final-shred tools. |

## Entry Criteria

All must be true:

- Already lean enough that a terminal push makes visual sense.
- The long cut or bulk has not left uncontrolled BP/HR, sleep, lipids, renal/hepatic markers, or glucose risk.
- Calories, protein, steps/cardio, training volume, electrolytes, and sleep are already stable.
- No acute illness, dehydration, injury flare, or major life stress window.
- The exact stop date and exit plan are written before escalation.

## Hard Blocks

Do not run final shred if:

- Body fat is still high enough that a normal cut would work.
- BP/HR is uncontrolled or resting HR is already elevated.
- Sleep is poor before stimulants are added.
- SGLT2, diuretic, sauna, low-carb, dehydration, and heavy sweating are stacking.
- The plan relies on "dryness" rather than measured fat loss and performance retention.

## Escalation Rules

1. Keep the base cut stable first.
2. Add only one high-risk stressor at a time.
3. If a tool worsens sleep, BP/HR, anxiety, hydration, or training quality, it loses.
4. Do not combine multiple adrenergic tools casually; stimulation load is cumulative.
5. Do not use thyroid as a substitute for adherence.
6. Peak-week or cosmetic water manipulation requires clinician oversight and current electrolytes.

## Exit Criteria

Exit when the date arrives, the visual target is reached, performance drops sharply, sleep breaks, BP/HR drifts, dehydration appears, or any red flag appears. The default after final shred is recovery, not another escalation.

## Monitoring

- Daily: BP/HR, bodyweight, sleep, temperature/heat tolerance, anxiety, hydration, cramps, training readiness.
- Several times weekly: photos, waist, performance trend, electrolyte/hydration review.
- Lab dependency: lipids, CMP/electrolytes, renal markers, glucose/ketone awareness if SGLT2 is anywhere in the system.
- Cross-check: [[Blood Pressure & Fluid Escalation Protocol]], [[Glucose & Insulin Sensitivity Protocol]], [[Renal & Hepatic Support Protocol]], [[Lipid Management Protocol]].

## Red Flags

Stop and seek review for chest pain, fainting, severe palpitations, confusion, heat illness symptoms, severe tremor, severe insomnia/anxiety, ketone symptoms, dehydration, severe cramps, shortness of breath, or neurological symptoms.

## Stock Math

- Final-shred inventory is pulse inventory: `active_days x units`, not year-round.
- Keep thyroid/stimulant/beta-agonist stock separated from base cut stock so it is not normalized.
- Track electrolyte, glycerol, protein, and intra-workout support as part of the block.
- Do not restock final-shred products unless there is a dated deployment plan.
