# Electrolyte Protocol

> [!abstract] Electrolytes are handled as a **daily floor + situational top-up**, not as a single fixed megadose.
> Protein is mostly a daily target. Electrolytes are partly daily targets, partly weather/training/drug-state variables. Sodium, water, and potassium change with sweat, carbs, fasting, SGLT2 use, diuretics, BP drugs, and GI losses. Magnesium is PM daily in this OS.

> [!danger] Boundary
> This is harm-reduction infrastructure, not a prescription. Any abnormal sodium, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, edema, severe BP drift, palpitations, fainting, vomiting/diarrhea, or SGLT2/diuretic overlap routes to [[Blood Pressure & Fluid Escalation Protocol]], [[Glucose & Insulin Sensitivity Protocol]], and [[Renal & Hepatic Support Protocol]].

## Current OS Assumptions

- Bodyweight context: ~90 kg cut context.
- Training rhythm: [[Training Rhythm]].
- Current cut hydration rule: [[Fat Loss Daily Schedule (Cut)]] requires **4-5 L water/day** when SGLT2 + stimulants + cardio are active.
- Magnesium rule: [[Magnesium Glycinate (210mg×240tab) — iHerb]] is **210 mg elemental magnesium PM daily**.
- Potassium caution: ARB lane is active via [[Irbesartan (150mg×120tab) — shopee-china]]. Use potassium-rich foods first; avoid high-dose potassium salt substitutes unless serum potassium and kidney markers are known safe.

## Daily Floor

| Electrolyte | OS floor | How to cover it |
|---|---|---|
| Water | Stable intake across the day; cut/SGLT2 days target 4-5 L | Spread across windows; avoid huge plain-water boluses. |
| Sodium / chloride | Consistent salted meals; add only as needed by sweat, fasting, pumps, BP, and edema | Iodized salt with meals is preferred because it also covers iodine. |
| Potassium | Primarily food-based | Potatoes, beans/lentils, yogurt/milk, fruit, vegetables, meat/fish. Do not rely on potassium pills. |
| Magnesium | 210 mg PM daily from Nutricost Magnesium Glycinate | Do not stack extra magnesium casually; GI tolerance is the ceiling. |
| Calcium | Food-first until diet log is built | Dairy/fortified foods/fish with bones/greens; avoid calcium near levothyroxine. |

## Baseline Cookie-Cutter Mix

This is the minimum "do not be obviously under-electrolyted" mix. It is deliberately modest because food already supplies sodium/potassium, and the OS already uses magnesium.

| Day type | Mix |
|---|---|
| Rest / low-sweat day | No automatic electrolyte drink. Salt meals normally; PM magnesium. |
| Normal training day | [[Intra-Workout (651g) — iHerb]] during training + normal salted meals. If sweat is clear/heavy, add 1/8-1/4 tsp iodized salt in 750-1000 ml water. |
| Cut + fasted cardio + SGLT2 day | 4-5 L total water/day; include at least one sodium-containing bottle, not plain water only. Keep potassium food-based unless labs say otherwise. |
| Heavy sweat / heat / long session | Intra-workout plus 1/4 tsp iodized salt per extra liter of training fluid, then reassess BP, edema, thirst, and bodyweight. |

Do not chase potassium powder as the default. Potassium is essential, but the safer OS move is **food potassium + labs**, especially with ARB use.

## Short-Term Bolus Rules

- Sodium bolus: can improve plasma volume, pumps, and thirst when sweat losses are real. Too much at once can raise BP, worsen edema, cause GI upset, and add temporary scale weight.
- Potassium bolus: do **not** use high-dose potassium salts casually. Too much potassium is an arrhythmia risk, especially with kidney impairment or potassium-retaining medication context.
- Magnesium bolus: best kept PM. High supplemental magnesium commonly causes diarrhea/cramping; the OS dose is already a full label serving.
- Calcium bolus: not a workout electrolyte priority. Avoid taking calcium within 4 hours of levothyroxine.
- Plain water bolus without sodium: can worsen dilutional symptoms when sweating heavily or when water intake is already high.

## Monitoring

- Daily during cuts: bodyweight, thirst, urine color, BP/HR, edema, cramps, pumps, dizziness, headache, training output.
- Labs on cadence: CMP/electrolytes, creatinine/eGFR, cystatin C if available.
- Red flags: palpitations, fainting, confusion, severe weakness, severe cramps, vomiting/diarrhea, low urine output, chest pain, severe headache, or abnormal sodium/potassium on labs.
