# Recovery & Repair Peptide Protocol

> [!abstract] Owns recovery and repair peptide routing plus the collagen substrate layer.
> Product owners include [[BPC-157 (100mg-box) — TCI]], [[TB-500 (Thymosin Beta-4) (10mg-vial ×10) — TCI]], [[GHK-Cu (1000mg-box) — TCI]], [[KPV (100mg-box) — TCI]], [[Thymosin Alpha-1 (10mg×10vial) — TCI]], and [[SS-31 (100mg-box) — TCI]].

> [!danger] Boundary
> Many peptide products in this stack are research/compounded/nonstandard supply items. This protocol is for ownership, logging, hygiene, and risk gates. It is not a claim of approved medical treatment.

## What This Protocol Owns

- Deciding whether a peptide is being used for acute injury, tendon/joint recovery, skin healing, immune support, gut/inflammation support, or mitochondrial support.
- Ensuring the collagen substrate layer is present: total protein, collagen-specific amino acids, vitamin C, copper/zinc sanity, calories, and training-load control.
- Separating recovery peptides from GH/partitioning peptides in [[Bulk Recovery & Partitioning Protocol]].
- Injection hygiene and reconstitution handoff to [[Injection Hygiene & Consumables Protocol]].
- INDEXA-funded peptide inventory versus personal maintenance inventory.

## Entry Criteria

Use this protocol when:

- There is a specific injury, tendon/joint issue, skin healing goal, inflammatory flare, immune-support goal, or recovery bottleneck.
- A peptide is being moved from Wishlist to Stock and needs a use-case before purchase.
- A vial is being opened, reconstituted, transferred, or counted in stock.
- A peptide overlaps with an Attractive-series protocol and needs one owner for the decision.

## Decision Framework

1. **Name the target tissue/problem.** No target, no peptide. "General recovery" is too vague for injection-based tools.
2. **Confirm the basics are handled.** Sleep, calories, protein, deload, rehab loading, hydration, and injury diagnosis come before peptide escalation.
3. **Pick one lane.** Tissue repair, skin/hair, immune/inflammation, and mitochondrial support are different use-cases. Do not stack all peptides because they are in stock.
4. **Use a cycle note for deployment.** This protocol owns criteria; the active cycle/recovery note owns the actual calendar.
5. **Document response.** Pain/function, range of motion, training load, skin changes, sleep, injection-site reactions, and adverse effects must be logged.

## Collagen Substrate Layer

GHK-Cu should be treated as a remodeling signal, not the building material itself. For collagen-heavy goals, the protocol only makes sense when the substrate layer is already in place:

- **Protein floor:** total daily protein must be high enough to support repair. If protein is low, peptide signaling has less raw material to work with.
- **Collagen-specific amino acids:** collagen is glycine/proline/hydroxyproline heavy, so gelatin/collagen peptides or glycine-rich food can be useful as a targeted add-on when tendon, skin, scar, or connective-tissue repair is the goal.
- **Vitamin C:** required for collagen hydroxylation and cross-linking; pair collagen-focused nutrition with [[Vitamin C (1000mg×60tab) — iHerb]] or vitamin-C-rich food rather than treating collagen intake alone as complete.
- **Copper/zinc balance:** [[GHK-Cu (1000mg-box) — TCI]] is copper-bound, and copper is involved in connective-tissue enzymes, but more copper is not automatically better. Cross-check [[Copper (Bisglycinate) (2mg×100tab) — iHerb]] and [[Zinc (50mg×120tab) — iHerb]] so the system does not quietly create an imbalance.
- **Calories and load management:** a deep deficit, poor sleep, and continuing to overload the injured tissue can erase most of the theoretical repair advantage.

Practical rule: no collagen-repair block is considered "active" until protein, vitamin C, sleep, and training-load management are written in the deployment note.

## GHK-Cu Placement

[[GHK-Cu (1000mg-box) — TCI]] belongs in the skin/hair/wound-remodeling lane first, and the broader connective-tissue lane second. The strongest rationale is collagen/extracellular-matrix remodeling, wound signaling, antioxidant tone, and skin-quality support. It should not be framed as a primary anabolic recovery compound.

## TB-500 Placement

[[TB-500 (Thymosin Beta-4) (10mg-vial ×10) — TCI]] belongs here with [[BPC-157 (100mg-box) — TCI]] as the tissue-repair pair. It should not remain protocol-less because it is not a generic peptide inventory item; it needs an injury/recovery indication, calendar, and stock burn projection.

## Monitoring

- **Before start:** target problem, baseline pain/function score, photos if skin-related, ROM/performance marker if injury-related.
- **During use:** injection-site reaction, water retention, fatigue, unusual inflammation, sleep change, pain/function trend.
- **Nutrition check:** protein target hit rate, collagen/glycine add-on if used, vitamin C consistency, calories, and bodyweight trend.
- **After block:** did the target improve enough to justify another block, or should the product return to reserve?
- **Hygiene:** vial dates, reconstitution date, storage, syringe count, swab count, and disposal.

## Red Flags

Stop and review for fever, spreading redness/warmth, abscess signs, allergic reaction, unexplained swelling, worsening pain, neurological symptoms, shortness of breath, or any systemic illness after injection.

## Stock Math

- Track by **total mg available**, **mg per vial**, **opened vial date**, and **planned block length**.
- Track nutrition support as part of the block: protein powder/food supply, collagen/glycine if used, and vitamin C stock.
- Separate INDEXA-funded stock from personal stock.
- Count consumables with peptide stock: insulin syringes, alcohol swabs, bacteriostatic water if tracked, sharps disposal, and labels.
- Do not buy additional peptide boxes until the target use-case and deployment calendar are written.
