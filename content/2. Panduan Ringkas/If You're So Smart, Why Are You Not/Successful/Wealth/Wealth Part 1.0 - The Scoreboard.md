Most of this project teaches you how to build specific things: a body, an income, a learning system, a financial coordinate. This series zooms all the way out and asks a different question. When you are deciding what to do with a life, what exactly are you keeping score of? The honest answer for most people is one number (money), and that single-metric scoreboard is the quiet reason a lot of objectively successful people feel like they are losing. This article installs a better scoreboard. The credit for it goes to Sahil Bloom; what this series does with it is ours.

## The old scoreboard has one number

The default scoreboard is financial. Hit a salary, hit a net worth, hit a FIRE number. It is seductive because it is legible: one axis, easy to compare, easy to track. But a scoreboard with one number has a fatal property. It tells you to optimise that number even when doing so quietly bankrupts everything the number was supposed to buy.

You already know people this describes. More money than you, less free, because the fixed costs and the chase ate the life. The problem was never the amount. ==Measure one thing and you will optimise that one thing, including all the way off a cliff.== The fix is not to care less about money. The fix is a scoreboard with more than one number on it.

## The five types of wealth

There are five, and money is only the last of them.

- **Time Wealth.** Freedom over how you spend your time, with whom, where, and whether to trade it at all. Built on the awareness that time is your one genuinely finite, non-renewable asset.
- **Social Wealth.** The depth and breadth of your connections, plus the respect you have earned. Who would actually show up for you. (This is the [[Stronghold Part 1.0 - The Stronghold|Stronghold]].)
- **Mental Wealth.** Connection to purpose, a growth posture toward your own mind, and the rituals that keep you clear instead of frayed.
- **Physical Wealth.** Health, fitness, vitality. The most entropic wealth (most exposed to decay and luck), won on the controllables: movement, nutrition, recovery.
- **Financial Wealth.** Assets minus liabilities, with one nuance the rest of this series turns on: your liabilities include your *expectations*, your definition of enough.

Each is genuinely important on its own. But the real game is what you do once you can see all five at once: you score them together in [[Wealth Part 1.2 - The Five Wealths|Part 1.2]], and then you use [[Wealth Part 2.0 - Financial Coordination|Part 2.0]] to define the financial "enough" that frees capacity for the rest. A scoreboard is only useful if you can read every number on it.

## Three things a better scoreboard does

Why bother with five numbers instead of one? Because the five-number board does three things the single number cannot.

**It measures the right things.** Your point-in-time picture now includes every pillar of a life that actually feels full, not just the bank balance. Measure for the war, not just the battle in front of you.

**It changes how you decide.** Every decision can now be read across five axes instead of one. A move that looks terrible on the old board (a pay cut, a slower career, a year off) can be an obvious win on the new one, because of what it does for Time, Social, or Mental wealth. The daunting decision becomes the exciting one once you stop reading only the money axis.

**It lets you design over time.** Your priorities shift across decades. A five-axis board lets you deliberately push one wealth in one season without pretending the others stopped mattering. You can lose individual battles on purpose and still win the war.

## The five questions that cut through

Each wealth has a question that strips away the abstraction. Sit with these; they are the emotional spine of the whole series.

| Wealth | The question |
|---|---|
| **Time** | How many moments do you have left with the people you love? |
| **Social** | Who will be sitting in the front row at your funeral? |
| **Mental** | What would your ten-year-old self say to you today? |
| **Physical** | Will you die having fulfilled your body's potential? |
| **Financial** | What is your definition of enough? |

Notice none of them can be answered with a number you would brag about. That is the point.

## Get your baseline

Before the rest of the series, establish where you stand, the same way you would once have checked your net worth. The quiz is five statements per wealth, scored **0** (strongly disagree) to **4** (strongly agree), for a maximum of 20 per wealth and 100 overall. [[Wealth Part 1.2 - The Five Wealths|Part 1.2]] collects all five quizzes in one place, so you build the full score in a single pass there.

> [!tip] The practical play
> Don't aim for 100. A perfect score across all five at once is neither possible nor the goal. Aim to know your *lowest* wealth, because that is almost always where the next unit of effort buys the most life. The board is a diagnostic, not a trophy.

Write down a guess at all five now (out of 20 each), then refine each as you read its article. The gap between your guess and your scored result is itself information.

## Part 1.0 Takeaways

> [!check] Key concepts to internalize
>
> - **One number is the wrong scoreboard:** Optimising money alone will quietly bankrupt the things money was meant to buy.
> - **Five types of wealth:** Time, Social, Mental, Physical, Financial. Money is the last, not the first.
> - **The board changes decisions:** A move that looks bad on the money axis can be an obvious win across the other four.
> - **Proactive design:** Five axes let you push one wealth per season on purpose without losing the others.
> - **The five questions:** Each wealth has a question that no impressive number can answer.
> - **Find your lowest:** The board is a diagnostic. Your weakest wealth is usually where effort buys the most life.

## Your Baseline Task List

Install the scoreboard before you read further.

1. **Guess all five** — write a rough score out of 20 for Time, Social, Mental, Physical, Financial right now.
2. **Sit with the five questions** — answer each honestly in a sentence, especially the one you want to avoid.
3. **Flag your lowest** — circle the wealth you scored worst. That is your priority wealth.
4. **Set a re-score date** — 90 days out, to retake all five and read the deltas (this becomes the cadence in [[Wealth Part 2.0 - Financial Coordination|Part 2.0]]).
5. **Read on** — the five wealths are scored and routed together in [[Wealth Part 1.2 - The Five Wealths|Part 1.2]].
