Part 1.0 installed the scoreboard. Before you score yourself against it, it is worth being precise about what this series is actually for, because the scoreboard itself is borrowed and there is no point restating a book you could just read. The value here is in the gap the book leaves open: the gap between being told what to measure and knowing exactly what to do on a Tuesday. This article is the thesis for the whole series.

## The gap between knowing and doing

Bloom's book is excellent at the thing it sets out to do: hand you a scoreboard, a quiz, and a set of questions that reframe what a rich life is. That is *diagnosis and aspiration*. It tells you where you are and where the destination is.

What it deliberately stays light on is the daily operation, and it has to, because it is written for everyone. A guideline cannot tell a 26-year-old in Kuala Lumpur and a 50-year-old in Ohio the same specific thing about how to actually raise their Physical score, so it stays at the level of principle. That generality is a feature of the book and a hole for the reader standing there asking *okay, but what do I do*. ==The gap between "you should build this" and "here is exactly how I run it" is the gap this whole project lives in.==

## A guideline builds, a manual executes

Two different jobs, and it is worth naming them cleanly.

- **Building** is the book's job. The scoreboard, the principles, the questions. It points at the war and tells you which fronts matter.
- **Executing** is this project's job. Specific, opinionated, mechanism-first, and Malaysian. It does not tell you that Physical Wealth matters; it hands you a blood panel, a training block, a supplement stack with RM prices. It does not tell you to value your time; it hands you the buffer math.

The book names the war. The blog wins the individual battles. Neither replaces the other, and the reason to read them together is that aspiration without execution is just a nicer kind of anxiety, while execution without a scoreboard is just busyness with good form.

## This series is a hub, not a re-teach

So this series does not re-explain the five wealths in more words. It is a **hub**. It adopts the scoreboard (credited), then routes each wealth into the part of this project that already builds it. The whole architecture is one of recognition: the sections of this blog turn out to *be* the five wealths, which is why the routing is clean.

| Wealth | Where the execution already lives |
|---|---|
| **Time** | the Peaceful / Living material (buffers, baseline, only-live-once) |
| **Social** | the [[Stronghold Part 1.0 - The Stronghold|Stronghold]] series |
| **Mental** | Cognitive Enhancement, Behavioral Change, Learning |
| **Physical** | the Fit, Healthy, and Attractive sections |
| **Financial** | the Rich section + [[Financial Freedom Is a Coordinate, Not a Number|the Coordinate framework]] |

All five of these briefs live together in [[Wealth Part 1.2 - The Five Wealths|Part 1.2]]: each one defines the wealth, gives its question, lets you self-locate with the quiz, and hands you to the manual, plus the one thing the book leaves out for that wealth.

## What the hub adds, and where the writing sits

If you read [[Wealth Part 1.2 - The Five Wealths|Part 1.2]] expecting deep treatment of each wealth, you will think it is thin. It is meant to be. The depth is one click away, in the execution series that has always done the heavy lifting. The Five Wealths article is a switchboard, not a textbook.

The real original writing in this series sits at the two ends: this thesis, and the [[Wealth Part 2.0 - Financial Coordination|Financial Coordination]] capstone. That capstone is the part Bloom genuinely under-specifies: how you actually *define enough*. It is not a generic "balance your life" idea. It is the specific financial coordinate (what you protect, and for how long) that locates enough and, by capping Financial Wealth, frees the capacity the other four wealths need.

## Part 1.1 Takeaways

> [!check] Key concepts to internalize
>
> - **The gap:** The book tells you what to measure; it stays light on what to do. That gap is what this series fills.
> - **Build vs execute:** A guideline builds (principles, scoreboard); a manual executes (specific, mechanism-first protocols).
> - **This series is a hub:** It adopts the scoreboard and routes each wealth into the execution content that already builds it.
> - **The sections are the wealths:** This blog's structure already maps onto the five, which is why the routing is clean.
> - **The briefs are short on purpose:** The depth lives in the linked series; the original work is this thesis and the Financial Coordination capstone (defining enough).
