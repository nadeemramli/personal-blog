## How to use this article

Each wealth below follows the same shape: a one-paragraph definition, the question that cuts through the abstraction, the quiz (five statements, **0** strongly disagree to **4** strongly agree, max 20), the single thing this project adds that the scoreboard only gestures at, and where the execution lives. The wealths are ordered the way the scoreboard is built: Time first (the master resource), Financial last (the means, not the end). Score as you go and keep a running total out of 100.

## Time Wealth

Freedom over your time: how you spend it, with whom, where, and whether you trade it at all. Built on the awareness that time is finite and non-renewable, it shows up as the ability to direct attention to what matters and to set the terms on which you say yes or no. A life with no Time Wealth is the busyness loop: running faster every year, never arriving.

**The question:** *How many moments do you have left with the people you love?* It converts an abstraction into a countable, shrinking number, and that is what turns the calendar from something you react to into something you defend.

**Score it (max 20):**

1. I have a deep awareness of the finite, impermanent nature of my time and its importance as my most precious asset.
2. I have a clear understanding of the two to three most important priorities in my personal and professional lives.
3. I am able to consistently direct attention and focus to the important priorities I have identified.
4. I rarely feel too busy or scattered to spend time on the most important priorities.
5. I am in control of my calendar and priorities.

**What the book leaves out:** Time is *bought* with money, but only up to the point where your expectations stop rising. You buy back hours through buffers and systems, and a bigger life eats them straight back if your definition of enough keeps climbing. Runway is just pre-purchased time, which is why this wealth meets Financial Wealth at the [[Wealth Part 2.2 - Financial Coordination|coordinate of enough]].

**Where the execution lives:** the Peaceful / Living material. Start with [[Baseline - buffers - freedom|Baseline, buffers, freedom]], then [[Min-maxxing|Min-maxxing]] for where to spend effort and [[Only Live Once|Only Live Once]] for what the freed time is for.

## Social Wealth

The depth and breadth of your connections plus the respect you have earned. It splits into three: a few deep bonds (depth), a wider circle of loose ties and belonging to something bigger (breadth), and the lasting respect of your peers earned through who you are rather than what you bought (esteem). A life with no Social Wealth chases acquired status and lacks the weighty relationships that hold under load.

**The question:** *Who will be sitting in the front row at your funeral?* The front row cannot be bought or faked, which makes it the most honest social metric there is.

**Score it (max 20):**

1. I have a core set of deep, loving, supportive relationships.
2. I am consistently able to be the partner, parent, family member, and friend that I would want to have.
3. I have a network of loose relationships I can learn from and build on.
4. I have a deep feeling of connection to a community (local, regional, national, spiritual, and so on) or to something bigger than myself.
5. I do not attempt to achieve status, respect, or admiration through material purchases.

(Statements 1–2 are depth, 3–4 are breadth, 5 is esteem.)

**What the book leaves out:** the *radius rule*. You run different economies at different distances, and the closer the ring, the less you keep score. Most relationship pain is importing the wrong economy into the wrong ring.

**Where the execution lives:** the full [[Stronghold Part 1.0 - The Stronghold|Stronghold]] series. Hub at [[Stronghold Part 1.0 - The Stronghold|Part 1.0]], then [[Stronghold Part 2.0 - Depth|Depth]], [[Stronghold Part 3.0 - Breadth|Breadth]], and [[Stronghold Part 4.0 - Esteem|Esteem]].

## Mental Wealth

Your connection to purpose, your posture toward your own growth, and the rituals that keep your mind clear. Grounded in curiosity, a belief that you can continuously change, and space to think and recharge. A life with no Mental Wealth is stasis: self-limiting beliefs, low-purpose activity, and a low hum of chronic stress.

**The question:** *What would your ten-year-old self say to you today?* A ten-year-old has no interest in your salary; they would ask whether you are still curious, brave, and worth looking up to. A purity test for purpose.

**Score it (max 20):**

1. I regularly embrace a childlike curiosity.
2. I have a clear purpose that provides daily meaning and aligns short- and long-term decision making.
3. I pursue growth and consistently chase my full potential.
4. I have a fundamental belief that I am able to continuously change, develop, and adapt.
5. I have regular rituals that allow me to create space to think, reset, wrestle with questions, and recharge.

**What the book leaves out:** a "growth mindset" with no protocol is a slogan. Belief that you can change is built from evidence (small votes cast through action), not affirmation. And the mind has a hardware layer (focus, memory) and a software layer (how you acquire skill), both trainable on protocols.

**Where the execution lives:** three bodies of work. Purpose and identity in [[Part 1.0 - The Pursuit of Greatness|Behavioral Change]]; skill acquisition in [[Part 1.0 - How Learning Actually Happens|Learning]]; cognitive hardware in the Cognitive Enhancement series (Productive section).

## Physical Wealth

Your health, fitness, and vitality. The most *entropic* wealth (the most exposed to decay and luck), which is why it is won on the controllables: movement, nutrition, recovery, repeated until they hold. A life with no Physical Wealth surrenders to the decline that robs the back half of life of its enjoyment.

**The question:** *Will you die having fulfilled your body's potential?* It reframes health from "avoiding illness" to "fulfilling a potential," a far more demanding standard.

**Score it (max 20):**

1. I feel strong, healthy, and vital for my age.
2. I move my body regularly through a structured routine and have an active lifestyle.
3. I eat primarily whole, unprocessed foods.
4. I sleep seven or more hours per night on a regular basis and feel rested and recovered.
5. I have a clear plan in place to allow me to physically thrive into my later years.

**What the book leaves out:** measurement and the natural-versus-enhanced split. Because this wealth is entropic, you instrument it (biomarkers, body composition, sleep data) rather than manage it on feel, and the right protocol flips depending on whether you are a natural or enhanced trainee.

**Where the execution lives:** three sections. Training and strength in Fit; the biology and longevity dashboard in Healthy (the Blueprint series); the signal layer (skin, hair, appearance) in Attractive.

## Financial Wealth

Assets minus liabilities, with one nuance the next article turns on: your liabilities include your *expectations*, your definition of enough. Built on growing income, keeping expenses below it, and compounding the difference. A life with no Financial Wealth is the treadmill: inflow matching outflow forever.

**The question:** *What is your definition of enough?* The most important question on the board, because it is the one that makes the others reachable. Without a defined enough, every gain raises the bar and freedom recedes as fast as you approach it.

**Score it (max 20):**

1. I have a clear definition of what it means to have enough financially.
2. I have income that is steadily growing alongside my skills and expertise.
3. I manage my monthly expenses so that they are reliably below my income.
4. I have a clear process for investing excess monthly income for long-term compounding.
5. I use my financial wealth as a tool to build other types of wealth.

**What the book leaves out:** how to actually *find* enough. That is financial coordination, and it is important enough to get its own article. The whole of [[Wealth Part 2.2 - Financial Coordination|Part 2.2]] is about it.

**Where the execution lives:** the Rich section, as a chain (generate income with Income 101, aim it with Capabilities and Competency, compound it with Multiplying Money 101), plus [[Wealth Part 2.0 - Financial Freedom Is a Coordinate|the Coordinate framework]] and [[Wealth Part 2.1 - The Beauty of Compounding|the Beauty of Compounding]]. Start with [[Part 1.0 - What Income Actually Is|Income 101 Part 1.0]].

## Tally your 100

Add the five scores. The total out of 100 is interesting, but the useful number is your **lowest** wealth, because that is almost always where the next unit of effort buys the most life.

> [!tip] The practical play
> Don't chase a balanced 100. A perfect, even score across all five at once is neither possible nor the goal. Find your lowest, follow its door, and re-score in 90 days. The board is a diagnostic, not a trophy.

One wealth, though, sits underneath the others, because left undefined it quietly consumes the capacity the rest need. That is Financial Wealth, and the tool that caps it is the subject of the final article.

## Part 1.2 Takeaways

> [!check] Key concepts to internalize
>
> - **Five wealths, one switchboard:** Each has a definition, a question, a quiz, and a door into the section that builds it.
> - **Order matters:** Time first (master resource), Financial last (the means). The ordering is the argument.
> - **Each has a mechanism the book skips:** buffers-and-enough (Time), the radius rule (Social), mindset-needs-a-protocol (Mental), measurement (Physical), how-to-find-enough (Financial).
> - **Score across all five:** Build your total out of 100 in one pass, here.
> - **Find your lowest:** That is your priority door, and where effort pays back most.
> - **Financial sits underneath:** Undefined, it eats the others, which is why the last article is about defining enough.

## Your Five-Wealth Task List

1. **Score all five** — run every quiz above and write a total out of 100.
2. **Circle your lowest** — the wealth that scored worst is your priority spoke.
3. **Open one door** — follow the execution link for that wealth and skim its map.
4. **Note your Financial "enough" gap** — if statement 1 of Financial Wealth scored low, that is the thread Part 2.0 picks up.
5. **Set a 90-day re-score** — the cadence you'll formalise in Part 2.0.
