> [!danger] Framing
> This article covers the **performance and aesthetic** compounds (fat-burners, the GLP and amylin agents, thyroid, the metabolic and mitochondrial tools, the fullness stack, pumps, and the stage line) that sit *beside* the anabolics. It is harm-reduction education, not medical advice or a protocol. Several of these (clenbuterol, T3, contest diuretics, insulin-adjacent agents) are genuinely dangerous and a few have killed people; doses are given only where they are standard harm-reduction reference or over-the-counter, and **never** for contest diuretics. The compounds are prescription-only, research chemicals, investigational drugs or banned substances in most jurisdictions, including Malaysia. Work with a physician.

## The goal shifts: from protection to the look

[[Part 3.0 - The Risk Map|Part 3]] is about **defense**: keeping your [[Part 3.3 - Protection|heart, liver, and hormones]] intact. This page is the opposite: the compounds whose entire job is **performance and appearance**, getting leaner, fuller, drier, more vascular, more conditioned. They are neither anabolics ([[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|Part 2.0]]) nor protective ancillaries ([[Part 3.5 - Choosing Your Ancillaries|Part 3.5]]); they are the third drawer of the toolbox.

Two honest framings before the list:

- **This is the shiniest of the shiny objects.** In [[Part 4.0 - Pharmacology|90/10]] terms, the anabolics are already the margin; *this* drawer is the margin on the margin, overwhelmingly **cut-phase and contest tools** layered on a physique that is already built. Most of the year, most people don't need any of it.
- **Most of these you've already met.** The [[Part 4.0 - Pharmacology|Fit series' Pharmacology chapter]] introduced clenbuterol, cardarine and the fat-loss compounds as "Half-Natty" tools. Here we look at them through the *enhanced athlete's* lens, and through the framing that makes this drawer make sense: direct vs. indirect effects.[^1]

## How to read a compound: direct vs. indirect

Every compound here has a **direct mechanism** (what it does at the receptor) and a set of **prominent indirect effects** (the downstream consequences that are usually the *real* reason people run it). ==Reading a compound by its indirect effects is how you actually predict what it will do to your physique and performance.== Each entry below is structured: *direct mechanism → direct effect → the prominent indirect effects.*

## Fat loss & conditioning

### Clenbuterol

- **Direct:** a β2-adrenergic agonist (an asthma drug) that spikes the central nervous system and metabolic rate.
- **Direct effect:** thermogenesis and direct lipolysis (fat-cell breakdown), raising basal metabolic rate.
- **Indirect effects:** mild **anti-catabolic muscle sparing** in a steep deficit; **bronchodilation → better oxygenation and aerobic output**; sharper **vascularity** as fat strips and BP rises.
- **Caveats:** real cardiovascular load: tachycardia, raised BP, and at higher doses or longer durations the *wrong* kind of cardiac hypertrophy (fibrotic). Used low-dose, short courses, late in a cut, with taurine and potassium support and ketotifen to re-sensitise β2 receptors. It is the compound in this drawer that has killed people. ([[Part 4.0 - Pharmacology|More in the Fit series.]])[^2]

### Mirabegron (the β3 alternative)

- **Direct:** a β3-adrenergic agonist (an overactive-bladder drug). β3 receptors sit on brown and beige fat, not on the heart, which is the whole point.[^9]
- **Direct effect:** activates brown-fat thermogenesis and adipocyte lipolysis. In trials, resting energy expenditure rose by roughly 200 kcal a day at the doses that activated brown fat, and four weeks of daily use raised HDL and insulin sensitivity.
- **Indirect effects:** a fat-loss tailwind that keeps running without the tremor, the anxiety, the potassium drain or the two-weeks-on, two-weeks-off receptor cycling that clenbuterol needs; no ketotifen required.
- **Caveats:** selectivity is relative. The bladder label dose does little for brown fat; the trial doses that moved energy expenditure were two to four times that, and at those doses β1 spillover raises heart rate and blood pressure, less than clenbuterol but not nothing. Prescription; stacked with a stimulant it adds to the cardiovascular load rather than replacing it. ==In this series it is the gentler first choice for the adrenergic lever, and clenbuterol stays in the drawer for the short, late-cut window where its stronger lipolysis and muscle-sparing are worth the price.==

### Ephedrine (and the ECA stack)

- **Direct:** a sympathomimetic that raises catecholamine activity.
- **Direct effect:** thermogenesis and appetite suppression.
- **Indirect effects:** reduced lethargy and restored **workout capacity at very low body fat** (sub-6%), where energy craters. Classically stacked as **ECA** (ephedrine + caffeine + aspirin) for synergy.
- **Caveats:** cardiovascular, BP and heart rate; not for anyone with hypertension or cardiac concerns.

### Retatrutide

- **Direct:** a GLP-1, GIP and glucagon triple-receptor agonist, injected weekly.[^10]
- **Direct effect:** suppresses appetite and slows gastric emptying (the GLP-1 and GIP arms), raises energy expenditure and hepatic fat oxidation (the glucagon arm), and sensitises GLUT4-mediated glucose uptake, which is why the same agent sits in the bulk stack as the [[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|Pathway 8]] partitioning lever.
- **Indirect effects:** the largest pharmacological fat loss recorded in a trial (about a quarter of body weight in 48 weeks at the top dose), with the glucagon arm keeping metabolic rate from sinking the way it does on a long deficit. On a cut it also makes the deficit *easy*, which is the thing most people fail at.
- **Caveats:** ==a quarter to a third of the weight lost on the GLP class is lean mass unless protein and training are held,== which on this series' cut they are (protein at the target-weight number, the anti-catabolic anchor, HMB, the lifts held). Heart rate rises a few beats and the phase 2 data carried an arrhythmia signal at the higher doses, so it is logged with the rest of the cardiovascular numbers. Nausea and the appetite crush are the mechanism, not a side effect, and they are exactly why it is scaled to carbohydrate intake on a bulk rather than run at a fat-loss dose. Investigational (phase 3), so grey-market sourcing, with everything [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|Part 1]] said about that.

### Eloralintide (the amylin class)

- **Direct:** a selective, long-acting amylin-receptor agonist, the same class as cagrilintide. (Often written "elorantide"; the compound is eloralintide.)[^11]
- **Direct effect:** amylin is the pancreatic hormone co-secreted with insulin that signals satiety, slows gastric emptying and suppresses glucagon; the analogue does the same for a week per injection.
- **Indirect effects:** fat loss in the same league as the GLP class in phase 2 (about a fifth of body weight at 48 weeks), with the two things the amylin class is being studied for: milder gastrointestinal effects, and a better fat-to-lean ratio in the weight lost. If that holds, it is the appetite tool for someone who cannot tolerate the GLP class, or who wants a second mechanism at a lower dose of each.
- **Caveats:** the lean-sparing claim is early and not settled; phase 3 is running, so there is no approved product and no long-term data; grey-market. Two appetite agents together can take food intake below what the muscle needs, which on an enhanced cut is the one failure the whole stack is built to prevent.

### Yohimbine / Rauwolscine

- **Direct:** an α2-adrenergic *antagonist*.
- **Direct/indirect effect:** blocks the α2 "brake" on lipolysis in **stubborn fat depots** (lower back, abs), so it spot-mobilises that fat, but only in a **fasted** state (insulin neutralises it). The same mechanism covered in the [[Part 4.0 - Pharmacology|Fit series' Tier-1 fat-loss section]].
- **Caveat:** raises BP, heart rate and anxiety; fasted morning cardio use only.

### Cardarine (GW-501516): named and retired

- **Direct:** a PPAR-δ agonist, *not* a hormone or stimulant. **Direct effect:** shifts the body's primary fuel from glucose to fatty acids. **Indirect effects:** endurance (you stop "gassing out"), glycogen sparing, and a better lipid panel (HDL up, triglycerides down).[^3]
- **Why it is out of this series' designs:** the high-dose rodent carcinogenicity is not a footnote, and [[Part 3.1 - Medical Screening and Monitoring|Part 3.1]] already lists it among the agents that can accelerate a pre-existing tumour. SLU-PP-332 covers the endurance-signalling job, mirabegron and the GLP class cover the fat-oxidation job, and the statin covers the lipid job, each without that particular bill. It stays named so you know what it is when it is offered.

### Cytolin (a UGL blend)

- **What it is:** in regional underground labs, "Cytolin" is typically a **brand blend of clenbuterol with T3/T4** to maximise thyroid-driven thermogenesis.
- **Indirect effects:** aggressive energy expenditure and fat loss, but it burns through glycogen fast, so expect **muscle flatness** unless carbs are carefully timed.
- **Caveat:** ==it is a UGL blend: you are trusting a lab's ratio of two already-harsh compounds.== Knowing exactly what (and how much) you are taking is the whole [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|Part 1]] quality problem; running the components separately and dialled is the safer route.

## Thyroid for cutting: T3 (vs. the protective T4)

[[Part 3.5 - Choosing Your Ancillaries|Part 3.5]] covered **T4 (levothyroxine)** in its *protective* role: replacing the thyroid output that GH burns through. **T3 (liothyronine)** is the *aggressive* version used here for **fat loss**:[^5]

- **Direct:** the active thyroid hormone, raising metabolic rate directly (no conversion step).
- **Indirect effects:** accelerated fat loss in a cut, overriding the metabolic slowdown of prolonged dieting; often paired with a β-agonist.
- **Caveats:** ==it is catabolic to muscle and taxing on the heart.== Demands FT3/FT4 [[Part 3.1 - Medical Screening and Monitoring|tracking]], conservative dosing (a ~25 mcg replacement-level dose is a different animal from hypermetabolic contest dosing), and a careful taper; abrupt cessation leaves the thyroid axis sluggish. A contest-prep tool, not a year-round one.

## Metabolic & mitochondrial adjusters

The fat-burners above (clenbuterol, ephedrine) work by flogging the central nervous system. This drawer works *underneath* the CNS, at the mitochondria and the cell's glucose machinery, to change how fuel is handled rather than just how fast it is burned. ==The appeal is a far lower cardiovascular cost,== which is why these are the backbone of a *mini-cut* aimed at restoring insulin sensitivity without crashing training.[^6]

### SLU-PP-332

- **Direct:** an ERR (estrogen-related receptor) agonist.
- **Direct effect:** mimics the cellular signalling of endurance exercise, "exercise in a molecule."
- **Indirect effects:** raised fat oxidation and metabolic rate *without* a stimulant load, so you get a fat-loss tailwind that doesn't spike heart rate or BP the way clenbuterol does.
- **Caveat:** a research chemical with scant human data; promising mechanism, thin evidence.

### MOTS-c

- **Direct:** a mitochondrial-derived peptide.
- **Direct/indirect effect:** enhances **metabolic flexibility** (the cell's ability to switch cleanly between burning fat and glucose) and helps **prevent diet-induced insulin resistance**, the exact failure mode a long deficit creates. Pairs naturally with SLU-PP-332, and with fasted LISS, which is when the cut reference uses it (at most three sessions a week).
- **Caveat:** peptide, research-tier; injectable, limited long-term data.

### Methylene Blue

- **Direct:** acts as an electron cycler in the mitochondrial electron transport chain.
- **Indirect effects:** increases ATP production and helps clear metabolic waste during periods of intense oxidative stress, i.e. a hard cut.
- **Caveats:** ==it is hormetic (a low dose helps, a high dose harms) and it is an MAOI;== combining it with serotonergic drugs risks serotonin syndrome. Not a casual add; it also turns urine blue-green.[^7]

### 5-amino-1MQ

- **Direct:** a small-molecule inhibitor of nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT), an enzyme over-expressed in fat tissue that consumes NAD+ precursors and the methyl donor SAM.[^12]
- **Direct effect:** blocking it raises NAD+ availability and energy expenditure inside adipocytes; in mice on a high-fat diet it reduced fat mass and adipocyte size without changing food intake.
- **Indirect effects:** proposed as an "end of cut" finisher that keeps fat oxidation up when the deficit has flattened everything else, and as a muscle-regeneration aid in the same mouse models; usually paired with an NAD+ precursor (NMN or NR) so the enzyme block has substrate to work with.
- **Caveat:** ==rodent data only.== Oral and membrane-permeable, sold as a research chemical, no human dosing or safety data. It is listed because it sits in the operating layer's open-decision column, not because it is established.

### Injectable L-carnitine

- **Direct:** carnitine is the shuttle that carries long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane (the CPT system); more muscle carnitine means more fat burned per minute of work.[^13]
- **Direct effect:** raised muscle carnitine shifts fuel use toward fat at low intensity and spares glycogen at high intensity; it also raises androgen-receptor content in muscle after training ([[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|Pathway 1]]), so a given androgen dose does more.
- **Why injectable:** oral carnitine is poorly absorbed (a fifth or less) and the gut converts much of the rest to TMAO, a cardiovascular marker you do not want rising; the injectable route skips both. Muscle uptake needs insulin, which is why it is taken before training with carbohydrate rather than fasted.
- **Caveats:** injection-site irritation; the AR effect is modest; it is a year-round, both-phase tool rather than a cut compound, which is why it sits in the Tier 1 baseline below.

### MIC blend (methionine, inositol, choline)

- **Direct:** a "lipotropic" injection: methionine (a methyl donor), inositol (a second messenger in insulin signalling and lipid transport) and choline (the backbone of phosphatidylcholine, which the liver needs to export fat as VLDL), usually with B12.[^14]
- **Direct effect:** the one effect with real support is hepatic: choline repletion clears liver fat, and choline deficiency produces fatty liver on its own.
- **Indirect effects:** marketed as fat-mobilising; the honest reading is a liver-fat and methylation support tool that may make a cut slightly cleaner, not a fat-burner.
- **Caveats:** thin evidence for body-fat change in humans; cheap and low-risk, which is why it sits at the bottom of this drawer rather than being left out. Add its B-vitamin content to the tally with the P5P ([[Part 1.2 - Course Design|Part 1.2]]).

> [!important] Glucose management for the cut (the "+" combo)
> The fastest way to restore insulin-receptor sensitivity in a mini-cut is to clear glucose from two directions at once: ==**an SGLT2 inhibitor (empagliflozin / ertugliflozin) + retatrutide**, with berberine as the over-the-counter third leg.==[^8] The SGLT2 inhibitor forces the kidneys to dump excess glucose into the urine; retatrutide suppresses appetite, sensitises GLUT4 and lifts metabolic rate; berberine activates AMPK in muscle and liver. Both prescription members double as [[Part 3.5 - Choosing Your Ancillaries|year-round glucose ancillaries]], which is why they are tagged "+": they may already be running.
>
> **Metformin is out of this combination in this series' designs.** It lowers systemic IGF-1, which is the signal the GH in every design is there to raise, and it blunts training hypertrophy through the same AMPK route. While GH is running, berberine, the GLP-class agent and the SGLT2 inhibitor do the glucose job without that cost; metformin keeps a place only in a phase with no GH ([[Part 3.5 - Choosing Your Ancillaries|Part 3.5]]).

## Intracellular fullness: glycerol, HBCD, L-citrulline

MK-677 used to hold this slot. The look it was run for, muscle that sits full and tight with the veins close to the skin, comes more cheaply and more controllably from three over-the-counter tools that move water and glycogen *into* the muscle, without a ghrelin agonist's hunger, water under the skin and glucose cost.[^4]

### Glycerol

- **Direct:** an osmolyte. Taken with fluid before training, it holds extra water in the body (hyperhydration) instead of letting the kidneys clear it.
- **Direct effect:** expanded total body water and blood volume for several hours.
- **Indirect effects:** fuller muscle under a pump, more sustained output in the heat, and the joint-and-tendon cushion of a well-hydrated cell that lets heavy loads feel better. The bulk reference takes it pre-workout at the 30 g level, with the water it needs.
- **Caveats:** gut upset if taken without enough fluid; it adds water weight, so it is a bulk and peak tool, not a cut one, and it goes in the scale log as water, not tissue.

### HBCD (highly branched cyclic dextrin)

- **Direct:** a carbohydrate with very low osmolality and a high molecular weight, so it leaves the stomach fast and digests steadily.
- **Direct effect:** intra-workout glucose without the gastric sloshing or the insulin spike of dextrose; muscle glycogen is topped up during the session, and every gram of glycogen stored brings about three grams of water into the cell with it.
- **Indirect effects:** the fullness itself, plus the training volume a fuelled session allows. One serving (about 25 g) intra-workout in the bulk reference.
- **Caveats:** it is carbohydrate, so it is counted in the day's number, and it is excluded from the cut, where the remaining carbohydrate is concentrated around training in food instead ([[Part 1.2 - Course Design|Part 1.2]]).

### L-citrulline (citrate or malate)

- **Direct:** the amino acid the body uses to make arginine; taken orally it raises plasma arginine more effectively than arginine itself, and arginine is the substrate for nitric oxide.
- **Direct effect:** vasodilation.
- **Indirect effects:** the pump, and the vascularity that goes with it; around 8 g before training has improved repetitions to failure and reduced soreness in trials. It stacks with beetroot (nitrate, the other nitric-oxide route) and with tadalafil (which stops the signal being broken down), which is why all three appear in the same pre-workout.
- **Caveats:** essentially none beyond gut tolerance. It stays in on the cut, because cell swelling is one of the few growth signals a deficit leaves intact ([[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|Part 2.0]]).

## Pumps & vascularity: tadalafil

You met tadalafil as a [[Part 3.5 - Choosing Your Ancillaries|BP "super ancillary."]] Here is its *aesthetic and performance* face:

- **Direct:** a PDE5 inhibitor; blocks the breakdown of cGMP → systemic vasodilation.
- **Indirect effects:** **unreal pumps** (more blood volume forced into working muscle → a rigid structural framework that improves force transfer), **persistent vascularity** (wider, closer-to-surface veins even at rest), and **faster ATP and nutrient delivery plus lactic-acid clearance** between sets, so peak strength holds into your 4th and 5th sets instead of dropping off. With citrulline and beetroot supplying the nitric oxide it protects, the three are one pre-workout mechanism.
- **Why it is a favourite:** it is one of the rare compounds that pays off for *both* health (BP, endothelium) and aesthetics (pumps, vascularity); genuinely dual-use.

## Stage aesthetics: the diuretic line

Melanotan-2 used to open this section; it is out (a tan with less sun is not worth nausea, darkening moles and an unanswered melanoma question; a competitor uses a topical). What remains is the one line in the drawer that is drawn in red.

> [!danger] Contest diuretics: the line where this gets people killed
> Pulling water for the stage with aggressive (often loop) diuretics is the single most dangerous thing in this entire series; it has killed competitors outright via potassium and electrolyte crashes and cardiac arrhythmia. ==There are no reference doses here, on purpose.== This is expert-supervised, contest-week-only territory, and it is categorically different from the gentle, BP-driven [[Part 3.5 - Choosing Your Ancillaries|indapamide *restraint*]] in Part 3.5. If you are not a competitor with a coach and medical supervision, this does not belong anywhere near you.

## The visual & performance drivers

Reading the drawer by *goal* rather than by drug:

| Desired look / output | Primary drivers | Key support |
|---|---|---|
| **Fat loss / lean-out** | Retatrutide; mirabegron or clenbuterol; T3 | Eloralintide, yohimbine, ephedrine |
| **Intracellular fullness** | Glycerol, HBCD, L-citrulline | Tadalafil (blood volume) |
| **Endurance & work capacity** | SLU-PP-332, injectable L-carnitine | Clenbuterol, tadalafil |
| **Dryness, vascularity, sharpness** | (water management) | Tadalafil and citrulline (veins), clenbuterol |
| **Insulin sensitivity / metabolic flexibility** | SLU-PP-332, MOTS-c, the glucose stack | Methylene Blue, 5-amino-1MQ, steps and cardio |

And the **indirect strength** angle: none of these are anabolics, but three raise the weight on the bar through non-androgen routes:

- **Glycerol and HBCD** → intracellular hydration and glycogen → joint and tendon cushion plus better leverage on heavy compounds.
- **Tadalafil** → pump-as-structural-support plus faster recovery between sets → sustained power across a workout.
- **Injectable L-carnitine** → glycogen sparing → more reps at a high percentage of your max → more total volume → neurological strength adaptation over weeks.

## Tiering the cut: light, medium, heavy

The single most important discipline with this drawer is *not deploying the heavy artillery for a light job.* ==You match the accelerator intensity to how lean you already are,== the [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course#Four clocks, one veto|deployment tier]] escalating to meet the [[Part 1.2 - Course Design|body-fat tier]], never the reverse. Three tiers:

- **Tier 1, light (you are soft, or opening a mini-cut).** Don't go full-blast. Add ~10k steps a day, run a deficit or maintenance with **protein held high**, give a gentle thyroid nudge (T4 with a little T3), and clear glucose with the [[#metabolic--mitochondrial-adjusters|SGLT2 + retatrutide (+ berberine)]] combo, on top of your year-round injectable L-carnitine. A mini-cut here should be **aggressive, brief (2 to 4 weeks), and hyper-efficient at restoring insulin sensitivity** without crashing training.
- **Tier 2, medium (a genuine mid-cut).** Add the **mitochondrial layer** on top of Tier 1: SLU-PP-332 and MOTS-c for metabolic flexibility, and Methylene Blue for ATP and oxidative-waste clearance. The adrenergic lever opens here with mirabegron, the gentler β3 route.
- **Tier 3, heavy (lean, chasing a final peak before bulking back).** Earned only when you have lean-bulked ~20+ weeks without much fat and want to get truly shredded for a short window. ==Skip the glucose-management layer; you are already insulin-sensitive.== Run the full fasted-morning fat-loss routine (yohimbine + caffeine + tyrosine + creatine + coleus forskohlii), the metabolic stack (L-carnitine, MOTS-c, SLU-PP-332, Methylene Blue, and 5-amino-1MQ if you have decided to run it), thyroid plus HGH, and the β-adrenergic lever at full strength: mirabegron through the window, or a short, low-dose fasted clenbuterol run with electrolyte support for the last stretch.

> [!caution] The tier is set by your body fat, not your impatience
> The tier you deploy is decided by [[Part 1.2 - Course Design|how lean you are]] (the food lever's read, [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course#Four clocks, one veto|Part 1.3]]), not by how badly you want to be shredded. Stepping straight to the heavy stack while soft just burns through your CNS and your insulin sensitivity for nothing, and the moment a marker the [[Part 3.2 - Continuous Monitoring|monitoring]] watches turns the wrong way, the tier steps back down.

## Part 2.2 Takeaways

> [!check] Key concepts to internalize
> 
> - **This drawer is performance and aesthetics, not protection or anabolism,** and it is the margin on the margin: mostly cut and contest tools on an already-built physique.
> - **Read every compound by its indirect effects;** that is what actually predicts the physique and performance outcome.
> - **The adrenergic lever has a gentle setting now.** Mirabegron (β3) for the long stretch; clenbuterol kept for the short, late window where its strength is worth the price.
> - **The GLP and amylin agents are the modern fat-loss core:** retatrutide makes the deficit easy and keeps metabolic rate up; eloralintide is the second appetite mechanism with a lean-sparing claim still to be proven. Both cost lean mass if protein and training slip.
> - **The metabolic drawer works under the CNS:** SLU-PP-332, MOTS-c, Methylene Blue, 5-amino-1MQ (rodent data), with injectable L-carnitine year-round and the MIC blend at the bottom as liver support.
> - **Metformin comes out while GH runs;** SGLT2 plus retatrutide plus berberine is the glucose combo.
> - **Fullness is glycerol, HBCD and citrulline, not MK-677.**
> - **Contest diuretics are the deadliest line in the series:** expert-and-coach-only, no DIY, categorically different from BP-management diuretics.
> - **Protection and monitoring matter more here, not less:** stimulants, the GLP class and thyroid move exactly the markers [[Part 3.0 - The Risk Map|Part 3]] watches.

## Your Extra-Compounds Task List

1. **Ask whether you even need this drawer.** Most of it is cut or contest only; if you are not lean and not prepping, the honest answer is usually "not yet."
2. **Map any compound to its indirect effects** before running it; know the downstream reason you are using it, not just the marketing.
3. **Choose the adrenergic setting deliberately:** mirabegron for the window, clenbuterol only for the short last stretch, both with electrolyte and taurine support and BP and heart-rate tracking.
4. **Hold protein and the lifts on any GLP or amylin agent,** and log the heart rate.
5. **Treat T3 as a contest tool:** track FT3/FT4, taper, never year-round.
6. **Leave contest diuretics alone** unless you are a supervised competitor.
7. **Layer protection underneath:** the [[Part 3.5 - Choosing Your Ancillaries|ancillary stack]] and [[Part 3.2 - Continuous Monitoring|monitoring]] run *through* any cut phase.
