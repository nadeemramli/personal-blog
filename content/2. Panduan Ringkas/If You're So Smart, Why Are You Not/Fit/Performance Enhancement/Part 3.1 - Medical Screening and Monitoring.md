## What screening can answer

Tell the clinician about symptoms, family history, fertility plans, and everything you take. Ask which tests fit the situation and what each result would change. Blood pressure, lipids, blood count, and relevant organ or hormone tests are examples of domains a clinician may consider. High muscle mass can complicate the interpretation of creatinine-based kidney estimates. [Primary-care testing guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10962511/)

## Cancer screening and fertility

Circulating tumour markers generally perform poorly as broad screening tests in asymptomatic people. Discuss age- and risk-appropriate screening with your clinician. If future fertility matters, ask what assessment or referral makes sense given your exposure history. [National Cancer Institute on tumour markers](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/diagnosis/tumor-markers-fact-sheet) · [HAARLEM reproductive follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33550376/)

Use the lab's preparation instructions and ask the ordering clinician how to interpret results in context. A baseline gives a point of comparison. It cannot establish that future exposure is safe.
