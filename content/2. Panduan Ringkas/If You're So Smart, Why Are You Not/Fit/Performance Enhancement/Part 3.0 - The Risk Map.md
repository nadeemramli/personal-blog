## What kills enhanced athletes

Not liver failure, not kidney failure, not the things the forums argue about. The long-term users who die early overwhelmingly die of **cardiovascular disease**: atherosclerotic events and cardiac remodelling, accelerated by years of unfavourable lipids, raised blood pressure, raised hematocrit and a thickened, stiffened heart.[^1] Everything else on this page matters, but the order below is the order of what ends careers and lives. The first two categories are the ones to defend hardest.

That is the Design-tier framing from [[The Upward Spiral — How the Order Compounds|Start Here]] made concrete: the margin of safety for this bet is a set of numbers, and the most important of them are the cardiovascular ones.

## 1. Cardiovascular: lipids and atherogenic burden

**Mechanism.** Androgens, oral androgens above all, accelerate hepatic lipase, which strips HDL-C and shifts LDL toward small, dense particles. Isotretinoin, if it is in the picture, pushes hepatic lipid synthesis the other way at the same time: triglycerides and VLDL up, HDL-C down. The combined picture is more atherogenic particles, fewer protective ones.

**Markers.**
- **ApoB (apolipoprotein B)**: the count of atherogenic particles in circulation. This is the number to steer by, because LDL-C can look acceptable while particle count is not.[^2]
- The standard panel: triglycerides, total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C.
- Particle quality and ratios: non-HDL-C, triglyceride-to-HDL ratio, and **ApoA1** (the protective-particle count), so the ApoB:ApoA1 ratio can be tracked.

**Risk.** Accelerated plaque formation over years; acutely, triglycerides high enough (above roughly 5.6 mmol/L, 500 mg/dL) to risk pancreatitis.

## 2. Cardiovascular: vascular inflammation and endothelial function

**Mechanism.** Oxidised lipids, raised blood pressure and androgen (and retinoid) exposure degrade the endothelial glycocalyx and inflame the arterial wall. The lipids in category 1 are the fuel; this is the fire.

**Markers.**
- **hs-CRP**: systemic inflammation.
- **Homocysteine**: independently damages the vessel lining; worth knowing because it is cheap to correct when it is a B-vitamin problem.
- **Lipoprotein(a)**: largely genetic, measured once; it decides how much margin you have before you start.
- Fibrinogen, and the blood-pressure log from [[Part 3.2 - Continuous Monitoring|3.2]].

**Risk.** Atherosclerosis, arterial stiffness, and over a career the probability of an event. The heart muscle itself (left-ventricular thickening and reduced function) is the other half of this category, tracked by echocardiogram, as [[Part 3.3 - Protection|3.3]] covers.

## 3. Hematology: red-cell mass and viscosity

**Mechanism.** Androgens stimulate erythropoietin; red-cell mass and blood viscosity rise. Isotretinoin can occasionally do the opposite (mild transient marrow suppression), which is why the whole count is read, not just hematocrit.

**Markers.** RBC, haemoglobin, **hematocrit**; ferritin, serum iron, TIBC (iron dynamics, because repeated phlebotomy empties the iron store); WBC and platelets.

**Risk.** Hematocrit in the low fifties, combined with the dehydration that both androgens and retinoids encourage, raises vascular resistance, blood pressure and thrombosis risk. This is the category most directly under your own control day to day: hydration, electrolytes ([[Part 3.6 - Electrolyte Management|3.6]]), and the hematocrit playbook in [[Part 3.4 - When the Numbers Move|3.4]].

## 4. Hepatic and renal integrity

**Mechanism.** Oral androgens and isotretinoin are both processed through hepatic cytochrome pathways, loading the liver. The kidneys are strained less by the compounds themselves than by what surrounds them: heavy training, high protein, altered haemodynamics and blood pressure.

**Markers.**
- Hepatic: ALT (most liver-specific), AST (also in skeletal muscle, so it rises with training), GGT (cholestasis and bile strain, and an early marker of oxidative load), total and direct bilirubin, ALP.
- Renal: **cystatin C** (unaffected by muscle mass or protein intake, unlike creatinine, so it is the honest kidney number for a trained person), creatinine, BUN, eGFR, the BUN:creatinine ratio, uric acid; urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio for early glomerular damage.

**Risk.** Transaminase spikes, cholestatic strain, and the opposite error: over-reading creatinine in a muscular athlete and panicking about kidneys that are fine. Cystatin C exists to prevent that.

## 5. Cellular stress and mitochondrial load

**Mechanism.** Retinoids disrupt mitochondrial membrane potential, raising reactive-oxygen-species generation and depleting glutathione. Androgens raise mitochondrial respiration rates and oxidative stress in muscle and liver. Together the antioxidant buffer is spent faster than it is rebuilt.

**Markers.** GGT (double duty: glutathione turnover), total antioxidant capacity, oxidised LDL, the GSH:GSSG ratio, serum malondialdehyde where available.

**Risk.** The quiet costs: muscle fatigue, slow recovery, the dry joint pain that creeps in on a cut, early hepatic strain. This is what the standing TUDCA and NAC in [[Part 3.3 - Protection|Part 3.3]] are defending.

## 6. Glycaemic control and insulin dynamics

**Mechanism.** Retinoids reduce peripheral insulin sensitivity; some androgen regimens cause transient insulin resistance or raise hepatic glucose output; growth hormone raises fasting glucose. The lean bulk in this series runs on insulin working well, so this category is also a *performance* category.

**Markers.** Fasting glucose, fasting insulin, **HOMA-IR** (fasting insulin × fasting glucose ÷ 405, with glucose in mg/dL), HbA1c.

**Risk.** Subclinical insulin resistance, postprandial spikes, and the loss of the partitioning efficiency the whole stack is built to exploit. If HOMA-IR drifts up during a bulk, the bulk is already failing.

## 7. The endocrine axis and fertility

**Mechanism.** Exogenous androgens shut the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis down through negative feedback. Isotretinoin does not itself cause male infertility, but systemic stress and altered hormone cascades affect vitality and spermatogenesis.

**Markers.** LH and FSH (the axis itself; on a course they read near zero, which is the point, and between courses they are the recovery signal); total and free testosterone, SHBG; sensitive estradiol, prolactin, progesterone; semen analysis if fertility is live.

**Risk.** Hypogonadism, testicular atrophy, oligospermia or azoospermia, libido and mood effects. Protecting this axis (HCG on a course, sperm banked before, a real PCT at least once) is [[Part 3.7 - Coming Off and PCT|3.7]], which this series now reads as the fourth part of Protection rather than as "coming off".

## Compounding factors: isotretinoin and friends

Anything stacked on top of the androgens changes the map. Isotretinoin is the common one and it compounds four of the seven categories (lipids, hepatic load, mitochondrial stress, insulin sensitivity) while adding dehydration that worsens the hematology picture. Others worth naming: thyroid agents (category 6 and the heart), stimulant-class fat-loss agents (blood pressure, the heart), oral-only stacks (categories 1 and 4 together). The rule: **every added compound gets mapped onto the seven categories before it is added**, and the categories it touches get drawn more often while it is in.

## How the cardiovascular trade is mitigated

Because categories 1 and 2 are what kill, this is the part of Protection to understand first. In order of leverage:

1. **Dose and compound choice.** The largest lipid damage comes from oral androgens and from high total androgen load. [[Part 1.1 - Goal Design|The equation]] (dose derived from training stress) and the [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course|adjustment ladder]] are cardiovascular protection before any drug is.
2. **ApoB as the target.** Cardiology sets ApoB and LDL-C targets by risk tier; an enhanced athlete with years of exposure ahead should treat themselves as high-risk and aim for the high-risk tier, agreed with a physician. The number is steered, not watched.
3. **Lipid-lowering drug classes.** **Statins** inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, cutting hepatic cholesterol synthesis and lowering LDL-C and ApoB substantially; **ezetimibe** blocks intestinal cholesterol absorption (NPC1L1) and adds a further reduction on top of a statin.[^3] Both are prescription, both have their own side-effect profiles (statins and muscle symptoms are a real conversation for a lifter), and [[Part 3.3 - Protection|3.3]] covers them in depth. The principle here is only that *they exist, they work on the number that matters, and refusing them while running oral androgens is choosing the wrong hill.*
4. **Blood pressure.** Managed with sodium and water discipline first ([[Part 3.6 - Electrolyte Management|3.6]]), then with the antihypertensive classes in [[Part 3.3 - Protection|3.3]]. Hypertension is the multiplier on everything above.
5. **Hematocrit.** Hydration, iron awareness, and the playbook in [[Part 3.4 - When the Numbers Move|3.4]].
6. **The heart as a muscle.** An echocardiogram before, and periodically during, a career. Cardiac remodelling is silent until it isn't.
7. **Aerobic work.** The [[Part 1.0 - What Athleticism Actually Is|Athletic series]] made the case that VO2 max is the strongest modifiable predictor of mortality. The AMPK lever being kept *off* around lifting (see [[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|2.0]]) is a scheduling rule, not an exemption: Zone 2 on rest days is cardiovascular protection the drugs cannot replace.

## The monitoring checklist, by category

| Category | High-yield markers to order |
|---|---|
| **Lipids / atherogenic burden** | **ApoB**, ApoA1, triglycerides, HDL-C, LDL-C, non-HDL-C |
| **Inflammation / endothelium** | **hs-CRP**, **homocysteine**, Lp(a) once, fibrinogen; BP log |
| **Hematology** | CBC (RBC, Hb, HCT, WBC, platelets), ferritin, iron, TIBC |
| **Hepatic** | ALT, AST, GGT, bilirubin, ALP |
| **Renal** | **Cystatin C**, eGFR, creatinine, BUN, uric acid, urine ACR |
| **Cellular / oxidative** | GGT, total antioxidant capacity, oxLDL |
| **Glycaemic** | Fasting glucose, fasting insulin (**HOMA-IR**), HbA1c |
| **Endocrine / fertility** | LH, FSH, total and free T, SHBG, sensitive E2, prolactin; semen analysis |

The cadence (what is drawn at baseline, what is drawn on a course and how often) is [[Part 3.1 - Medical Screening and Monitoring|3.1]]'s job; this table is the *why* behind its panel.

## Part 3.0 Takeaways

> [!check] Key concepts to internalize
> - **Cardiovascular disease is what kills enhanced athletes.** Defend categories 1 and 2 hardest.
> - **ApoB is the number to steer by,** with ApoA1 beside it. LDL-C alone can lie.
> - **Cystatin C is the honest kidney number** for a trained person; creatinine over-reads.
> - **Seven categories, one map.** Every compound you add gets mapped onto it before it goes in.
> - **Mitigation has an order:** dose and compound choice, then the lipid target, then the drug classes that hit it, then blood pressure, hematocrit, the heart itself, and aerobic work.
> - **The axis is the seventh category and the fourth part of Protection,** not an afterthought at the end of a course.

## Your Risk-Map Task List

1. **Order the baseline** from the checklist above, including the one-time Lp(a), before anything is added.
2. **Set the ApoB target** with a physician, at the high-risk tier.
3. **Book the echocardiogram** and write the date of the next one.
4. **Map every compound in the current or planned stack** onto the seven categories; mark which categories need more frequent draws.
5. **Write the mitigation order** into the operating layer, so that when a number moves ([[Part 3.4 - When the Numbers Move|3.4]]) the response is already decided.
