## Snapshot and trend

For home blood pressure, use a validated upper-arm monitor with a correctly sized cuff. Follow standard positioning and rest instructions and record readings so the clinician can interpret the trend. A wearable can help you record patterns in activity, sleep, or heart rate. Bring persistent changes or concerning symptoms to a clinician. [American Heart Association home monitoring guidance](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings/monitoring-your-blood-pressure-at-home)

## When a reading changes

For an unexpected reading without symptoms, follow the device instructions and your clinician's plan for repeating it or making contact. Chest pain, severe shortness of breath, or stroke symptoms warrant urgent medical assessment. Do not delay care to gather more readings. [American Heart Association home monitoring guidance](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings/monitoring-your-blood-pressure-at-home)
