## Fluid balance has context

Sweat, illness, medicines, and kidney function can change how the body handles fluid and electrolytes. A change in scale weight or visible water retention does not tell you which electrolyte, if any, is out of range.

## Potassium deserves particular care

Kidney disease, some blood-pressure drugs, and potassium-sparing diuretics can increase the risk of high blood potassium. Supplements and potassium-based salt substitutes can add to the risk. Ask a clinician or pharmacist about those products if you have kidney disease or take a medicine that affects potassium. [NIH potassium fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/potassium-healthprofessional/)

Keep an account of the products you use and the circumstances in which symptoms appear. Share that context when seeking advice about persistent symptoms or an abnormal lab result.
