## Start with the indication

An ancillary is an additional drug or supplement taken alongside another substance. The term says nothing about whether it is indicated. Ask which diagnosed condition a proposed drug treats and whether the evidence applies to the person taking it.

## Read the trade-offs together

Aspirin for primary prevention has a bleeding trade-off. Some blood-pressure medicines and potassium-sparing diuretics can raise potassium, especially when kidney function is impaired. Adding products can change the risks of products already in use. [USPSTF aspirin recommendation](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/document/RecommendationStatementFinal/aspirin-to-prevent-cardiovascular-disease-preventive-medication) · [NIH potassium fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/potassium-healthprofessional/)

Bring the entire list, including supplements and over-the-counter products, to a clinician or pharmacist. Ask who will review interactions and monitor for adverse effects.
