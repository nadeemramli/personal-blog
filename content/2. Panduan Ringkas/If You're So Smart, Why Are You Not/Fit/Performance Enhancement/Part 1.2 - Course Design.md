## Courses, not cycles

The word everyone uses is *cycle*, and Broderick Chavez's objection to it is worth adopting whole.[^1] A cycle is a loop: seasons, weather, a wheel. You go round and end up where you started. That is exactly what a plan of this kind is *not* for. Nobody takes on the risk in [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|Part 1.0]] in order to come back to baseline. A **course** is a line from A to B: where you are now, where you want to be, and a map between them that admits there are different routes, obstacles and starting points. Three things follow from the word, and they are the structure of this page.

1. **A course is individual, because it starts from a baseline.** A cycle is a recipe people copy and repeat. A course begins with an assessment of where you stand (how much muscle you carry, how much fat), and the direction changes with the start point: a frame that needs mass travels a different road from a physique that needs density, and the compounds follow the road rather than the other way round.
2. **A course has a defined length.** Around sixteen weeks, inside a range of twelve to twenty, because that is roughly the limit of what a human can sustain at full intensity before fatigue, markers and judgement all degrade. Chavez's critique of indefinite "blasting" is the critique of a plan with no end: staying on because the bloods still look acceptable at week seventeen is not a decision, it is drift. A course ends on the date it was given, and the time off afterward is part of the course, not an interruption of it.
3. **A course is measured, so the next one can be designed.** You quantify what it produced (the tape, the lifts, the panel), you take the time off, and the next course is designed from the new baseline. That is how a plan compounds rather than repeats.

Derek's "you only have so many blasts" from [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|Part 1.0]] is the same idea from the cost side: a blast is a course, the count is finite, and a course spent going round in a circle is a course gone. The rest of the series uses *course* for the planned block, *cruise* or *down-period* for the time between, and keeps *cycle* only where it is a fixed term the rest of the world uses (PCT, post-cycle therapy, is the one that stays).

## The two courses: cut and build

The composition target from [[Part 1.1 - Goal Design|Goal Design]] (lean-mass cap, fat target, water cap) can only be approached from one of two directions at a time, because building tissue needs a surplus and losing fat needs a deficit, and the body will not do both well in the same week.

| Course | Job | Lead variable | What "winning" looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Build** (lean bulk) | Add contractile tissue while keeping fat and water inside their caps | Carbohydrate, and the insulin environment it creates | Scale up slowly, lifts up, waist flat, Vacuum Rule still passes at night |
| **Cut** | Remove fat while holding every gram of muscle and strength | The deficit, and the lipolytic environment it needs | Scale down on a 7-day average, waist down at the navel, lifts held |

Everything else in this series follows from which side of this fork you are on: which pathways get pulled and which compound class does each job ([[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|Part 2.0]]'s by-phase tables), what the food looks like (below), what the monitoring watches for ([[Part 3.2 - Continuous Monitoring|3.2]]). ==The two are not the same course at different calories. They are different courses, with different compounds, different timing and different water.==

There is no third course called "maintenance" in the sense of a plan; maintenance is the short bridge between a cut and a build, one to two weeks at maintenance calories so appetite and hormones settle, and the cruise is a drug posture, not a course at all (see [[#course-duration-and-the-time-between-courses|below]]).

## The build course is lean by definition

The most common, most expensive mistake in enhanced bodybuilding is treating the offseason as permission to eat everything and balloon up. A natural lifter can get away with a sloppy surplus because the natural ceiling on protein synthesis is the bottleneck, not the metabolic environment. On a course the ceiling has been raised, which means the environment *becomes* the bottleneck, and a dirty bulk degrades exactly the environment the compounds are supposed to exploit:

- **Insulin sensitivity falls** as fat mass rises, and every pathway downstream of insulin (partitioning, mTORC1 signalling, GLUT4 transport) gets weaker. You are paying for an anabolic environment and then switching it off with fat.
- **Systemic inflammation rises** with adiposity, and inflammation suppresses muscle protein synthesis. Same money, less muscle.
- **Aromatase activity rises** with body fat, so the estrogen side of the stack gets harder to manage at the same dose. The [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|readiness gate]] already said to be under ~12% for this reason; a dirty bulk walks you back over it.
- **The health cost is paid in full for a fraction of the benefit.** Lipids, blood pressure and hematocrit move with the drugs regardless of whether the calories became muscle or fat.
- **It costs you the cut as well.** Every kilogram of fat the build adds has to come off later, and on a course the cut is the expensive phase: it is where the sharp tools get spent and where the heart pays fastest. A dirty bulk buys a longer, harder, more compound-heavy cut. In [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|Part 1.0]]'s arithmetic that is one blast spent putting fat on and another spent taking it off.
- **It makes the dashboards unreadable.** When scale weight, water, fat and tissue all rise at once, the [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course|four clocks]] cannot tell you what moved. A lean build is slow enough to read.

So "bulk" on a course only ever means *lean* build: the smallest surplus that keeps strength rising, with the waist and the Vacuum Rule as the governors. The professional version is **strategic consistency**: success is not sheer mass; it is the meticulous management of digestive health, nutrient partitioning and the avoidance of systemic edema, staying lean, marketable and high-functioning year-round. It is the same lesson as [[Part 1.1 - What Actually Matters|Part 1.1]]'s clean-bulk decision tree with the stakes raised: on a course, a sloppy surplus does not just blur your abs, it degrades the metabolic environment the drugs are supposed to exploit.

## Two kinds of building course

"Build" hides a fork of its own, and it is worth naming even though this series writes only one side of it for now.

- **The mass course** builds overall size and the strength that carries it: compound movements, progressive load, the belly of the muscle. Its nutrition is the surplus described below and its compounds are the ones [[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|Part 2.0]] maps. This is the build course the rest of the series means.
- **The shape course** builds maturity and proportion rather than mass: isolation and unilateral work, controlled tempo, the shape of a muscle rather than its size, with a smaller surplus and a drier, more cosmetic compound emphasis. Enhanced bodybuilders report that this kind of regional, shape-first work pays off in a way it rarely does for a natural lifter, whose hormonal ceiling limits how much a single muscle can be pushed on its own.

They are different courses, with different programs, different food and different compound emphasis, and the KPIs below are written for the mass course. The shape course gets its own page when it is written; until then, treat it as the second road, not a variation of the first.

## Where you stand decides which course comes first

A course is a tool for a direction. If you don't know whether the next course is a cut or a build, you cannot design it, and whether it is a cut or a build is not a preference. It falls out of two numbers.

### Two numbers locate you

**Fat-free mass index (FFMI)** is lean mass divided by height squared (kg/m²), the same shape as BMI but applied only to the part of you that isn't fat. It answers "how much muscle am I carrying for my frame?" A normalised version adds 6.1 × (1.8 − height in metres) so that short and tall lifters can be compared.[^2] The reference points this series uses:

| Normalised FFMI (men) | Reading |
|---|---|
| Below 20 | Well inside the natural range. Training age, food and sleep are the levers; pharmacology would be spending a blast on an execution problem. |
| 20 to 23.5 | The natural middle. Most of the ceiling is still unused. |
| 23.5 to 25.5 | At the natural ceiling. This is where the [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|readiness gate]] starts to be a real conversation. |
| 25.5 to 29.5 | Past the natural ceiling, inside the lean aesthetic target this series aims at. |
| Above 29.5 | The open-class realm this series deliberately stops short of ([[Part 1.2 - Course Design#The fourth year and after: the horizon|the horizon]]). |

Women's references sit roughly three points lower across the board.

**Body fat percentage** answers the other question: "is the environment ready for a surplus, or does the fat have to come off first?" On a course this is not cosmetic. Adipose tissue is where aromatase lives and where insulin sensitivity is lost, so body fat decides whether a surplus becomes muscle or becomes side effects.

Together: FFMI says how far you are from the lean-mass target, body fat says whether you are allowed to chase it yet.

### Reading FFMI honestly

FFMI is only as honest as the body-fat number that feeds it. Three rules:

1. **Use the same instrument every time.** A DEXA or a calibrated scanner for the anchor every few months, the scale and the mirror for the trend between. Switching instruments mid-course produces fake progress and fake stalls.
2. **Measure in the same state.** Morning, after the bathroom, before food and water. On a course, water swings of two to three kilograms are ordinary, and water counts as lean mass on every scanner.
3. **Don't let an inflated FFMI skip the gate.** A watery, glycogen-loaded 26 is not a lean 26. [[Part 1.1 - Goal Design|Goal Design]] put a water cap in the composition target for exactly this reason.

### Estimating body fat without a scan

If you don't have a scan, the mirror is a usable instrument as long as you ask it specific questions rather than "how lean am I?":

| What you see (men, relaxed, normal light) | Roughly |
|---|---|
| No abs visible; fat on the back and face as well as the belly | Above 18%, often above 20% |
| Upper abs only; love handles present | 15 to 18% |
| Most of a six-pack; lower belly still soft | 12 to 15% |
| Full six-pack, obliques showing, veins on the forearms and shoulders | Below 12% |

Then ask where fat still sits. If it is everywhere, add two points. If there is very little anywhere and the arms are vascular, take two off. Women run roughly eight points higher at the same look. The estimate is ±3 points, which is good enough to pick a course and not good enough to track a week, so the trend numbers (7-day average weight, waist at the navel) do the tracking.

### The decision rule

For men (women: add eight points to every body-fat threshold):

| Where you are | What comes next | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Body fat above ~15%** | **Cut**, to about 10 to 12% | Above the surplus ceiling. A build here feeds aromatase and insulin resistance, not muscle. The leaner you start the build, the more of the signal goes to tissue. |
| **Body fat 12 to 15%, FFMI well inside the natural range** | **Recomp**, or a short cut then a build | The "skinny-fat" square. A recomposition at maintenance (protein high, training as the lever) works here because there is so much unused natural ceiling; a short cut to the abs line and a clean build is faster and easier to read. |
| **Body fat 12 to 15%, FFMI near or past the ceiling** | **Short cut** to the abs line | You have earned the build; a four-to-eight-week cut makes it clean. |
| **Body fat 8 to 12%** | **Build**, 16 to 26 weeks | Inside the push window with room under the ceiling. Build, with the waist and the Vacuum Rule as governors, and audit every eight weeks. |
| **At the lean-mass target, body fat in the window** | **Hold and peak** | More size is a different sport with a steeper bill. The job is maintenance, refinement and recovery. |

Two gates sit underneath the rule, both expanded in the [[#the-body-fat-gates|KPIs]] below: never run a surplus above ~15% body fat, and when a build crosses it (or weight rises while strength stalls), a two-to-four-week mini-cut restores the environment before the build resumes.

## Course duration, and the time between courses

**A build course runs 16 to 26 weeks, a cut course runs in blocks of 4 to 8.** The asymmetry is the physiology. Muscle is built in grams per day whether or not you are enhanced, so the build needs months; the enhanced cut is anti-catabolic by design (the 19-nor, the dry oral, HMB, protein held, [[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|pathway 5]]), so the deficit can be aggressive and *short*, and a long enhanced cut is where the heart pays. Chavez's sixteen weeks is the centre of the build range; the cut's eight-week cap is the 19-nor's cap and the heart's.

| Course | Length | Natural rate | Enhanced rate | Why the difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cut** | **4 to 8 weeks**, then a maintenance week; repeat if more fat has to come off | 0.5 to 0.75% of bodyweight per week | 0.75 to 1.0% of bodyweight per week | The stack holds the muscle, so the deficit can be steep and brief. Longer is where the heart pays. |
| **Build** | **16 to 26 weeks**, audited every 8 | 0.25 to 0.4% of bodyweight per week | 0.35 to 0.5% of bodyweight per week | Tissue is built in grams a day either way; the enhanced advantage is the rate and the recovery, not the licence to eat. |
| **Recomp** | 12 to 16 weeks | Scale flat; waist down 2 to 4 cm; lifts up every two weeks | Same, faster | Works when the natural ceiling is far away; stops working as you approach it. |
| **Maintenance bridge** | 1 to 2 weeks | 0 | 0 | Lets appetite and hormones settle between a cut and a build. |

Worked example, so the shape is clear: an 80 kg man at 18% body fat is 65.6 kg lean. The cut to 11% removes about 6.3 kg of fat; enhanced, at 0.6 to 0.8 kg a week, that is eight weeks, which fits one block. A maintenance week, then a 24-week build at 0.26 to 0.37 kg a week adds about 7.5 kg, most of it tissue if the audits pass, ending near 81 kg at 14 to 15%, back at the gate. That is the year: one cut, one build, a mini-cut if the gate is hit early, and the next decision is made by re-running the two numbers.

**The time between courses is part of the course.** Professional longevity favours *courses by the calendar*: pharmacological intensity aligned with lifestyle and environmental stress rather than running hot indefinitely.[^3]

- **Environmental syncing.** Taper during periods of high ambient heat and humidity or high life stress. Androgens and GH raise metabolic rate; in a brutal hot season that extra thermogenic load can tip you into sympathetic overload and burnout. Tapering in windows when 100% training intensity is environmentally impossible lets the system clean out.
- **The down-period is 3 to 4 months, not 3 to 4 weeks.** A structured ~8-month "on" / 3-to-4-month "down" split across the year. ==Clearing a course and *restoring* your health are not the same thing:== it takes roughly 4 to 6 weeks just for the last course to clear your system, and longer still for lipids, liver, kidneys and fertility to actually normalise, so a *real* down-period runs 3 to 4 months (a 4-week break is a clean-out, not a recovery). During it, HCG and HMG restore fertility and the neurosteroids heavy courses sequester ([[Part 3.7 - Coming Off and PCT|3.7]]). The payoff is a **buffer of health** to spend on the next push.
- **You do not need to fear the down-period.** Thanks to muscle memory, the [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|permanent myonuclei]] accrued while on, volume lost during a clean-out is typically reclaimed within about six weeks of re-introducing a moderate course. The gains aren't gone; they're banked.[^4]

> [!caution] Why this beats perpetual blast-and-cruise
> Blast-and-cruise keeps your axis suppressed and your organs under continuous androgenic load with no recovery window. Courses by the calendar build in mandatory clean-out periods for your heart, liver, kidneys and HPTA, while muscle memory protects the gains. It is the difference between sprinting until you collapse and pacing a race you intend to finish at sixty.

> [!note] Cruise vs. build vs. "HRT+": the words matter
> These overlap in conversation but are not the same thing:
> - **Cruise** is a *drug posture*: a low maintenance dose that holds your gains while health markers recover. It is **not** a nutrition phase and it is not a course.
> - **Build / off-season** is a *nutrition phase*: a caloric surplus to add tissue. You can be off-season *and* cruising, but you won't build much new muscle while cruising; you're maintaining and cleaning out.
> - **"HRT+"** at the *start* of a build course is the course's opening anabolic incentive, not a cruise.
>
> The full rhythm is a four-phase loop: **Preparation** (natural optimisation, sub-12% body fat, a strength logbook) → **Build course** (start low, climb the [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course|adjustment ladder]]) → **Cut course** (deficit, the dry half of the stack holds muscle) → **Cruise / down-period** (3 to 4 months restoring health). Then the numbers are re-run and the next course is designed from them: push to calculated unhealthiness, then pull back to recover.

## Course KPIs: how you know it is working, week by week

A course has four kinds of number: the rate, the gates, the lifts and the audits. [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course|Running the Course]] is how you decide what to change when one of them moves; this section is what the numbers should be.

### The rates

The weekly rate from the table above is the first KPI, read on a **7-day average** of morning weight, never a single day:

- **Build:** 0.35 to 0.5% of bodyweight a week. Faster than this is fat with a muscle-building excuse; slower while the lifts still rise is fine, and usually means the surplus is being partitioned well.
- **Cut:** 0.75 to 1.0% of bodyweight a week. Faster is water and muscle, not fat; a week of no change on the average with the waist still falling is water, not failure.
- **Waist at the navel**, weekly, same conditions: on a build it holds; on a cut it falls. The waist is what separates tissue from fat when the scale is moving.
- **The Vacuum Rule**, nightly on a build: you should still be able to pull a deep abdominal vacuum at the end of the day, even at a 5,000-calorie intake. If you can't, GI transit is compromised and metabolic stagnation is coming.

### The body-fat gates

The decision is driven by **body-fat percentage plus strength progress**, not the scale. The hard rule: ==a man should **never run a surplus above ~15% body fat.**== Men tend to grow best pushing in the **~8 to 12%** window and pull back with a cut around **~15 to 17%**. Push body fat too high and you feel sluggish, watery, inflamed and flat in the gym (and insulin sensitivity craters, exactly the environment that hinders synthesis). Push it too low and recovery suffers, hunger ramps, sleep degrades and performance drops. ==The goal was never to stay shredded year-round; it is to spend most of your time in the range where you can train hard and recover like a normal human.==

### What strength should do

Strength is the KPI that tells you whether the weight is tissue, and it behaves differently on each course and each program.

- **On a mass course, the big lifts should keep moving.** Expect a load increase on the main compound lifts every week or two for most of a sixteen-week course, fastest in the first six to eight weeks (the non-genomic strength rise and the glycogen and water arrive before the tissue does), then slower but steady. A first course moves them most: in the one controlled trial that measured it, ten weeks of a supraphysiological testosterone dose plus training raised squat and bench strength by roughly a fifth to a third in men who were not advanced lifters.[^5] Later courses move them less, and the expectation should fall with each one. ==A main lift that has not moved for two weeks while you are lean is a food problem, not a drug problem== (the [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course|adjustment ladder]]).
- **On a shape course, the bar is the wrong instrument.** Progress is reps at a given load on the isolation and unilateral work, controlled tempo, and the tape; a shape course that is chasing 1RMs has turned back into a mass course.
- **On a cut, the lifts are held, not pushed.** Top sets within about 5% of where the course started, with volume reduced rather than intensity. A drop of around 10% on a main lift is the signal: the deficit is too deep, the retention tools (the 19-nor, the dry oral, HMB, protein) are missing, or the course has run past its eight weeks.
- **Program type sets the expectation.** A strength-biased program (low reps, compound, long rests) moves the bar and is the right instrument for a mass course; a hypertrophy-biased program (moderate reps, isolation, shorter rests) moves the tape and the reps. The natural series' program pages ([[Part 3.1 - The Program - Concepts|Concepts]]) still apply; what enhancement changes is the rate and the recovery between sessions, not the arithmetic of progression.

### The audits inside a course

Neither a build nor a cut runs as a flat line:

- **Inside a build: the eight-week audit.** Run it eight weeks, then assess: ==if you are holding roughly 0.5% bodyweight gain per week or less with the lifts rising, the surplus is mostly muscle; continue.== Gain much faster than that and you are adding fat, which is the cue for a brief, aggressive mini-cut (2 to 4 weeks) to restore insulin-receptor sensitivity before resuming. The mid-course bloods ([[Part 3.1 - Medical Screening and Monitoring|3.1]]) land at the same point, and a marker out of range overrides the mirror.
- **Inside a cut: refeeds and diet breaks.** A longer cut is broken up so performance and hormones don't collapse. A representative ~21-week cut (a natural one; the enhanced cut is shorter) places **refeeds** (single high-carb days restoring glycogen and leptin) around weeks 3, 6, 12, 15 and 21, with longer **diet breaks** (a week back at maintenance) around weeks 9 and 18. The schedule is the point: planned breaks beat the involuntary binge. The enhanced cut's version is the maintenance week between its 4-to-8-week blocks.
- **The end-of-course audit** is the one Chavez insists on: the tape, the lifts, the panel, written down, so the next course is designed from a new baseline rather than repeated.

> [!note] This is where the food lever hands off to the drug lever
> The whole point of running the course on a schedule is that it gives you a clean answer to "what do I change next?" If you are lean and progressing, the food is working: touch nothing. If you stall while lean, add carbohydrate. Only when extra food turns to *fat* instead of strength does the question pass up to the [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course|adjustment ladder]] and the gear. The deployment of any [[Part 2.2 - The Extra Compounds|fat-loss accelerator]] is then *tiered to how lean you are*: a light mini-cut gets light tools; only a genuinely lean physique chasing a peak earns the heavy stack.

## What the build course looks like: the food

Going enhanced raises the ceiling on how many calories you can *partition* into muscle, but only if the gut and the metabolic environment can keep up. Four protocols and two numbers define the professional approach.

**What a kilogram of muscle costs.** Two numbers matter, and both argue against a large surplus. First, the energy content of the tissue itself: muscle is mostly water; the stored energy in a kilogram of new lean mass is in the low thousands of kilocalories, with the metabolic cost of synthesising it on top. Second, the rate: even enhanced, the body adds contractile tissue at a rate measured in grams per day, not hundreds of grams. Divide the cost of a kilogram by the days it takes to build it and the *daily* energy requirement for new muscle is small, a few hundred kilocalories at most above maintenance.[^6] Everything in the surplus beyond that has one place to go. This is why the surplus is set from the lifts and the waist, not from a calculator. The working build reference in this series starts at roughly **2,400 kcal** for an 86 kg target, a surplus so modest it would read as a maintenance diet to a natural lifter; that is the point.

**Why protein comes down when carbs go up.** The natural series was protein-first for a reason: without the hormonal signal, dietary protein is the limiting input to protein synthesis. On a course the limiting input changes. Androgens raise the efficiency of protein use: testosterone increases synthesis and, crucially, the *reutilisation* of intracellular amino acids, so the same intake buys more synthesis.[^7] This is the mechanism behind the "high protein myth on gear" that the Anabolic Round Table takes apart.[^8] Carbohydrate is protein-sparing: in a surplus with high carbohydrate the body does not need to oxidise amino acids for fuel, and the insulin the carbohydrate drives is itself anti-catabolic. And excess protein has a cost: nitrogenous waste loads the kidneys the series is trying to protect ([[Part 3.0 - The Risk Map|renal integrity]]) and displaces the carbohydrate that is doing the partitioning work. ==More carbs means you need *less* protein, not more.== As carbohydrate scales toward the four-figure intakes of the top pros, protein can come down to around 175 to 200 g even for advanced athletes.

**The working numbers.** Protein at roughly **2.2 g per kg of target lean mass** (the operating layer's reference; at single-digit body fat it is within a few grams of 2.2 g per kg of target bodyweight), fat at a **floor of 40 g** rather than a fixed ceiling, and carbohydrate as the variable that scales with training stress: **250 to 300 g a day** at the start of a build, climbing toward the reference's long-run target of about 1,000 g as the lifts and the Vacuum Rule allow, with the four-figure number treated as where the road goes, not where it starts. Protein does not drop on the cut; the deficit comes out of carbohydrate, and the carbohydrate that remains is concentrated around training.

**The four protocols that make the surplus usable:**

- **Digestive fluid management.** Maintain a high daily water intake (6 to 8 L on the build, with electrolytes) to support blood volume and waste clearance, but **suspend fluids ~30 minutes before and after meals.** Drinking with meals dilutes hydrochloric acid and digestive enzymes, impairing protein breakdown and promoting intestinal fermentation. Hydrate *between* meals, not during.
- **Electrolyte stability.** Systemic water retention and blood-pressure spikes are frequently driven by *unstable electrolyte ratios*, not by the compounds themselves. Keep sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium and phosphorus consistent day to day ([[Part 3.6 - Electrolyte Management|3.6]]). Erratic minerals, not the gear, are often the real driver of subcutaneous edema and the blood pressure that comes with it.
- **The elimination diet.** Digestive efficiency is the ultimate bottleneck for growth; an inflamed gut cannot partition the nutrients exogenous androgens are driving. Remove any food that causes reflux, bloating or gas; prefer easily digested, low-FODMAP carbohydrate sources.
- **The Vacuum Rule** is the diagnostic for all three, nightly.

> [!important] Gut health is a pharmacological prerequisite
> Peak digestive health isn't a wellness nicety here; it is the precondition for tolerating the compounds at all. This is the [[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|90/10 rule]] again: the drugs are the margin, and the margin does nothing if the nutrition substrate underneath it is inflamed and leaking.

## A day on each course

Which compound class each course calls for, and why, is [[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|Part 2.0]]'s by-phase table. This section is the day itself: where the food, the training and the phase's tools sit in the twenty-four hours. The shape, not the gram counts, is what to copy.

### A build day

The two things being managed across the day are **mTORC1** (which needs insulin, amino acids and mechanical tension, in pulses) and **lipolysis** (which needs windows where insulin is low). A build day keeps the pulses large and the windows short.

| Window | Purpose | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Waking, fasted** | The one lipolytic window of the day; the peptides and thyroid support go here, away from food | Water and electrolytes. Growth hormone and T4 support (fasted morning, by the reference). The morning supplement group: garlic extract, TMG, DIM, calcium D-glucarate, B-complex. |
| **Meals through the day** | Four to five mTORC1 pulses; protein at every one | Whole food, weighed. The post-meal group after the first: omega-3 (4 softgels), CoQ10, nicotinamide riboside, zinc, D3 + K2. Fluids *between* meals, not with them. |
| **Pre-workout (45 to 60 min)** | Load the cell for tension and swelling | Carbohydrate-led meal. Glycerol 30 g, L-citrulline citrate 8 g, beetroot powder 6 g (cellular swelling, nitric oxide). Caffeine 200 mg and L-tyrosine 2 g (β2 via endogenous catecholamines). Arachidonic acid 45 minutes out. Alpha-GPC on training days. The pre-workout oral goes here by the reference. |
| **Intra-workout** | Keep insulin and amino acids present through the tension | Highly branched cyclic dextrin, one serving (about 30 g carbohydrate); EAAs 15 to 20 g actual amino acids. |
| **Post-workout** | The largest mTORC1 pulse of the day | Fast carbohydrate and a complete protein; the biggest meal. |
| **Evening** | Recovery; the oral's liver support | NAC 1,200 mg (2 × 600 mg), apigenin, vitamin C, tocotrienols. TUDCA daily while an oral is in the stack. |
| **All day** | Water and minerals | 6 to 8 L, electrolytes held steady day to day ([[Part 3.6 - Electrolyte Management|3.6]]); creatine daily. |
| **Rest days** | The AMPK lever, kept off the training days | Cardio, if any, lives here (once or twice a week, Zone 2) so AMPK signalling doesn't blunt mTOR after lifting. Training drinks are replaced by cooked rice in the rest template. |

The elimination diet runs underneath all of it, and the test is still the Vacuum Rule at the end of the day.

### A cut day

Same body, inverted priorities. The lipolytic windows get longer and are *used* (fasted LISS), carbohydrate is pulled to the training window, protein stays at target to protect tissue, and the cut's tools slot into the fasted morning. The timing is event-based, not clock-based: the reference deliberately avoids a fixed 06:00 start so it survives shift changes.

| Window | Purpose | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Waking, fasted** | Thyroid absorption; nothing competes with it | Thyroid support (T4 and T3) with plain water, 30 to 45 minutes before food, protein or caffeine. Sleep goal is 7 hours, which sets when this window opens. |
| **Morning** | The lipolytic block | The β-agonist (mirabegron through the window, or the clenbuterol class for a short last stretch) and methylene blue (morning, daily by the reference). The morning supplement group as on the build, then the fasted additions ~30 min later: coleus forskohlii 400 mg, acetyl-L-carnitine 500 mg, theacrine 50 mg. |
| **Morning, LISS days only** | Fasted low-intensity cardio with the adrenergic tools | Citrulline citrate and a PDE5 inhibitor *first* (nitric oxide and cGMP before the adrenergic load), then the α2-antagonist, then LISS. MOTS-c before LISS when it is used, at most three times a week. This block is absent on ordinary days. |
| **Caffeine** | Fasted, before work *or* the workout, and no later than 6 hours before sleep | One serving; the sleep goal governs the cutoff. |
| **Meals** | Protein at every meal; carbohydrate concentrated around training | Weighed food, within a counted total; shakes count inside the total. Post-meal supplement group as on the build. |
| **Pre-workout, training days** | Tension and swelling preserved in a deficit | The DHT-derivative oral (pre-workout, by the reference). Alpha-GPC 600 mg, beetroot powder 8 g, HMB 2 capsules. |
| **Intra-workout** | Amino acids present; carbohydrate drink *excluded* on the cut | EAAs, one serving (~19 g powder; actual amino-acid content still being confirmed). No HBCD. |
| **Immediately post-workout** | Muscle retention | HMB 2 capsules (four per training day in total), then the recovery meal. |
| **Rest days** | No training blocks | TUDCA (the cut reference carries it on rest days only). |
| **Night** | Recovery and the sleep pulse | NAC 1,200 mg, vitamin C 1,000 mg, apigenin 50 mg, tocotrienols. Growth hormone before sleep (by the reference). |
| **All day** | Water and tracking | 3 to 4 L; creatine 5 g; the 7-day weight average and the waist at the navel are the progress numbers. |

### What changes between the two days

Put the two tables side by side and five things move, and each one is a principle:

1. **Growth hormone moves from fasted morning to bedtime.** On the build it sits in the one fasted window so its partitioning side works with the first meals; on the cut it sits before sleep, away from the day's lipolytic tools and on top of the natural pulse.
2. **Thyroid goes from support to a lever.** T4 alone defends against GH depletion; adding T3 raises rate, and rate costs muscle unless the retention tools are in place. That is why HMB and the 19-nor are in the cut and not optional.
3. **The oral changes jobs.** The build's oral is wet, estrogenic and appetite-driving; the cut's oral is dry, anti-catabolic and strength-preserving. Same slot (pre-workout), opposite compound.
4. **Water halves.** 6 to 8 L to drive cellular swelling and GI transit on the build; 3 to 4 L on the cut, where the water target is about conditioning and the tracking is about a flat 7-day trend.
5. **The fasted morning becomes the working window.** On the build it is a brief gap before the pulses start. On the cut it is where the adrenergic, mitochondrial and thyroid tools do their work, and where LISS lives.

The ancillaries that do not change with the course, because they defend the organs regardless (NAC, TUDCA while an oral runs, P5P beside any 19-nor, the cardiovascular group, the baseline supplement group), are listed once in [[Part 3.3 - Protection|Part 3.3]].

## The courses in sequence: the first course to the fifth year

A course is designed one at a time, from the numbers the last one left. But the *order* of courses is not arbitrary; it follows two rules, one from this page and one from Part 2: the [[Part 2.1 - The Anabolic Steroid Family Tree|learning ladder]] (testosterone, then the DHT family, then a 19-nor, exhausting the bioidentical before the exotic) and courses by the calendar (the on-block in the cooler months, a real down-period after it, never running hot indefinitely). This section is the illustration of those two rules over a few years, for a lifter who has cleared [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|the readiness gate]] and wants a patient, lean, longevity-minded run rather than a reckless one. It is an illustration, not a prescription: the compounds are named by class and job, the doses are operating-layer numbers, and the only thing to copy is the order.[^9]

> [!important] Know the target, and that it is not "as big as possible"
> The sequence below aims at a lean, aesthetic-elite physique, roughly an **FFMI of 28 to 29.5**, a few points past the [[Part 1.1 - What Actually Matters|natural ceiling of about 25]]. That is the leaner enhanced look, not open-class mass. ==An FFMI past 30 at 120 kg and above is a different sport==, the territory of four forced pathways and short high-dose blasts, with a far steeper health bill. The sequence deliberately stops short of it. The whole moderate, longevity-minded thesis only holds if the destination is moderate too.

### The first course: earn it, then test the water

Before anything suppressive, [[Part 3.7 - Coming Off and PCT#Before the first course: test the reserve|test the reserve]]: a step-up HCG titration over a few weeks, reading how your own testosterone answers. It is reversible data-gathering before the one-way door, and it doubles as practice at injections and at reading a panel.

The first course is the most bioidentical possible start, about sixteen weeks: a modest testosterone base ([[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|the AR pathway]]), ideally injected often for stable levels, plus a Masteron-class agent from [[Part 2.1 - The Anabolic Steroid Family Tree|the DHT family]] to keep [[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|SHBG]] in a healthy window and add a dry, free-testosterone edge, and nothing that aromatises beside the base. Estrogen is held in range by injection frequency, not by an aromatase inhibitor. Bloods at week eight to ten ([[Part 3.1 - Medical Screening and Monitoring|the mid-course panel]]), home blood pressure and heart rate daily ([[Part 3.2 - Continuous Monitoring|Part 3.2]]), and the protection stack only as the markers call for it.

Then the fork the series keeps returning to: PCT or cruise. The longevity-minded call, and [[Part 3.7 - Coming Off and PCT|Part 3.7]]'s, is to run **at least one full PCT** and prove the axis recovers before any blast-and-cruise decision is made. You may cruise on a replacement dose later; do it knowing recovery works, not assuming it. The rest of the first year is a real three-to-four-month down-period: long enough to restore lipids, liver, kidneys and fertility, not just to clear the compounds (a few weeks clears them; restoring takes months). Train hard, eat well, let the panel normalise.

> [!important] Why the first course is so conservative
> Testosterone plus one DHT-family agent looks underwhelming next to the internet's gram-of-everything stacks. The restraint *is* the strategy: you learn how your own body answers the simplest possible signal, you confirm you can recover, and you do not [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|waste a blast]] you cannot get back. Everything in the second course is more legible because the first was simple.

### The second course: the real build

The main build, about twenty weeks and timed to the cooler months, built on [[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|several pathways]] rather than one slammed compound:

- **The testosterone base**, at a modest, tolerated level: the AR engine again.
- **A DHT-family dial** (Primobolan-class, with a Masteron-class agent optional for SHBG): the aromatase modulator from [[Part 2.1 - The Anabolic Steroid Family Tree#The family tree in motion: wet vs. dry is a setting, not a property|the family tree in motion]], so the base's estrogen is tuned by compound choice rather than by an aromatase inhibitor. This is what makes a long block tolerable: the dial, not a second aromatising compound, is the second injectable.
- **Growth hormone, low, with T4 beside it**: the [[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|GH/IGF-1 pathway]], taken fasted in the morning on a build, for recovery, sleep and slow partitioning gains. Cancer-marker screening first ([[Part 3.1 - Medical Screening and Monitoring|Part 3.1]]).
- **A GLP-class agent**: the [[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|insulin-sensitivity lever]], so the carbohydrate the build runs on actually partitions.
- **Insulin only as the dependent of a high-carbohydrate phase, and last.** [[Part 1.1 - Goal Design|Goal Design]] makes it a derivation of the carbohydrate number, never a standalone choice; it is the [[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|most dangerous compound]] in the kit, and many people should simply skip it and let GH, the GLP agent and food do the partitioning.
- **The wet oral (Anadrol-class), pre-workout**: the estrogenic pathway, red-cell mass for strength and vascularity, appetite. It is an oral, so GGT and lipids are watched and TUDCA runs daily while it is in. (The dry Anavar-class oral belongs to the cut, not the build.)

You climb into these; you do not start at them. Per [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course|the adjustment ladder]], each level is a ceiling you earn by holding it while progress continues, adding food when it stalls, adjusting training, and raising a compound only when the extra food turns to fat instead of strength. The block ends in [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course|calculated unhealthiness]], skewed markers at the peak, which is the cue for the down-period, not for pushing on. Through the block: mid-block and end-of-block bloods, daily home readings, and the [[Part 3.5 - Choosing Your Ancillaries|protection stack]] active throughout (the statin for the GH lipid shift and the oral's HDL hit, the ARB for pressure, a blood-donation plan for the hematocrit the base and the wet oral push, and a glucose tool if GH nudges fasting glucose: berberine or the GLP agent, not metformin alongside GH).

Then the down-period again, three to four months: the [[Part 3.7 - Coming Off and PCT|coming-off sequence]] (HCG and HMG to restore testicular function and the neurosteroids heavy courses suppress, then SERMs to restart the brain), [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|muscle memory]] banking the gains, and a re-baselined panel before the next push. You want a buffer of health, not a token few weeks.

### The third course: the sharp tools, last

The same build block, but the order of additions follows [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course#Adding a compound: the sequencing rules|the sequencing rules]], not impatience:

- **First exhaust the bioidenticals and the DHT family.** Before any 19-nor, max the golden triangle (testosterone and GH; insulin only for the full-time athlete) and the DHT-family compound (Masteron- or Primobolan-class) for hardening and estrogen management. For many lifters this *is* the ceiling, and the 19-nor never needs to enter at all.
- **The 19-nor (trenbolone-class), at a microdose, only if a specific peak demands it.** The genuine last rung: added after the DHT family and the triangle are exhausted, for a contest or a peak, by someone with proven recovery, capped at about eight weeks, with prolactin on the panel (P5P prophylactically, cabergoline only reactively) and a hard eye on the [[Part 2.2 - The Extra Compounds|mood, sleep and neuro]] effects it is infamous for. The moment mood, sleep or blood pressure deteriorates, it comes out.
- **A cut course late in the year, with [[Part 2.2 - The Extra Compounds|the cut's toolbox]].** A lean-out that is a different map, not the build at a deficit ([[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|Part 2.0]]): the base drops to a near-replacement band and the dial comes out; the 19-nor stays as the anti-catabolic anchor; the oral switches to the dry Anavar-class; GH drops and moves to bedtime; T3 joins T4 on waking; a short β-agonist run with electrolyte support; an α2-antagonist only on fasted LISS mornings; HMB around training. All of it on top of the protection stack, with monitoring tighter, not looser, because this is the course that costs the heart quickly.

> [!caution] The third course is where discipline is tested most
> This is the most loaded the sequence ever gets, and it is still moderate by internet standards: the 19-nor is a microdose, the GH is low, insulin may never appear, and every addition has a marker watching it and a pull-back trigger ([[Part 3.4 - When the Numbers Move|the number is the boss]]). The hematocrit ceiling in [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course#Adding a compound: the sequencing rules|the sequencing rules]] can take every erythropoietic add-on off the table, the 19-nor included. The athlete who lasts treats the third course like the first: the least that works, measured.

### The fourth year and after: the horizon

Past the third course, two things change: the gains slow to a crawl, and the decisions become about restraint and longevity rather than mass. For a reader targeting the 28-to-29.5 aesthetic rather than open-class size, years four and five are mostly refinement and maintenance; you have largely arrived.

**The biological accounting of slowing gains.** The bigger you get, the more of your protein synthesis is spent maintaining existing mass rather than building new tissue. Progress that took weeks in the first course takes months now, and each marginal kilogram costs a larger hormonal signal. That is the natural off-ramp: the point where more drug buys less and less.

> [!warning] The road this sequence does not take
> For full-time, open-class bodybuilders chasing an FFMI past 30, the stack evolves from the golden triangle (testosterone, GH, insulin) into four pathways forced at once by adding IGF-1, to drive nutrients into muscle that no longer wants to grow; some switch to short, high-dose blasts of about six weeks, because only a massive signal moves an established baseline. ==That is exactly the territory this reader stops short of.== It is a different sport with a far steeper bill: insulin and IGF-1 are the riskiest tools in the kit, and the [[Part 3.1 - Medical Screening and Monitoring|cancer-marker]] caveat bites hardest there. At a 28-to-29.5 target the triangle on a calendar is already plenty. *We do not want to be too big.*

**Mental health and the "fluff" offseason.** Staying shredded for years, for the mirror, for social media or for marketing, quietly wrecks mood, libido and sanity; chronic restriction plus chronic drugs is a grind. ==The fix is permission to be fluffy:== cover up in a hoodie through the offseason, eat in a real surplus, let conditioning slip, protect your head. That comfortable-surplus period is where the long-term gains come from, and it is the difference between a sustainable decade and a burnout.

**The long-term suppression reality.** By the fifth year the cumulative cost is real and worth stating plainly. Your natural baseline declines: come off after five years and your endogenous testosterone is likely well below where it started, and you would be lucky to hold about three quarters of peak muscle, drifting back over time toward something only slightly above your original natural potential. That is the [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|lifetime-commitment]] reality made concrete. And the organs keep the score: after years of calculated unhealthiness, kidney markers (cystatin C) and liver enzymes (GGT, ALT, AST) are non-negotiable, and the asymmetry holds: the liver regenerates, the kidneys largely do not. Protect them proactively, because that damage does not undo.

The fourth-year playbook is less heroic, more disciplined: keep the courses by the calendar (the three-to-four-month down-periods with HCG and HMG are not optional now; they are what keeps a buffer of health), micromanage the [[Part 3.5 - Choosing Your Ancillaries|ancillaries]] GH and insulin demand, and guard digestion so a 5,000-calorie offseason still lets you pull a deep vacuum. The goal stops being "grow" and becomes "stay here, healthy, for as long as I want to."

## Part 1.2 Takeaways

> [!check] Key concepts to internalize
> - **A course, not a cycle.** A line from A to B with a map, individual from a baseline, sixteen weeks or so with a defined end, measured so the next one can be designed. A cycle goes round; a course goes somewhere.
> - **Two courses, cut and build.** Different compounds, timing and water; not the same course at different calories. Maintenance is a bridge and a cruise is a posture, not a course.
> - **There is no dirty bulk.** Fat switches off the environment the drugs are paid to create and buys a harder cut.
> - **Two kinds of building course**, mass and shape; the series writes the mass course.
> - **Two numbers decide which course comes first.** FFMI says how far from the target; body fat says whether you may chase it yet. Above 15%, cut; 8 to 12% with room under the ceiling, build.
> - **The courses come in an order.** Bioidentical first, and prove recovery; the DHT dial and the GH layer second; the 19-nor last and only for a peak; then restraint. The target is a lean 28 to 29.5 FFMI, not maximum size, and the down-periods are what keep a buffer of health.
> - **Builds are long and modest, cuts are short and aggressive.** 16 to 26 weeks at 0.35 to 0.5% a week; 4 to 8 weeks at 0.75 to 1% a week. The down-period is 3 to 4 months and is part of the course.
> - **Four kinds of KPI**: the rate on a 7-day average, the gates, the lifts (moving every week or two on a build, held within 5% on a cut), and the audits (eight weeks in, and at the end).
> - **Muscle is cheap per day; protein comes down as carbs go up.** The surplus is set from the lifts and the waist; the gut is the bottleneck.
> - **Five things move between a build day and a cut day:** GH timing, thyroid's role, the oral's job, the water target, and what the fasted morning is for.

## Your Course-Design Task List

1. **Get the anchor:** a DEXA or calibrated scan, or an honest mirror estimate, and write the date and the instrument.
2. **Run the calculator** and write down the course, the weekly rate, the block length and the projected end state. That is the course's A and B.
3. **Give the course an end date** inside the 12-to-20-week range (or the 4-to-8-week block for a cut) and the down-period after it, before anything is bought.
4. **Place this course in the sequence**: first (prove recovery), second (the build), third (the sharp tools), or the horizon; the order decides what may be added.
5. **Write the KPIs for the block:** the 7-day average and the waist; the lifts you expect to move (build) or hold (cut); the eight-week audit on the calendar.
6. **Set protein from target lean mass**, fat at its floor, then carbohydrate from training stress. Write all three.
7. **Map the day** into the windows above with your own meals and the course's tools; mark which blocks are conditional (LISS-only, training-only, rest-only).
8. **Run the elimination diet** for two weeks and log what fails the Vacuum Rule.
9. **At the end of the course, quantify it** (tape, lifts, panel) and design the next one from the new numbers.
