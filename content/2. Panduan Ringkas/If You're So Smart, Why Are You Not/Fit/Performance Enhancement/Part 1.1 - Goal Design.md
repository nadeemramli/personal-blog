## Why the goal comes before the compound

Most people who go enhanced pick a compound first, then a dose, then a program to justify them, and discover a year later that nothing was pointed at anything. The [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|Decision]] page already said why that wastes finite blasts. This page is the fix: you write the goal down as numbers, and every other choice becomes a *derivation*.

That is the same move the whole [[The Upward Spiral — How the Order Compounds|series]] runs on. A bet gets sized to a target, not to an appetite. If you can't state the target, you are not ready to size the bet.

## The dual-core goal

An enhanced physique has two drivers, and they pull on different systems:

| Driver | What it means | The adaptation it demands |
|---|---|---|
| **Strength performance** | Peak output on the compound lifts (bench press, deadlift, RDL, and whatever else you benchmark) | Neural drive, and the hypertrophy that raises the ceiling on it |
| **Body composition** | A lean-mass cap for your frame, a fat-mass target, and a *water* target | Muscle protein synthesis, nutrient partitioning, and fluid control |

Both are written as numbers. For a 173 cm frame, the worked target in this series is **~85 kg at ~5% body fat, with extracellular and subcutaneous water capped at roughly 2 to 3 kg.** The water cap is the line most people never write down, and it's the one that separates a physique that holds from one that looks good for a week.

> [!important] Two dashboards, written before anything else
> This is [[Part 1.0 - The Decision|Part 1.0]]'s "two dashboards" made concrete. The Fit dashboard has strength KPIs and composition KPIs. The Health dashboard ([[Part 3.0 - The Risk Map|the Risk Map]]) is what the bet is allowed to cost. Neither moves until both are written.

## The equation

The whole page in one flow:

$$\text{Primary Goals (Strength \& Lean-Mass Targets)} \longrightarrow \text{Training Program \& Intensity}$$

$$\text{Training Intensity} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{Diet \& Carbohydrate Intake} \\ \text{Anabolic Steroid Intake} \end{cases}$$

$$\begin{cases} \text{Anabolic Steroid Intake} \longrightarrow \text{HGH Dose} \\ \text{Carbohydrate Intake} \longrightarrow \text{Insulin Dose (Partitioning Support)} \end{cases}$$

Read it left to right. The goal sets the work. The work sets the fuel and the hormonal support. The hormonal support sets its own dependents. Nothing on the right is chosen on its own; each node is set by the one to its left. That is what "lowest effective dose" means in practice: a dose is the smallest number that satisfies the node feeding it.

## Step 1: define the target variables

- **Strength targets.** Milestones on the benchmark lifts, written as loads and dates, in the same relative-to-bodyweight language as [[Part 1.1 - What Actually Matters|the natural standards]], but against the *enhanced* elite band rather than the natural one.
- **Composition targets.** Lean mass at the frame's cap, fat mass at the target, water at the cap. Three numbers, each measured a different way (DEXA or a calibrated scanner for lean and fat; morning weight and the [[Part 1.2 - Course Design|Vacuum Rule]] for water).

If a number can't be measured, it isn't a target yet.

## Step 2: derive the program and the intensity

The goals dictate the program, not the other way round. Strength milestones set the periodisation and the load (RPE/RIR targets, mechanical tension). Composition milestones set the volume and the phase (surplus or deficit). The autoregulated structure from [[Part 3.1 - The Program - Concepts|the natural series]] still runs underneath; enhancement changes the recovery constants, not the logic.

The output of this step is a single quantity: **training stress**, the amount of work and recovery the next steps have to pay for.

## Step 3: derive the baseline support (nutrition and the AAS base)

Training stress branches into two things, and they are derived in parallel:

- **Nutrition baseline.** Calories, protein and carbohydrate are scaled to the training demand and the phase (mass accrual or cutting). Carbohydrate is the lead variable on a bulk, because it is the protein-sparing, insulinogenic driver (see [[Part 1.2 - Course Design|Part 1.2]] for why protein comes *down* as carbs go up).
- **AAS base.** The androgen load is matched to the training stress and the recovery it demands, and to nothing else. Not to a forum number, not to what someone bigger runs. This is the rule that prevents megadosing: if the work doesn't require more, the dose doesn't rise.

Both are derived from the same input, which is why a sloppy program produces both a sloppy diet and an inflated dose.

## Step 4: align the dependent compounds (HGH and insulin)

Two compounds in the enhanced toolbox are **dependents**: they are not set from the goal directly but from the nodes above them.

- **Growth hormone** is scaled to the total anabolic load, because its job (the IGF-1 pathway, nutrient partitioning) is to work *with* the androgen signal, not in place of it. More androgen without proportionate GH wastes the synergy; more GH without the androgen base is expensive water.
- **Insulin** is scaled to carbohydrate intake, because its job during a heavy high-carb phase is partitioning and pancreatic support, and its risk (hypoglycaemia, which is immediate and can be fatal) rises with any mismatch between the dose and the carbohydrate it is covering. It is the one compound in this series where the dependency is also the safety mechanism: if the carbs aren't there, the insulin isn't either.

The ratios that set each dependent (how much GH per unit of androgen, how much insulin per gram of carbohydrate) are **operating-layer numbers.** They are titrated against bloodwork and a glucose log, with a physician where you can get one, and this series does not print them. What it prints is the dependency, because the dependency is the principle: *these two are never set on their own.*

## The functional checklist

```
[ ] Step 1: Define target variables
    ├── Strength: bench, deadlift, RDL progression milestones (load × date)
    └── Composition: lean-mass cap @ ~5% BF for the frame; water ≤ 2–3 kg

[ ] Step 2: Establish the program and the intensity
    └── Volume, load, mechanical tension that the targets require → training stress

[ ] Step 3: Derive the baseline support
    ├── Nutrition: calories and carbohydrate matched to training demand and phase
    └── AAS base: androgen load matched strictly to training stress and recovery

[ ] Step 4: Align the dependent compounds
    ├── HGH: scaled against total anabolic load (ratio lives in the operating layer)
    └── Insulin: scaled against daily carbohydrate (ratio lives in the operating layer)
```

Then, and only then, the series moves to the course ([[Part 1.2 - Course Design|1.2]]): cut or build, which comes first, how long it runs, and the weekly numbers that say whether it is working; *what* is in the stack is [[Part 2.0 - The Anabolic Pathways|Part 2]], and what it costs is [[Part 3.0 - The Risk Map|Part 3]].

## What this rules out

- **Compound-first thinking.** If a compound isn't satisfying a node in the equation, it isn't in the stack. "I want to try Tren" is not a node.
- **Dose creep.** The AAS node rises only when training stress rises *and* the [[Part 1.3 - Running the Course|adjustment ladder]] (food first, training second, dose last) has been climbed.
- **Orphan dependents.** GH without a proportionate androgen base; insulin without the carbohydrate it covers. Both are the signature of a stack built by appetite.
- **Unmeasured targets.** A goal without a measurement is a wish, and wishes get megadosed.

## Part 1.1 Takeaways

> [!check] Key concepts to internalize
> - **Goal before compound.** The target is written as numbers; everything else is derived.
> - **Dual core.** Strength performance and body composition are separate drivers, and the composition target includes a *water* cap.
> - **The equation.** Goals → program and intensity → (nutrition, AAS base) → (HGH from the AAS load, insulin from the carbohydrate). Each node is set by the one feeding it.
> - **Dependents are never set alone.** GH follows the androgen load; insulin follows the carbs. The ratios live in the operating layer, not on a public page.
> - **Lowest effective dose has a definition now:** the smallest number that satisfies the node above it.

## Your Goal-Design Task List

1. **Write the strength milestones** as load × date for each benchmark lift, against the enhanced elite band.
2. **Write the composition targets** (lean mass, fat mass, water) and name the instrument that measures each.
3. **Derive the training stress** from the program those targets require; write the recovery it demands.
4. **Set the nutrition baseline and the AAS base from the training stress**, in that order, and write *why* each number is what it is.
5. **Write the two dependencies** (GH ← AAS load; insulin ← carbohydrate) in your operating layer, with the titration log that will move them.
