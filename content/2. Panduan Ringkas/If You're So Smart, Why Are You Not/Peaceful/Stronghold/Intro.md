Nassim Taleb principle - be socialist with your family, be democrats with your close friends, be capitalist with your external circle. 

This principle is a core tenet of Nassim Taleb’s concept of **layered fractal political systems** (most famously articulated in his book _Skin in the Game_ and his _Incerto_ series). It argues that moral, economic, and social rules cannot be applied universally; they must scale according to the closeness of your relationship. [[1](https://medium.com/@swiish.etc/having-skin-in-the-game-4db8c87f19fd), [2](https://www.shortform.com/blog/nassim-taleb-philosophy/), [3](https://medium.com/@matthewcohen115/asymmetries-of-life-skin-in-the-game-book-review-ce6d65affff1)]

Here is how the principle breaks down:

1. "Be Socialist with Your Family"

- **The Concept:** Within the immediate family unit, resources are shared equitably based on need, not on individual economic output. You do not charge your children for meals, nor do you profit off your spouse.
- **Why it works:** The family operates on unconditional support, altruism, and collective survival.

2. "Be Democrats with Your Close Friends"

- **The Concept:** In your inner circle, relationships are egalitarian and based on mutual respect, reciprocity, and shared decision-making.
- **Why it works:** Friendship requires a balance of power and peer-to-peer consensus rather than strict hierarchy or purely transactional economics.

3. "Be Capitalist with Your External Circle"

- **The Concept:** In the broader, external world (business, strangers, and the free market), interactions are voluntary, competitive, and driven by value exchange.
- **Why it works:** Applying socialism or unchecked altruism to strangers can lead to inefficiency and exploitation. Capitalism provides honest price signals, accountability, and mutual benefit without requiring emotional intimacy.

“With my family, I’m a communist. With my close friends, I’m a socialist. At the state level of politics, I’m a Democrat. At higher levels, I’m a Republican, and at the federal levels, I’m a Libertarian.”

The larger the group of people you have together, the less trust there is and the more cheating takes place – the more you gear towards capitalism The smaller the group you’re in – then by all means be a socialist
