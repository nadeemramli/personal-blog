## Planned contents

**The core principle: systems, not willpower.** Willpower is a finite, unreliable resource that collapses exactly when you're tired and stressed (which is most evenings). The goal is to ==never let your in-the-moment mind make the decision==, and instead build life systems that make the healthy choice the default and the unhealthy one high-friction.

**Breaking a bad habit: the named-saboteur method.** A concrete, slightly playful technique (adapted from the Blueprint approach to stress-eating):
1. **Identify the version of you** responsible for the self-destructive behaviour and **give it a name** ("Evening Bryan").
2. **Write down the persuasion tactics** it uses ("tonight's the last night", "we'll burn it off tomorrow").
3. **Prepare rebuttals** so you can hold the line ("this is not what we actually want", "this makes us sad and regretful").
4. **Decide which version of you should be in charge** (Morning You, Sleep You) and give *that* version the authority.
5. **Make firm rules**, because "none is easier than some." A bright line is easier to hold than a negotiated limit.

**Designing the environment.** Habit stacking, prepping the night before (gym bag, food), removing the friction from good choices and adding it to bad ones, and making the default path the healthy one.

**The low-friction, compounding stack (the work-from-home advantage).** A worked example of how a flexible day quietly builds health, structured around a Pomodoro rhythm (a short break roughly every 45 minutes):
- **Air squats**, ~1 minute (post-meal, this blunts the glucose spike and improves insulin sensitivity).
- **Light movement** to nudge up total daily energy expenditure (TDEE).
- **The 20/20/20-plus vision break**: every ~45 minutes, look ~20 feet away for 1–2 minutes to rest the eyes.
- **Posture reset and a quick stretch.**
- **Box breathing** (4-4-4-4) for one minute to drop the nervous system down a gear.
- **Sunlight exposure**, ideally in the morning, to set the circadian clock.
- **A sip of water**, keeping hydration on track.
- The thesis: *low friction, compounding returns.* None of these are heroic; stacked across a day, they move real numbers.

**The loop back to income.** Working from home is itself a health intervention because it hands you the flexibility to do all of the above. This is the upward spiral the intro essays describe: a flexible income funds and frees the time for health, which raises everything else.
