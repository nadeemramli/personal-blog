## Planned contents

**The thesis.** If you do only one thing for your health, lower your resting heart rate (RHR) before bed. It is free, it is within your power, and it sits at the bottom of a cascade that touches every other marker on the dashboard.

**The cascade (why this one lever moves everything).**
- Low RHR before bed produces **better sleep**.
- Better sleep restores the **self-control** that makes you exercise and eat well.
- Exercising and eating well improves nearly every marker, *including RHR itself*, which closes the loop and spins it upward.
- A high pre-sleep RHR runs the same loop in reverse: poor sleep destroys self-control, you skip the gym and eat badly, and RHR climbs. A vicious cycle.

**Why RHR before bed is such a clean signal.** It is an objective, nightly readout of how parasympathetic ("rest and digest") your nervous system is at the moment you're asking it to sleep. It moves *before* you consciously feel anything, which is what makes it a leading indicator (the [[Part 1.0 - What Healthy Actually Is|Part 1.0]] theme).

**The six actions that lower it** (the practical core):
1. **Eat your last meal 4 hours before bed.** Late food keeps RHR high and wrecks sleep quality. (The illustrative Blueprint claim: dinner an hour before bed pushing RHR from ~39 to ~56, and sleep quality down 30–40%.) Experiment with 2, 4, and 6 hour gaps and watch your own number.
2. **Screens off 60 minutes before bed.** Give the nervous system runway to wind down instead of doomscrolling it awake.
3. **Do a calming thing in that hour:** read, walk, journal, breathwork, call a friend.
4. **Respect caffeine's half-life (~6 hours).** A 4pm coffee leaves the equivalent of half a cup in your system at 10pm.
5. **Use red/amber light in the evening** and blue-light filters (f.lux, night modes, glasses).
6. **No fights after 5pm.** Resolve conflict in the morning when you're rested. Emotional arousal is physiological arousal.

**How to run it as an experiment.** Use the wearable from [[Part 1.1 - Measuring Yourself|Part 1.1]]: establish your baseline pre-sleep and sleeping RHR, change one variable (move dinner earlier), and watch the number move over a week. Let the data, not the theory, convince you.
