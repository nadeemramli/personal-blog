## The core claim

Value = competency.

Competency comes in three levels:

- **Level 1 — Tactical.** You can do the task well. Execution. The hands.
- **Level 2 — Strategical.** You can pick which tasks, in what order, against which battles. Judgement. The eyes.
- **Level 3 — Equity.** You own the thing the value accrues to. Ownership. The claim.

Those who reach Level 3 (equity, and most of the time capital) win over the long run, because that's how the capitalist market is wired: returns flow to owners, not to effort.

## The thing I want to name

The "ambitious bum" is the person with *plenty* of ambition, effort, and hustle, who never leaves Level 1 (or treats Level 2 as a hobby). All motion, no position. Always the hardest worker in the room, never the owner of the room. They confuse activity with ownership, and the market quietly never pays them for the difference.

So: be ambitious, but don't be a bum about it. Convert competency into equity instead of renting it out forever.

## Building it out (sharpening, to react to)

**The ladder isn't one kind of thing — there's a category jump.** Level 1 and Level 2 are *competencies* (things you are good at). Level 3 is a *position* (something you own). The first two answer "how good are you?"; the third answers "what do you have a claim on?" The whole point of the essay lives in that jump from L2 to L3 — ambition that never crosses it stays a bum.

**Re-read the levels as "how you get paid":**

- L1 → paid for your *time / output* (a wage; capped by hours).
- L2 → paid for your *judgement* (higher fees, but still largely rented).
- L3 → paid for *ownership* (the value compounds into an asset you hold while you sleep).

The ambitious bum is stuck being paid for motion, not position.

**This is the career-side mirror of the [[Part 3.1 - Power|Power]] article.** There: at a business without power, sales is a treadmill; with power, it compounds. Same logic at the personal level — tactical/strategic effort *for someone else's equity* is a treadmill; the same effort building *your own* equity compounds. And it ties to [[Part 3.0 - Making Value vs Keeping Value|Making Value vs Keeping Value]]: L1/L2 = making value, L3 = keeping/owning it.

**Important guardrail — you can't just skip to equity.** Equity without competency is gambling (lottery, inheritance, hype tokens). Tactical competency is the *on-ramp* that earns you the right (and the capital) to take equity. So the message isn't "ownership > skill." It's "use your skill to acquire ownership, instead of trading it for wages forever."

**The failure modes of the ambitious bum:**

1. *Tactical hamster wheel* — endlessly becoming a better operator, never an owner.
2. *Strategy-as-hobby* — endless planning, frameworks, and reading; no stake, no shipping.
3. *Busy-ness as identity* — the hustle itself feels like progress, so the absence of ownership never registers.
